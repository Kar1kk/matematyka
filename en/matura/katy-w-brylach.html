Angles in solids: line–plane angle, dihedral angle

You compute every angle in a solid in a flat triangle. The art is to choose that triangle well: for the angle between a line and a plane you need a projection, and for the angle between faces you need two segments perpendicular to the common edge.

X.2, X.3

1. Definitions

The angle between a line and a plane is the angle between the line and its orthogonal projection onto that plane. Its measure lies in $[0^\circ,90^\circ]$: $0^\circ$ when the line is parallel to the plane, $90^\circ$ when it is perpendicular.
The dihedral angle (between half-planes with a common edge $k$): choose a point $K\in k$ and in each half-plane draw a ray from $K$ perpendicular to $k$. The angle between these rays is the plane angle of the dihedral angle. It does not depend on the choice of $K$.
A dihedral angle can be obtuse (e.g. between adjacent lateral faces of a pyramid). The angle between planes is always the acute (or right) one.

2. Method

Line–plane

  1. From a point of the line drop a perpendicular onto the plane (the foot = the projection of the point).
  2. The projection of the line passes through the foot and the point where the line meets the plane.
  3. A right triangle: the segment, its projection, the height. Compute $\sin$, $\cos$ or $\tan$.

Face–face

  1. Find the common edge.
  2. In both faces draw segments perpendicular to it at the same point (e.g. altitudes of triangles; the three perpendiculars theorem).
  3. The angle between them: from a right triangle or from the law of cosines.

4. Step-by-step example

All edges of the regular square pyramid $ABCDS$ have length $2$. Find the cosine of the dihedral angle between faces $ABS$ and $BCS$.

  1. The common edge: $SB$. The faces are equilateral triangles, so the altitudes $AK$ and $CK$ dropped onto $SB$ have a common foot $K$ (the midpoint of $SB$): $|AK|=|CK|=\sqrt3$.
  2. $|AC|=2\sqrt2$ (the diagonal of the base).
  3. Law of cosines in triangle $AKC$: $8=3+3-2\cdot3\cos\varphi$.

$\cos\varphi=-\frac13$ (an obtuse angle, about $109.5^\circ$)

In the demo: the “face–face” mode, $H\approx1.4$ ($H=\sqrt2$).

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Find the tangent of the angle between the diagonal of a cube and the plane of the base.
    Solution
    The projection of diagonal $AG$ is the base diagonal $AC=a\sqrt2$, the height is $CG=a$. $\tan\alpha=\frac{a}{a\sqrt2}$. $\tan\alpha=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$
  2. medium
    In cube $ABCDEFGH$ ($E$ above $A$) find the tangent of the angle between plane $BDE$ and the plane of the base.
    Solution
    The common edge is $BD$. $O$ is the midpoint of $BD$: $AO\perp BD$, and $EO\perp BD$ (the three perpendiculars theorem, or triangle $BDE$ is equilateral). Angle $EOA$: $\tan=\frac{|AE|}{|AO|}=\frac{a}{a\sqrt2/2}$. $\tan\alpha=\sqrt2$
  3. medium
    A regular triangular pyramid has base edge $6$ and height $3$. Find the angle of inclination of a lateral face to the base and the tangent of the angle of inclination of a lateral edge.
    Solution
    $r=\frac{6\sqrt3}{6}=\sqrt3$, $R=2\sqrt3$. $\tan\beta=\frac{3}{\sqrt3}=\sqrt3$, $\tan\alpha=\frac{3}{2\sqrt3}$. $\beta=60^\circ$, $\tan\alpha=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}$
  4. exam-level
    Find the cosine of the angle of inclination of a lateral face of a regular tetrahedron to the plane of the base.
    Solution
    Triangle $SOM$: $|SM|=h$ (the altitude of a face), $|OM|=r=\frac13h$ (the foot is at the centre of the equilateral triangle). $\cos\beta=\frac{r}{h}$. $\cos\beta=\frac13$
  5. exam-level
    A regular square pyramid has base edge $4$, and the dihedral angle between adjacent lateral faces is $120^\circ$. Find the height of the pyramid.
    Solution
    Triangle $AKC$ ($AK$, $CK$ are the altitudes of the faces onto $SB$): isosceles with angle $120^\circ$, $|AC|=4\sqrt2=|AK|\sqrt3$, so $|AK|=\frac{4\sqrt6}{3}$. The area of face $ABS$ in two ways, $b=|SB|$: $b\cdot|AK|=4\sqrt{b^2-4}$, hence $\frac{32}{3}b^2=16(b^2-4)$, $b^2=12$. $H^2=b^2-R^2=12-8$. $H=2$

6. On the exam

  • In the drawing mark the angle with an arc and name it ($\sphericalangle SMO$). Without this the examiner does not know which angle you are computing.
  • Justify the right angle: “$SO\perp ABC$, so $SO\perp OM$”, and for a dihedral angle: both arms $\perp$ the edge.
  • Angle between lateral faces: the altitudes from $A$ and $C$ hit the same point $K$ only by symmetry (a regular solid); in general, draw the perpendiculars from one point.
  • A negative result from the law of cosines = an obtuse dihedral angle. The angle between the planes is then $180^\circ-\varphi$.
