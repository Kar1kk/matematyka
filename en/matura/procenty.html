Percentages, compound interest, deposits and loans

A change by $p\%$ is a multiplication by $1\pm\frac{p}{100}$. Successive changes are multiplied, not added — hence compound interest, which is just an ordinary geometric sequence.

I.8 (with VI.6: geometric sequence)

1. Percentages and percentage points

A change by $p\%$ (to memorize)
$$\text{increase: } a\cdot\left(1+\tfrac{p}{100}\right)$$ $$\text{decrease: } a\cdot\left(1-\tfrac{p}{100}\right)$$
by what %
$\frac{\text{new}-\text{old}}{\text{old}}\cdot100\%$ — always relative to the original value
Percentage points measure the difference of two quantities expressed in percent. An interest rate going from $5\%$ to $7\%$: an increase by $2$ percentage points, but by $\frac{2}{5}=40\%$.
After a $20\%$ discount you must raise the price by $25\%$, not $20\%$, to get back: $0.8\cdot1.25=1$.

2. Interactive: $+p\%$, then $-p\%$

A price of $100$ PLN rises by $p\%$, then falls by $p\%$. Click “▶ Play” and see that it does not return to $100$. The loss is always $\frac{p^2}{100}\%$, and the order of the changes does not matter.

4. A loan repaid in equal instalments

The debt grows every month by interest (multiplication by $q=1+\frac{r}{100}$), and the instalment $R$ reduces it. After $n$ instalments the debt should be $0$.

Instalment not on CKE sheet – derive it
$$K q^n=R\left(1+q+\dots+q^{n-1}\right)$$ $$R=\frac{K q^n (q-1)}{q^n-1}$$
$K$, $r$
the loan amount, the interest rate per period (e.g. a month) in %
cost
$nR-K$ (the sum of instalments minus the borrowed amount)
Where does this equation come from?

The debt after $k$ months: $D_k=D_{k-1}\cdot q-R$, $D_0=K$. Expanding:

$$D_n=Kq^n-R(q^{n-1}+\dots+q+1)$$

The condition $D_n=0$ and the sum of a geometric sequence (on the formula sheet, see geometric sequence) give the formula for $R$. Intuition: each instalment “repays” a part of the debt together with the interest accrued until the end.

5. Step-by-step example

A deposit of $10\,000$ PLN for $2$ years, $4\%$ per year, quarterly compounding, a $19\%$ tax on interest at every compounding. How much will we withdraw?

  1. The rate per quarter: $\frac{4\%}{4}=1\%$; after tax $0.81\cdot1\%=0.81\%$ remains.
  2. The number of quarters: $4\cdot2=8$.
  3. $K=10\,000\cdot1.0081^8\approx10\,666.67$.

approx. $10\,666.67$ PLN (without the tax it would be $10\,828.57$ PLN)

6. Tasks

  1. easy
    A price of $250$ PLN rose by $12\%$, and then fell by $12\%$. What is the final price?
    Solution
    $250\cdot1.12\cdot0.88=$ $246.40$ PLN (a decrease by $1.44\%$)
  2. easy
    The interest rate of a loan rose from $6\%$ to $8\%$. By how many percentage points, and by how many percent?
    Solution
    The difference is $8-6=2$, relatively $\frac26=\frac13$. By $2$ p.p., i.e. by $33\frac13\%$
  3. medium
    After a $20\%$ discount a jacket costs $360$ PLN. How much did it cost before the discount, and by what percent would the new price have to be raised to get back to the old one?
    Solution
    $0.8x=360\Rightarrow x=450$. $\frac{450}{360}=1.25$. $450$ PLN, an increase by $25\%$
  4. medium
    $5000$ PLN for a year, $6\%$ per year. How much more does monthly compounding give than yearly (without tax)?
    Solution
    Monthly: $5000\cdot1.005^{12}\approx5308.39$ PLN. Yearly: $5000\cdot1.06=5300$ PLN. approx. $8.39$ PLN more
  5. exam-level
    A deposit at $5\%$ per year with yearly compounding. After how many full years does the capital at least double for the first time?
    Solution
    $1.05^n\ge2\iff n\ge\frac{\log 2}{\log1.05}\approx14.2$ (see logarithms). Check: $1.05^{14}\approx1.98$, $1.05^{15}\approx2.08$. After $15$ years
  6. exam-level
    A loan of $12\,000$ PLN is repaid in $12$ equal monthly instalments (the first after a month), interest $1\%$ per month. Find the instalment and the cost of the loan.
    Solution
    $q=1.01$: $R=\frac{12\,000\cdot1.01^{12}\cdot0.01}{1.01^{12}-1}\approx1066.19$ PLN. Cost: $12\cdot1066.19-12\,000\approx794.28$ PLN. Instalment approx. $1066.19$ PLN, cost approx. $794$ PLN

7. On the exam

  • Successive changes are multiplied: $+10\%$ and $+10\%$ make $\cdot1.21$, i.e. $+21\%$, not $+20\%$.
  • Percentage points ≠ percent. Read what is being asked.
  • Compounding $m$ times a year: rate $\frac pm$, exponent $mn$. A typical mistake: $\left(1+\frac{p}{100}\right)^{mn}$.
  • Round at the end, to the grosz (0.01 PLN); keep intermediate powers exact (in the calculator).
  • “After how many years…” is an exponential inequality: a logarithm or checking successive $n$. Give the number of full years.
