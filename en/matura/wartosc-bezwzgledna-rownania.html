Absolute value equations and inequalities

$|x-a|$ is the distance between $x$ and $a$ on the number line. $|x-a|=r$: points at distance $r$ from $a$; $|x-a|<r$: closer than $r$. When there are several absolute values, you split the line into intervals and drop the absolute values in each.

I.7, III.R.4

1. Definition and interpretation

Definition $|x|=x$ for $x\ge0$ and $|x|=-x$ for $x<0$. The number $|x-a|$ is the distance between the points $x$ and $a$ on the number line.
Basic types ($r>0$)
$$|x-a|=r\iff x=a-r\ \vee\ x=a+r$$ $$|x-a|<r\iff a-r<x<a+r$$ $$|x-a|>r\iff x<a-r\ \vee\ x>a+r$$
$r=0$
$|x-a|=0\iff x=a$
$r<0$
$|x-a|=r$ and $|x-a|<r$ have no solutions, and $|x-a|>r$ holds for every $x$
$|x+4|=|x-(-4)|$: this is the distance from $-4$, not from $4$.

2. The interval method

  1. Find the zeros of the expressions inside the absolute values: they split the line into intervals.
  2. In each interval determine the sign of each expression and drop the absolute values: $|A|=A$ when $A\ge0$, $|A|=-A$ when $A<0$.
  3. Solve the resulting linear or quadratic equation (inequality).
  4. Intersect the result with the interval in which you were working. At the end, take the union of the results from the intervals.

Shortcuts: $|A|=|B|\iff A=B\ \vee\ A=-B$. $\;|A|=B$ requires $B\ge0$. Both sides nonnegative? You may square: $|A|<|B|\iff A^2<B^2$.

3. Interactive: distance from $a$

4. Interactive: a sum of two absolute values

$y=|x-a|+|x-b|$ is the sum of the distances from $a$ and from $b$. The three colors are three intervals, and the formula is linear in each. Between $a$ and $b$ the sum is constant and equal to $|a-b|$ (a flat bottom). The level $c$ and the sign determine the solution set.

5. Worked example

Solve the inequality $|2x-3|\le5$.

  1. $-5\le2x-3\le5$.
  2. $-2\le2x\le8 \iff -1\le x\le4$.

$x\in[-1,4]$

Solve the equation $|x+1|+|x-2|=5$.

  1. Zeros: $-1$ and $2$. Three intervals.
  2. $x<-1$: $-x-1-x+2=5 \iff x=-2$. It belongs to the interval ✓.
  3. $-1\le x<2$: $x+1-x+2=3=5$, contradictory.
  4. $x\ge2$: $2x-1=5 \iff x=3$ ✓.

$x\in\{-2,3\}$

The distance from $-1$ to $2$ is $3$; a sum of distances equal to $5$ requires going $1$ beyond the segment on each side. This is the starting setting of demo 4.

6. Tasks

  1. easy
    Solve the equation $|x+4|=5$.
    Solution
    Points at distance $5$ from $-4$. $x=-9$ or $x=1$
  2. easy
    Solve the inequality $|x-2|>3$.
    Solution
    Farther than $3$ from $2$. $x\in(-\infty,-1)\cup(5,\infty)$
  3. medium
    Solve the inequality $|3-2x|<7$.
    Solution
    $-7<3-2x<7 \iff -10<-2x<4 \iff -2<x<5$. $x\in(-2,5)$
  4. medium
    Solve the equation $|x-1|=2x+1$.
    Solution
    $x\ge1$: $x-1=2x+1$, $x=-2\notin[1,\infty)$, rejected. $x<1$: $1-x=2x+1$, $x=0$ ✓. $x=0$
  5. exam-level
    Solve the inequality $|x-3|+|x+1|\le6$.
    Solution
    $x<-1$: $-2x+2\le6$, $x\ge-2$, so $x\in[-2,-1)$. $-1\le x<3$: $4\le6$ always, $x\in[-1,3)$. $x\ge3$: $2x-2\le6$, $x\le4$, so $x\in[3,4]$. $x\in[-2,4]$
  6. exam-level
    Solve the equation $|x^2-4|=3x$.
    Solution
    The left side is $\ge0$, so $x\ge0$. Then $x^2-4=3x$ or $x^2-4=-3x$. $x^2-3x-4=0$: $x=4$ or $x=-1$ (rejected). $\;x^2+3x-4=0$: $x=1$ or $x=-4$ (rejected). $x\in\{1,4\}$

7. On the exam

  • A result from an interval must belong to it: check it and write that down. A missing check is the most common lost point.
  • Ends of intervals: assign each dividing point to exactly one interval (e.g. $x<-1$, $-1\le x<2$, $x\ge2$).
  • $|A|=B$ with $B$ depending on $x$: first the condition $B\ge0$.
  • Solve simple types by the distance interpretation: faster and without intervals.
