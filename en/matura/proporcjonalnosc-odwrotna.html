Inverse proportionality: f(x) = a/x

Two quantities are inversely proportional when their product is constant: $x\cdot y=a$. Twice as many workers, half the time. On the graph every rectangle “under” the hyperbola has the same area $|a|$.

V.13, V.12

1. Definition and properties

Definition Quantities $x$ and $y$ are inversely proportional if $y=\frac{a}{x}$ for some $a\neq0$, that is $x\cdot y=a$. The number $a$ is the constant of proportionality.

$f(x)=\frac{1}{x}$ is not decreasing on its whole domain: $-1<1$, yet $f(-1)=-1<f(1)=1$. It decreases separately on each of the two intervals.

You can bring the formula $\frac{cx+d}{x-p}$ to this form by dividing the numerator: e.g. $\frac{2x+1}{x-1}=2+\frac{3}{x-1}$ (example below).

4. Worked example

Write $f(x)=\frac{2x+1}{x-1}$ in the form $\frac{a}{x-p}+q$, and give the asymptotes, the range and the monotonicity.

  1. Numerator divided by denominator: $2x+1=2(x-1)+3$.
  2. $f(x)=\frac{2(x-1)+3}{x-1}=2+\frac{3}{x-1}$, that is $a=3$, $p=1$, $q=2$.
  3. This is $y=\frac3x$ translated by $[1,2]$: asymptotes $x=1$, $y=2$, range $=\R\setminus\{2\}$.
  4. $a=3>0$, so it decreases on each interval of the domain.

$f(x)=\frac{3}{x-1}+2$; decreasing on $(-\infty,1)$ and on $(1,\infty)$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    The graph of $f(x)=\frac ax$ passes through the point $(-2,3)$. Find $a$ and compute $f(4)$.
    Solution
    $a=x\cdot y=(-2)\cdot3=-6$. $f(4)=\frac{-6}{4}$. $a=-6$, $f(4)=-1.5$
  2. easy
    $6$ workers do a certain job in $10$ days. How many workers (working at the same pace) will do it in $4$ days? Write the number of days $d$ as a function of the number of workers $n$.
    Solution
    $n\cdot d=6\cdot10=60$, so $d(n)=\frac{60}{n}$ and $n=\frac{60}{4}$. $15$ workers; $d(n)=\frac{60}{n}$
  3. medium
    For $f(x)=\frac{3}{x+2}-1$ give the domain, the range, the asymptotes and the zero.
    Solution
    $\frac3x$ translated by $[-2,-1]$. $\frac{3}{x+2}=1\iff x+2=3$. domain $=\R\setminus\{-2\}$, range $=\R\setminus\{-1\}$, asymptotes $x=-2$, $y=-1$, zero $x=1$
  4. medium
    Read from the graph (or justify) for which $x$ the function $f(x)=\frac6x$ takes values not less than $2$.
    Solution
    For $x<0$ we have $f(x)<0$, so no. For $x>0$ the function is decreasing and $f(3)=2$, so $f(x)\ge2\iff 0<x\le3$. $x\in(0,3]$
  5. exam-level
    The graph of the function $f(x)=\frac{a}{x-p}+q$ has asymptotes $x=2$ and $y=-1$ and passes through the point $(3,2)$. Find $a$ and solve the inequality $f(x)>0$.
    Solution
    $p=2$, $q=-1$. $f(3)=\frac a1-1=2$, so $a=3$. $f(x)>0\iff\frac{3}{x-2}>1$. For $x<2$ the left side is negative. For $x>2$: $3>x-2\iff x<5$. The zero $x=5$ is also visible on the graph. $a=3$, $\;f(x)>0\iff x\in(2,5)$

6. On the exam

  • Monotonicity: always two separate intervals, never “decreasing on $\R\setminus\{0\}$”.
  • Recognize inverse proportionality in the statement: “constant product” (time × speed = distance, price × quantity = amount).
  • From the asymptotes you get $p$ and $q$ at once; you find $a$ from one point of the graph.
  • With an inequality $\frac{a}{x-p}>c$ do not multiply “blindly” by $x-p$: consider the sign of the denominator, or multiply by $(x-p)^2$.
