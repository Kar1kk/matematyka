Systems with a quadratic equation

The goal is always the same: reduce the system to one quadratic (or linear) equation in one unknown. The number of solutions of the system is the number of common points of the line and the curve, and $\Delta$ tells you that.

IV.R

1. Three techniques

Substitution (line + curve)
$$\begin{cases} x^2+y^2=10\\ x-y=2 \end{cases}$$ $$(y+2)^2+y^2=10$$
step
solve the linear equation for $x$ or $y$, substitute into the quadratic one, solve, and compute the other unknown from the linear equation
Subtraction (two circles)
$$\begin{cases} x^2+y^2+ax+by=c\\ x^2+y^2+dx+ey=f \end{cases}$$ $$(a-d)x+(b-e)y=c-f$$
next
$x^2+y^2$ cancels, leaving the line through the common points. Now substitute as above
Sum and product
$$\begin{cases} x+y=s\\ xy=p \end{cases}\;\Rightarrow\; t^2-st+p=0$$
$x,y$
are the roots of this trinomial (Vieta's formulas), in either order
Why is the difference of two circle equations the line through their common points?

A common point satisfies both equations, so it also satisfies their difference. The difference no longer has $x^2+y^2$, so it is a linear equation, i.e. a line (when $a\neq d$ or $b\neq e$). This line contains both common points, so it is the line through them. For concentric circles the difference is $0=c-f$: a contradiction, or the same circles.

2. How many solutions?

Number of solutions of the system = number of common points of the graphs. After substituting $y=mx+n$ you get a trinomial in $x$, and each $x$ gives one $y$:

systempossible number of solutions
line and circle$0$, $1$ (tangent, $\Delta=0$), $2$
line and hyperbola $xy=k$$0$, $1$, $2$; horizontal or vertical line: $1$ or $0$
two circles$0$, $1$, $2$ or infinitely many (the same circle)

Geometrically (distance from the center to the line, $|S_1S_2|$ versus $r_1\pm r_2$): line and circle, two circles. The equation of a circle in standard and general form is on the formula sheet.

If the coefficient of $x^2$ in the trinomial comes out $0$ (e.g. a horizontal line and $xy=k$), the equation is linear and you do not compute $\Delta$.

5. Worked example

Solve $\begin{cases} x^2+y^2=25\\ x^2+y^2-12x-6y=-35 \end{cases}$ and give an interpretation.

  1. Subtract the second from the first: $12x+6y=60$, i.e. $y=10-2x$.
  2. Substitute into the first: $x^2+(10-2x)^2=25$, i.e. $5x^2-40x+75=0$, $x^2-8x+15=0$.
  3. $\Delta=64-60=4$, $x_1=3$, $x_2=5$. From the line: $y_1=4$, $y_2=0$.
  4. Check in the second: $9+16-36-24=-35$ ✓, $25-60=-35$ ✓.

$(3,4)$ and $(5,0)$: the circles $S_1=(0,0)$, $r_1=5$ and $S_2=(6,3)$, $r_2=\sqrt{10}$ intersect at two points

6. Exercises

  1. easy
    Solve $\begin{cases} x^2+y^2=10\\ x-y=2 \end{cases}$
    Solution
    $x=y+2$: $(y+2)^2+y^2=10$, $2y^2+4y-6=0$, $y^2+2y-3=0$, $y=1$ or $y=-3$. From the line $x=3$ or $x=-1$. $(3,1)$, $(-1,-3)$
  2. easy
    Solve $\begin{cases} x+y=5\\ xy=6 \end{cases}$
    Solution
    $x,y$ are the roots of $t^2-5t+6=0$, i.e. $2$ and $3$. $(2,3)$, $(3,2)$ (two different pairs!)
  3. medium
    Solve $\begin{cases} x^2+y^2=5\\ y=2x+5 \end{cases}$ and give the geometric interpretation.
    Solution
    $x^2+(2x+5)^2=5$, $5x^2+20x+20=0$, $(x+2)^2=0$, $x=-2$, $y=1$. One solution, because $\Delta=0$. $(-2,1)$: the line is tangent to the circle
  4. medium
    How many solutions does the system have: a) $\begin{cases} xy=4\\ x+y=3 \end{cases}$   b) $\begin{cases} xy=4\\ x+y=4 \end{cases}$?
    Solution
    a) $t^2-3t+4=0$, $\Delta=9-16<0$. No solutions (the line misses the hyperbola).
    b) $t^2-4t+4=0$, $\Delta=0$, $t=2$. One: $(2,2)$ (the line is tangent to the hyperbola).
  5. exam-level
    Solve $\begin{cases} x^2+y^2+2x-4y=20\\ x^2+y^2-6x+4y=4 \end{cases}$ and give the equation of the line through the common points of the circles.
    Solution
    Subtract: $8x-8y=16$, i.e. $y=x-2$. Substitute into the second: $x^2+(x-2)^2-6x+4(x-2)=4$, $2x^2-6x-8=0$, $x^2-3x-4=0$, $x=4$ or $x=-1$. $(4,2)$, $(-1,-3)$; line $y=x-2$
  6. exam-level
    The line $x+y=10$ meets the circle $x^2+y^2-4x-6y=12$ at the points $A$ and $B$. Find the area of the triangle $ABS$, where $S$ is the center of the circle.
    Solution
    $y=10-x$: $x^2+(10-x)^2-4x-60+6x=12$, $2x^2-18x+28=0$, $x^2-9x+14=0$, $x=2$ or $x=7$. Hence $A=(2,8)$, $B=(7,3)$. Center: $(x-2)^2+(y-3)^2=25$, $S=(2,3)$. $\vec{SA}=[0,5]$, $\vec{SB}=[5,0]$ are perpendicular, so the area $=\frac12\cdot5\cdot5$. $P=\frac{25}{2}$

7. On the exam

  • Compute the second unknown from the linear equation. From $y^2=25-x^2$ you get $\pm$ and false pairs.
  • The result is pairs, ordered: $\begin{cases} x=3\\ y=4 \end{cases}$ or $\begin{cases} x=5\\ y=0 \end{cases}$. Do not mix up the $x$'s with the $y$'s.
  • The question "how many solutions" = $\Delta$ of the trinomial after substitution (first check that the coefficient of $x^2$ is not zero).
  • Two circles: after subtracting you still have a line–circle system to solve. The line alone is not the answer.
  • Check every pair in both original equations; it takes a few seconds.
