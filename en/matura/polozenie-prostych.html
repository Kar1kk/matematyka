Relative position of lines, perpendicular lines

Two lines in the plane either intersect (one common point) or are parallel. Everything is decided by direction: the same direction means parallel, rotated by $90^\circ$ means perpendicular.

IX.1, IX.2, IX.R.4

1. Conditions for parallelism and perpendicularity

$y=a_1x+b_1$
$y=a_2x+b_2$		$A_1x+B_1y+C_1=0$
$A_2x+B_2y+C_2=0$
parallel$a_1=a_2$$A_1B_2-A_2B_1=0$
perpendicular$a_1\cdot a_2=-1$$A_1A_2+B_1B_2=0$
intersecting$a_1\neq a_2$$A_1B_2-A_2B_1\neq0$

All four conditions are on the formula sheet. Parallel lines with $b_1=b_2$ (or proportional $A,B,C$) are the same line, i.e. coincident.

The condition $a_1a_2=-1$ does not cover the pair “horizontal + vertical” ($x=c$ has no $a$). The general form always works.
The intersection point is the solution of the system of equations of both lines. The system has one solution (the lines intersect), no solution (parallel and distinct) or infinitely many (they coincide).
Why $a_1a_2=-1$?

Shift both lines so that they pass through $O$: $y=a_1x$, $y=a_2x$. The points $P=(1,a_1)$ and $Q=(1,a_2)$ lie on them. Angle $POQ$ is right $\iff |OP|^2+|OQ|^2=|PQ|^2$:

$$1+a_1^2+1+a_2^2=(a_1-a_2)^2$$ $$2=-2a_1a_2\iff a_1a_2=-1$$

3. A parallel and a perpendicular line through a point

Given a line $y=ax+b$ and a point $P=(x_0,y_0)$
$$\parallel:\ y-y_0=a(x-x_0)$$ $$\perp:\ y-y_0=-\tfrac{1}{a}(x-x_0)$$
Given a line $Ax+By+C=0$
$$\parallel:\ Ax+By+C'=0$$ $$\perp:\ Bx-Ay+C''=0$$
$C',C''$
find them by substituting the coordinates of $P$

Drag the points of line $l$ and point $P$, then ▶. A copy of $l$ moves to $P$ (parallel) and then rotates by $90^\circ$ (perpendicular). $F$ is the projection of $P$ onto $l$.

4. Step-by-step example

Find the line perpendicular to $l\colon y=2x-1$ passing through $P=(-2,5)$, and the point $F$ where it meets $l$.

  1. $a_\perp=-\frac12$, because $2\cdot\left(-\frac12\right)=-1$.
  2. $y-5=-\frac12(x+2)$, so $y=-\frac12x+4$.
  3. The system: $2x-1=-\frac12x+4$, so $\frac52x=5$, $x=2$, $y=3$.
  4. Check: $F$ lies on both lines: $2\cdot2-1=3$, $-1+4=3$ ✓.

$y=-\frac12x+4$, $F=(2,3)$

Incidentally: $|PF|=\sqrt{16+4}=2\sqrt5$ is the distance from $P$ to line $l$, computed without a separate formula (you can also use the formula from the CKE sheet: $l\colon 2x-y-1=0$, $d=\frac{|2\cdot(-2)-5-1|}{\sqrt{2^2+1}}=\frac{10}{\sqrt5}=2\sqrt5$).

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    For what $m$ are the lines $y=(2m-1)x+3$ and $y=5x-2$ parallel, and for what $m$ perpendicular?
    Solution
    Parallel: $2m-1=5$. Perpendicular: $(2m-1)\cdot5=-1$, so $2m-1=-\frac15$. Parallel for $m=3$, perpendicular for $m=\frac25$
  2. easy
    Find the common point of the lines $x+2y-4=0$ and $3x-y-5=0$.
    Solution
    From the first, $x=4-2y$. Into the second: $12-6y-y-5=0$, $y=1$, $x=2$. Check: $6-1-5=0$ ✓. $(2,1)$
  3. medium
    Through $P=(2,-1)$ draw a line parallel and a line perpendicular to $3x-4y+1=0$.
    Solution
    Parallel: $3x-4y+C=0$, $6+4+C=0$, $C=-10$. Perpendicular: $4x+3y+C=0$, $8-3+C=0$, $C=-5$. $3x-4y-10=0$ and $4x+3y-5=0$
  4. medium
    The lines $mx+2y-1=0$ and $2x+my+3=0$. For what $m$ are they parallel, and for what perpendicular?
    Solution
    Parallel: $m\cdot m-2\cdot2=0$, $m=\pm2$. Do they coincide? For $m=2$: $2x+2y-1=0$ and $2x+2y+3=0$, different $C$; for $m=-2$: $-2x+2y-1=0$ and $2x-2y+3=0$, i.e. $x-y+\frac12=0$ and $x-y+\frac32=0$, also different. Perpendicular: $2m+2m=0$, $m=0$ (the horizontal line $y=\frac12$ and the vertical line $x=-\frac32$). Parallel for $m\in\{-2,2\}$, perpendicular for $m=0$
  5. exam-level
    Find the point $P'$ symmetric to $P=(-2,5)$ with respect to the line $y=2x-1$.
    Solution
    From the example: the projection of $P$ onto the line is $F=(2,3)$ and $F$ is the midpoint of $PP'$. $P'=(2\cdot2+2,\ 2\cdot3-5)$. $P'=(6,1)$
  6. exam-level
    A triangle has vertices $A=(-3,-1)$, $B=(5,3)$, $C=(1,6)$. Find the equation of the altitude from $C$, its foot $H$ and the area of the triangle.
    Solution
    $a_{AB}=\frac{4}{8}=\frac12$, so the altitude has $a=-2$, $y-6=-2(x-1)$, $y=-2x+8$. Line $AB$: $y=\frac12x+\frac12$. The system: $-2x+8=\frac12x+\frac12$, $x=3$, $y=2$. $|CH|=\sqrt{4+16}=2\sqrt5$, $|AB|=\sqrt{64+16}=4\sqrt5$, $P=\frac12\cdot4\sqrt5\cdot2\sqrt5=20$. $y=-2x+8$, $H=(3,2)$, area $20$

6. On the exam

  • A line perpendicular to a vertical line $x=c$ is horizontal: $y=y_0$ (and vice versa). Here $-\frac1a$ will not work.
  • In problems with a parameter, for “parallel” check whether for the $m$ you found the lines coincide, if the problem speaks of distinct lines.
  • Always check the intersection point in both equations. It takes 10 seconds and catches most mistakes.
  • Altitude of a triangle, perpendicular bisector of a segment, projection of a point onto a line: all of these are a perpendicular line through a point plus a system of equations.
