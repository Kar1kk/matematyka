Lines and planes in space

In a plane two lines either intersect or are parallel. In space there is a third possibility: skew lines, which pass by each other. They can even be perpendicular without having a common point.

The angle between skew lines Through any point of one line draw a line parallel to the other. The angle between these intersecting lines (from the interval $(0^\circ, 90^\circ]$) is the angle between the skew lines. When it equals $90^\circ$, the lines are perpendicular, even though they do not intersect.
“Perpendicular” in space does not mean “intersecting at a right angle”. The edges $AB$ and $CG$ of a cube are perpendicular and skew.

2. A line and a plane, two planes

A line and a plane:

  • the line lies in the plane (infinitely many common points),
  • the line is parallel to the plane (no common points),
  • the line pierces the plane (1 point), a special case: it is perpendicular to it.

Two planes: are parallel (or coincide) or intersect along a line.

A plane is determined by: 3 non-collinear points, a line and a point outside it, two intersecting lines, two parallel lines.

A line perpendicular to a plane is a line perpendicular to every line of that plane. It is enough to check two intersecting lines of the plane: this is a theorem from the topic on three perpendiculars.

4. Step-by-step example

In the cube $ABCDEFGH$ (lower base $ABCD$, $E$ above $A$) find the angle between the lines $AC$ and $BG$.

  1. $AC$ lies in the lower base, $BG$ in the face $BCGF$; they are not parallel and have no common point, so they are skew.
  2. Translate $BG$ parallel to the point $A$: $BG\parallel AH$ (diagonals of opposite faces).
  3. Triangle $ACH$ has sides that are face diagonals, i.e. $a\sqrt2$: it is equilateral.

The angle between $AC$ and $BG$ is $60^\circ$.

Set in the demo: line $AC$, and $BG$.

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Determine the relative position of the lines in the cube $ABCDEFGH$: a) $AB$ and $GH$, b) $AB$ and $CG$, c) $AB$ and $BF$.
    Solution
    a) $GH\parallel CD\parallel AB$: parallel. b) No common point, different directions: skew, perpendicular. c) Common point $B$: intersecting, perpendicular.
  2. easy
    How many edges of a cube are skew to a given edge?
    Solution
    Of the $12$ edges: the edge itself ($1$), the parallel ones ($3$), the ones sharing a vertex with it ($4$, $2$ at each end). What remains is $12-1-3-4$. 4 edges.
  3. medium
    Find the angle between the lines $AB$ and $CH$ in the cube $ABCDEFGH$.
    Solution
    The lines are skew. $AB\parallel DC$, so the required angle is the angle $DCH$ in the face $CDHG$. $CH$ is a diagonal of the square, so it forms an angle of $45^\circ$ with the side.
  4. medium
    Determine the position relative to the plane $ABC$ of: a) the line $EG$, b) the line $AG$, c) the line $CG$.
    Solution
    a) $EG\parallel AC$, and $AC$ lies in $ABC$: parallel. b) $A$ lies in the plane, $G$ does not: pierces at the point $A$ (not perpendicularly). c) $CG\perp AB$ and $CG\perp BC$: perpendicular.
  5. exam-level
    Prove that in a regular tetrahedron $ABCD$ the edges $AB$ and $CD$ are perpendicular.
    Solution
    Let $M$ be the midpoint of $AB$. Triangles $ABC$ and $ABD$ are equilateral, so the medians $CM$ and $DM$ are altitudes: $CM\perp AB$, $DM\perp AB$. The line $AB$ is perpendicular to two intersecting lines $CM$, $DM$ of the plane $CDM$, so it is perpendicular to the whole plane $CDM$, and therefore to the line $CD$ lying in it. $AB\perp CD$ (skew perpendicular lines).

6. On the exam

  • The angle between skew lines: always translate one of them parallel (e.g. an edge to the opposite face) and compute the angle in a triangle.
  • The angle between lines has a measure in $(0^\circ,90^\circ]$. If the law of cosines gives an obtuse angle, the answer is $180^\circ$ minus that angle.
  • Justify the perpendicularity of a line to a plane with two intersecting lines of that plane, not one.
  • Drawing: hidden edges as dashed lines, vertex labels as in the statement of the task.
