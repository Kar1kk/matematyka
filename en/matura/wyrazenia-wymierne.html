Rational expressions

Fractions with polynomials in place of numbers. The arithmetic is the same as for ordinary fractions. The only new thing is the domain: the denominator cannot be zero, and canceling does not change that.

II.4, II.R.6

1. Domain and canceling

Definition A rational expression is a quotient $\dfrac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$ of polynomials, with $Q$ nonzero. Its domain is all $x$ for which $Q(x)\neq0$.

Canceling: factor the numerator and denominator and cancel the common factors. Find the domain before canceling: $$\frac{x^2-4}{x^2-2x}=\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x(x-2)}=\frac{x+2}{x}$$ for $x\in\R\setminus\{0,2\}$. Without the note “$x\ne2$” the equality is false: for $x=2$ the left side does not exist, while the right side equals $2$. On the graph this is a “hole” (demo below).

You cancel factors, not terms: $\frac{x+2}{x}\neq 2$, and $\frac{x^2+1}{x}\neq x+1$.

2. Operations

Multiplication
$$\frac{A}{B}\cdot\frac{C}{D}=\frac{AC}{BD}$$
Division
$$\frac{A}{B}:\frac{C}{D}=\frac{A}{B}\cdot\frac{D}{C}$$
condition
$B\ne0$, $D\ne0$ and $C\ne0$
Addition (common denominator)
$$\frac{A}{B}+\frac{C}{D}=\frac{AD+CB}{BD}$$
better
factor the denominators and take the least common denominator (each factor to the highest power in which it occurs). The product $BD$ often gives an unnecessarily large numerator.

Before multiplying, first factor and cancel “crosswise”, then multiply. When subtracting, put the numerator of the subtrahend in parentheses. The identity $b-a=-(a-b)$ is handy, e.g. $\frac{1}{2-x}=-\frac{1}{x-2}$. Equations and inequalities with such expressions: Rational equations and inequalities.

5. Worked example

Perform the division and state for which $x$ the result holds: $$\frac{x^2-9}{x^2+3x}:\frac{x-3}{x^2}$$

  1. Domain: the denominators $x^2+3x=x(x+3)\ne0$ and $x^2\ne0$, and in addition the divisor $\frac{x-3}{x^2}\ne0$, that is $x\ne3$. So $x\notin\{-3,0,3\}$.
  2. Change to multiplication by the reciprocal and factor: $$\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x(x+3)}\cdot\frac{x^2}{x-3}$$
  3. Cancel $(x-3)$, $(x+3)$ and $x$.

$x$ for $x\in\R\setminus\{-3,0,3\}$

The condition $x\ne3$ is easy to lose: it comes from the numerator of the divisor.

6. Tasks

  1. easy
    Simplify the expression $\dfrac{x^2-6x+9}{x^2-9}$ and state the assumptions.
    Solution
    $\frac{(x-3)^2}{(x-3)(x+3)}$. $\frac{x-3}{x+3}$ for $x\ne\pm3$
  2. easy
    Compute $\dfrac{x^2-4}{3x}\cdot\dfrac{6x^2}{x+2}$.
    Solution
    $\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{3x}\cdot\frac{6x^2}{x+2}$, cancel $(x+2)$ and $3x$. $2x(x-2)$ for $x\notin\{-2,0\}$
  3. medium
    Compute $\dfrac{1}{x-2}-\dfrac{4}{x^2-4}$.
    Solution
    Common denominator $(x-2)(x+2)$: $\frac{x+2-4}{(x-2)(x+2)}=\frac{x-2}{(x-2)(x+2)}$. $\frac{1}{x+2}$ for $x\ne\pm2$
  4. medium
    Prove that for $x\ne\pm1$ the value of the expression $\dfrac{x}{x-1}-\dfrac{1}{x+1}-\dfrac{2}{x^2-1}$ does not depend on $x$.
    Solution
    Common denominator $(x-1)(x+1)$: $$\frac{x(x+1)-(x-1)-2}{(x-1)(x+1)}=\frac{x^2-1}{x^2-1}$$ The expression equals $1$. $\blacksquare$
  5. exam-level
    Simplify $\dfrac{x^3-8}{x^2-4}:\dfrac{x^2+2x+4}{x^2+4x+4}$.
    Solution
    $x^2+2x+4>0$ always ($\Delta<0$), so the divisor adds no new exclusions; $x^2-4\ne0$ and $(x+2)^2\ne0$ give $x\ne\pm2$. $$\frac{(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)}{(x-2)(x+2)}\cdot\frac{(x+2)^2}{x^2+2x+4}$$ $x+2$ for $x\ne\pm2$
  6. exam-level
    Find the numbers $A$, $B$ for which $\dfrac{5x+1}{(x-1)(x+2)}=\dfrac{A}{x-1}+\dfrac{B}{x+2}$ for all $x\notin\{-2,1\}$.
    Solution
    The right side over a common denominator: $\frac{A(x+2)+B(x-1)}{(x-1)(x+2)}$. The numerators are equal polynomials: $(A+B)x+(2A-B)=5x+1$ (comparing coefficients), so $A+B=5$, $2A-B=1$. $A=2$, $B=3$

7. On the exam

  • Write the assumptions at the start, from the denominators before canceling. When dividing, add the zeros of the divisor's numerator. Missing assumptions are a typical lost point.
  • Least common denominator: first factor the denominators ($x^2-1$, $x^2-x$, …).
  • At the end, check whether the result can be canceled further (factor the numerator).
  • A minus in front of a fraction applies to the whole numerator: $-\frac{x+1}{\ldots}$ is $\frac{-x-1}{\ldots}$.
