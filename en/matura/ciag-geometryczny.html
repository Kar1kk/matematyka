Geometric sequence

At each step you multiply by the same number $q$. An arithmetic sequence grows linearly (like simple interest), a geometric one exponentially (like compound interest), and for negative $q$ it jumps from one side of the axis to the other.

VI.4, VI.6, VI.7, I.8 (compound interest)

1. Definition and formulas

Definition A sequence $(a_n)$ is geometric if there is a number $q$ (the common ratio) such that $a_{n+1}=a_n\cdot q$ for every $n$. When the terms are nonzero: $\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=q$ is constant.
The $n$-th term on CKE sheet
$$a_n=a_1\cdot q^{n-1}$$
$q>1$
for $a_1>0$ increasing, for $a_1<0$ decreasing
$0<q<1$
for $a_1>0$ decreasing, for $a_1<0$ increasing; the terms tend to $0$
$q<0$
alternating signs: not monotonic (when $a_1\neq0$)
Sum of the first $n$ terms on CKE sheet
$$S_n=a_1\cdot\frac{1-q^n}{1-q},\quad q\neq1$$ $$S_n=n\cdot a_1,\quad q=1$$
Geometric mean of the neighbours on CKE sheet
$$a_n^2=a_{n-1}\cdot a_{n+1}$$
$x,y,z$
nonzero numbers form (in this order) a geometric sequence $\iff y^2=xz$
Without the assumption “nonzero” the condition $y^2=xz$ fails: $0, 0, 5$ satisfies it, yet it is not a geometric sequence (you cannot get $5$ from $0$ by multiplying).
Theorem In a geometric sequence $a_n=a_1q^{n-1}$, and for $q\neq1$: $S_n=a_1\cdot\frac{1-q^n}{1-q}$.
Proof (required on the exam)

The formula for $a_n$. $a_2=a_1q$, $a_3=a_2q=a_1q^2$, … each step adds one factor $q$. From $a_1$ to $a_n$ there are $n-1$ steps, so $a_n=a_1q^{n-1}$ (formally: induction on $n$).

The formula for $S_n$ (the $S_n-qS_n$ method). Multiply the sum by $q$. Since $a_kq=a_{k+1}$, each term shifts one place:

$$\begin{aligned}S_n&=a_1+a_2+\ldots+a_n\\ qS_n&=a_2+\ldots+a_n+a_nq\end{aligned}$$

After subtracting, everything cancels except $a_1$ from the top and $a_nq=a_1q^n$ from the bottom:

$$S_n-qS_n=a_1-a_1q^n$$ $$S_n(1-q)=a_1(1-q^n)$$

For $q\neq1$ we divide by $1-q$ and get the formula. For $q=1$ all terms equal $a_1$, so $S_n=na_1$.

Compound interest on CKE sheet
$$K_n=K_0\left(1+\frac{p}{100}\right)^n$$ $$K=K_0\left(1+\frac{p}{100\,m}\right)^{m\cdot n}$$
$K_0$
initial capital, $p$ – annual interest rate in %, $n$ – number of years
$m$
number of compounding periods per year (quarterly: $m=4$, monthly: $m=12$)
meaning
the account balances after consecutive periods form a geometric sequence with ratio $q=1+\frac{p}{100m}$

2. How the terms behave

Change $q$ and watch the shape: $|q|>1$ “explodes”, $|q|<1$ fades to $0$, $q<0$ jumps alternately above and below the axis. Empty circles at the edge = the term is outside the graph.

4. Step-by-step example

In a geometric sequence $a_3=12$ and $a_6=-96$. Find $S_7$.

  1. $a_6=a_3q^3$, so $q^3=-8$ and $q=-2$.
  2. $a_1=\frac{a_3}{q^2}=\frac{12}{4}=3$.
  3. $S_7=3\cdot\frac{1-(-2)^7}{1-(-2)}=3\cdot\frac{129}{3}=129$.

$S_7=129$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    In a geometric sequence $a_2=6$ and $a_5=48$. Find $S_6$.
    Solution
    $q^3=\frac{48}{6}=8$, $q=2$, $a_1=3$. $S_6=3\cdot\frac{1-2^6}{1-2}=3\cdot63$. $S_6=189$
  2. medium
    The numbers $x-1$, $x+3$, $5x+3$ form (in this order) a geometric sequence. Find $x$.
    Solution
    $(x+3)^2=(x-1)(5x+3)$, that is $x^2+6x+9=5x^2-2x-3$, $x^2-2x-3=0$, $x\in\{-1,3\}$. Check (terms nonzero): $x=3$: $2,6,18$ ($q=3$); $x=-1$: $-2,2,-2$ ($q=-1$). $x=3$ or $x=-1$
  3. medium
    $5000$ PLN was deposited at $4\%$ per year with quarterly compounding. How much will be in the account after $2$ years (ignore tax)?
    Solution
    Quarterly $\frac{4\%}{4}=1\%$, $8$ quarters: $K=5000\cdot1.01^8\approx5414.28$. $\approx 5414.28$ PLN
  4. medium
    How many initial terms of the sequence $1, 2, 4, 8, \ldots$ must be added for the sum to exceed $1000$?
    Solution
    $S_n=\frac{1-2^n}{1-2}=2^n-1>1000 \iff 2^n>1001$. $2^9=512$, $2^{10}=1024$. $n=10$ (then $S_{10}=1023$).
  5. exam-level
    Three numbers form a geometric sequence. Their sum is $7$ and their product is $8$. Find the numbers.
    Solution
    The numbers are $\frac{b}{q}, b, bq$. The product $b^3=8$, so $b=2$. The sum: $\frac2q+2+2q=7$, that is $2q^2-5q+2=0$, $q\in\{2,\frac12\}$. $1, 2, 4$ or $4, 2, 1$
  6. exam-level
    Three numbers with sum $15$ form an arithmetic sequence. If we add $1$, $4$ and $19$ to them respectively, we get a geometric sequence. Find these numbers.
    Solution
    The numbers are $5-r, 5, 5+r$ (sum $15$). After adding: $6-r,\ 9,\ 24+r$, so $81=(6-r)(24+r)$, that is $r^2+18r-63=0$, $r\in\{3,-21\}$. Check: $r=3$: $2,5,8 \to 3,9,27$; $r=-21$: $26,5,-16 \to 27,9,3$. $2,5,8$ or $26,5,-16$

6. On the exam

  • $q$ from two terms: $a_m=a_kq^{m-k}$. For even $m-k$ there are two solutions $q=\pm\ldots$ (e.g. $q^2=4$). Consider both.
  • The formula for $S_n$ only for $q\neq1$. If $q$ depends on a parameter, treat the case $q=1$ separately.
  • Three numbers: $\frac bq, b, bq$ (when the product is given) or the condition $y^2=xz$. Always check that the terms are nonzero.
  • Deposits: $p\%$ per year with compounding $m$ times a year gives $\frac{p}{m}\%$ per period and $mn$ periods. Round the result in zloty to the grosz (cent) only at the end.
