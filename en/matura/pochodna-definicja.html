Definition and interpretation of the derivative

The derivative is the slope of the graph at a single point. You take a secant through two nearby points and bring them closer together: the secant rotates and in the limit becomes the tangent.

XIII.R.3

1. The difference quotient and the derivative

Difference quotient (slope of the secant)
$$\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}=\frac{f(x_0+h)-f(x_0)}{h}$$
$h$
increment of the argument, $h\neq0$ (may be negative)
$\Delta y$
increment of the function value
Definition The derivative of a function $f$ at a point $x_0$ is the limit of the difference quotient (if it exists and is finite): $$f'(x_0)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x_0+h)-f(x_0)}{h}$$ Then $f$ is differentiable at $x_0$. The function $x\mapsto f'(x)$ is the derivative $f'$.
Geometric interpretation
$$f'(x_0)=\tan\alpha$$
$\alpha$
the angle between the tangent at $(x_0,f(x_0))$ and the positive $x$-axis
Physical interpretation
$$v(t)=s'(t),\quad a(t)=v'(t)$$
$s(t)$
position (distance) at time $t$
$v(t)$
instantaneous velocity

4. Step-by-step example

From the definition, find the derivative of $f(x)=x^2$ at a point $x_0$.

  1. $f(x_0+h)-f(x_0)=x_0^2+2x_0h+h^2-x_0^2=2x_0h+h^2$.
  2. The quotient: $\frac{2x_0h+h^2}{h}=2x_0+h$ (you can see this in the demo: the slope of the secant).
  3. $\lim\limits_{h\to0}(2x_0+h)=2x_0$.

$f'(x_0)=2x_0$, e.g. $f'(1)=2$.

From the definition, find the derivative of $f(x)=\frac1x$ at a point $x_0\neq0$.

  1. The difference: $\frac{1}{x_0+h}-\frac1{x_0}=\frac{x_0-(x_0+h)}{x_0(x_0+h)}=\frac{-h}{x_0(x_0+h)}$.
  2. The quotient: $\frac{-1}{x_0(x_0+h)}$.
  3. The limit as $h\to0$: $\frac{-1}{x_0\cdot x_0}$.

$f'(x_0)=-\frac{1}{x_0^2}$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    From the definition, find $f'(1)$ for $f(x)=x^2+3x$.
    Solution
    $f(1+h)-f(1)=(1+h)^2+3(1+h)-4=h^2+5h$. The quotient $h+5\to5$. $f'(1)=5$
  2. medium
    From the definition, find $f'(2)$ for $f(x)=x^3$.
    Solution
    $(2+h)^3-8=12h+6h^2+h^3$. The quotient $12+6h+h^2\to12$. $f'(2)=12$
  3. medium
    From the definition, find $f'(4)$ for $f(x)=\sqrt x$.
    Solution
    Multiply by the conjugate: $$\frac{\sqrt{4+h}-2}{h}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4+h}+2}$$ $\to\frac1{2+2}$. $f'(4)=\frac14$
  4. medium
    A body moves along a straight line, $s(t)=2t^2+t$ (metres, seconds). Find the average velocity on the interval $[3,5]$ and the instantaneous velocity at $t=3$.
    Solution
    Average: $\frac{s(5)-s(3)}{5-3}=\frac{55-21}{2}=17$. Instantaneous: the quotient $\frac{s(3+h)-s(3)}{h}=13+2h\to13$. $17$ m/s and $13$ m/s.
  5. exam-level
    Justify from the definition that $f(x)=|x|$ has no derivative at $x_0=0$.
    Solution
    The quotient: $\frac{|h|-0}{h}$. For $h>0$ it equals $1$, for $h<0$ it equals $-1$. The one-sided limits satisfy $1\neq-1$. The limit of the quotient does not exist, so $f'(0)$ does not exist (the graph has a “corner”).
  6. exam-level
    $f(x)=x^2$ for $x\le1$, $f(x)=ax+b$ for $x>1$. Choose $a$, $b$ so that $f$ is differentiable at $1$.
    Solution
    Differentiable ⇒ continuous: $a+b=1$. The quotient from the left $\to2$ (the derivative of $x^2$), from the right: $\frac{a(1+h)+b-1}{h}=a$ (when $a+b=1$). So $a=2$, $b=-1$. $a=2$, $b=-1$ (the line $y=2x-1$ is tangent to $y=x^2$ at $1$).

6. On the exam

  • “From the definition” means: difference quotient, simplification, limit. A formula from the tables is not enough.
  • Cancel $h$ only after factoring it out. Do not substitute $h=0$ before cancelling (you get $\frac00$).
  • A differentiable function is continuous. The converse is false: $|x|$ is continuous at $0$, yet has no derivative.
  • Physical interpretation: velocity $=s'$, acceleration $=v'$. Average velocity is a difference quotient, instantaneous velocity is the limit.
