Trigonometry in a right triangle

All right triangles with the same acute angle $\alpha$ are similar, so the ratios of their sides are the same. These ratios are exactly $\sin\alpha$, $\cos\alpha$, $\tan\alpha$: they depend only on the angle, not on the size of the triangle.

VII.4, VIII.12 (also VII.1)

1. Definitions

Definition In a right triangle with hypotenuse $c$ and acute angle $\alpha$, opposite to which lies leg $a$ and adjacent to which lies leg $b$: $$\sin\alpha=\frac{a}{c},\quad \cos\alpha=\frac{b}{c},\quad \tan\alpha=\frac{a}{b}$$ In words: sine = opposite / hypotenuse, cosine = adjacent / hypotenuse, tangent = opposite / adjacent.
Relations on CKE sheet
$$\sin^2\alpha+\cos^2\alpha=1$$ $$\tan\alpha=\frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}$$
proof
$\frac{a^2+b^2}{c^2}=\frac{c^2}{c^2}=1$ (Pythagoras); $\;\frac{a/c}{b/c}=\frac{a}{b}$
Complementary angles ($\alpha+\beta=90^\circ$)
$$\sin(90^\circ-\alpha)=\cos\alpha$$ $$\cos(90^\circ-\alpha)=\sin\alpha$$ $$\tan\alpha\cdot\tan(90^\circ-\alpha)=1$$
why
for the other acute angle the roles of legs $a$ and $b$ are swapped
$\alpha$$30^\circ$$45^\circ$$60^\circ$
$\sin\alpha$$\frac12$$\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$$\frac{\sqrt3}{2}$
$\cos\alpha$$\frac{\sqrt3}{2}$$\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$$\frac12$
$\tan\alpha$$\frac{\sqrt3}{3}$$1$$\sqrt3$

The values for $30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ$ are in the tables; where they come from (half a square, half an equilateral triangle), see the topic angles 0°–180°.

For an acute angle $0<\sin\alpha<1$ and $0<\cos\alpha<1$ (a leg is shorter than the hypotenuse), while $\tan\alpha$ can be any positive number.

2. Interactive: the ratios depend only on the angle

Drag vertex $B$ (changes the angle and the size) and point $D$ (a smaller similar triangle). The ratios of the sides in both triangles are always equal.

When the hypotenuse has length $1$, the legs are the sine and cosine. The tangent is the segment on the tangent line $x=1$ (a similar triangle with leg $1$). This is a bridge to the unit circle.

3. Method

  1. Mark the right angle and the angle you are asked about. Name the sides relative to that angle: opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse.
  2. Choose the function that links the known side and the unknown side (without the hypotenuse → $\tan$).
  3. When one function is given and another is needed: draw a triangle with sides from the fraction and find the third side from Pythagoras.

4. Step-by-step example

An acute angle $\alpha$ satisfies $\sin\alpha=\frac{5}{13}$. Find $\cos\alpha$ and $\tan\alpha$.

  1. A triangle with opposite side $5$ and hypotenuse $13$.
  2. Adjacent: $\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=\sqrt{144}=12$.
  3. $\cos\alpha=\frac{12}{13}$, $\;\tan\alpha=\frac{5}{12}$.

$\cos\alpha=\frac{12}{13},\ \tan\alpha=\frac{5}{12}$

The same from the Pythagorean identity: $\cos\alpha=\sqrt{1-\frac{25}{169}}$ (positive root, because the angle is acute).

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    In right triangle $ABC$ the angle at $C$ is right, $|AC|=6$ and $\tan\angle BAC=\frac43$. Find the perimeter of the triangle.
    Solution
    $\tan\angle A=\frac{|BC|}{|AC|}$, so $|BC|=6\cdot\frac43=8$, $|AB|=\sqrt{36+64}=10$. Perimeter $24$.
  2. easy
    The hypotenuse of a right triangle has length $10$, and one of the acute angles is $30^\circ$. Find the lengths of the legs and the area.
    Solution
    Opposite $30^\circ$: $10\sin30^\circ=5$; adjacent to it: $10\cos30^\circ=5\sqrt3$. Area $\frac12\cdot5\cdot5\sqrt3$. $5$, $5\sqrt3$, $P=\frac{25\sqrt3}{2}$
  3. medium
    A leg of an isosceles triangle has length $10$, and the base angle is $30^\circ$. Find the area of the triangle.
    Solution
    The altitude divides it into two right triangles: $h=10\sin30^\circ=5$, half of the base is $10\cos30^\circ=5\sqrt3$, the base is $10\sqrt3$. $P=\frac12\cdot10\sqrt3\cdot5$. $P=25\sqrt3$
  4. medium
    Prove that for every acute angle $\alpha$ we have $\sin\alpha+\cos\alpha>1$.
    Solution
    In a right triangle with angle $\alpha$: $\sin\alpha+\cos\alpha=\frac{a+b}{c}$. By the triangle inequality $a+b>c$, so $\sin\alpha+\cos\alpha>1$. (Algebraically: $(\sin\alpha+\cos\alpha)^2=1+2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha>1$.)
  5. exam-level
    From point $A$ the top of a tower is seen at an angle of $30^\circ$ to the horizontal. After walking $20$ m towards the tower (on level ground) the angle is $60^\circ$. Find the height of the tower.
    Solution
    Let $h$ be the height and $x$ the distance of the second point from the base of the tower. $\tan60^\circ=\frac{h}{x}$, $\tan30^\circ=\frac{h}{x+20}$, so $x=\frac{h}{\sqrt3}$ and $x+20=h\sqrt3$. Subtracting: $20=h\sqrt3-\frac{h}{\sqrt3}=\frac{2h}{\sqrt3}$, hence $h=10\sqrt3\approx17.3$. $h=10\sqrt3$ m

6. On the exam

  • Relative to which angle? “Opposite” changes when you change the angle. The most common mistake: $\sin$ computed for the wrong acute angle.
  • Find the value of another function from an auxiliary triangle or from the Pythagorean identity, always with a justification of the sign (“the angle is acute, so $\cos\alpha>0$”).
  • Leave the result exact ($10\sqrt3$), do not round unless the problem says so. The CKE formula sheet contains a table of values of trigonometric functions, but in problems exact values usually suffice.
  • In problems with figures (rhombus, trapezoid, altitude) look for a right triangle: an altitude or a diagonal usually forms one.
