Limit of a sequence, the squeeze theorem

$g$ is the limit when around $g$ you can draw an arbitrarily narrow strip and still, from some point on, all the terms fit inside it. In practice you compute limits with a few techniques: dividing by the highest power, the conjugate, the squeeze theorem.

VI.R.1

1. Definition and basic limits

Definition A sequence $(a_n)$ is convergent to $g$ (we write $\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}a_n=g$) when for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $N$ such that for all $n\ge N$: $$|a_n-g|<\varepsilon$$ In words: in every strip $(g-\varepsilon,\,g+\varepsilon)$ lie almost all the terms (all except finitely many). A sequence that has no limit is divergent.
Divergence to $\pm\infty$ $\lim a_n=+\infty$ when for every $M$ almost all terms are $>M$ (analogously $-\infty$: $<M$). E.g. $n^2$, $\sqrt n$, $2^n$ tend to $+\infty$, while $(-1)^n$ and $(-2)^n$ have no limit, neither finite nor infinite.
Limits you rely on
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^k}=0\quad(k>0)$$ $$\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{a}=1\quad(a>0)$$ $$\lim_{n\to\infty}q^n=0\quad(|q|<1)$$
also
$\frac{c}{n}\to0$, $\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\to0$, $\left(\frac23\right)^n\to0$; $\ q^n\to+\infty$ for $q>1$
Theorem (operations on limits) If $\lim a_n=a$ and $\lim b_n=b$ (numbers), then $$\lim(a_n\pm b_n)=a\pm b$$ $$\lim(a_n\cdot b_n)=a\cdot b$$ $$\lim\frac{a_n}{b_n}=\frac ab\quad(b\neq0\ \text{and}\ b_n\neq0)$$ With infinities: if $a_n\to+\infty$, then $\frac{1}{a_n}\to0$; if $a_n\to+\infty$ and $b_n\to b>0$, then $a_nb_n\to+\infty$; if $a_n\to0$ and $a_n>0$, then $\frac1{a_n}\to+\infty$.
Indeterminate forms: $\left[\frac{\infty}{\infty}\right]$, $[\infty-\infty]$, $[0\cdot\infty]$, $\left[\frac00\right]$. The result can be anything, so first transform the expression. $\infty-\infty$ is not $0$, and $\frac\infty\infty$ is not $1$.
Squeeze theorem If $a_n\le b_n\le c_n$ (for almost all $n$) and $\lim a_n=\lim c_n=g$, then $\lim b_n=g$.
Why it works (sketch)

Take the strip $(g-\varepsilon,g+\varepsilon)$. From some point on $a_n$ and $c_n$ are in the strip. $b_n$ lies between them, and the strip is an interval, so it is in the strip too. The strip was arbitrary, hence $b_n\to g$.

4. Method

  • Quotient of polynomials $\left[\frac\infty\infty\right]$: divide the numerator and denominator by the highest power of the denominator. Result: equal degrees → the ratio of the leading coefficients; lower numerator → $0$; higher numerator → $\pm\infty$.
  • Powers $2^n, 5^n$: divide by the power with the largest base (then the rest is $q^n\to0$).
  • Difference of square roots $[\infty-\infty]$: multiply and divide by the conjugate, $\sqrt A-\sqrt B=\frac{A-B}{\sqrt A+\sqrt B}$.
  • Bounded expressions ($\sin n$, $(-1)^n$) or $\sqrt[n]{\ldots}$: estimate from both sides and use the squeeze theorem.

5. Worked example

Find $\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{3n^2-n+1}{1-2n^2}$.

  1. The form $\left[\frac\infty\infty\right]$; divide the numerator and denominator by $n^2$: $$\frac{3-\frac1n+\frac1{n^2}}{\frac1{n^2}-2}$$
  2. $\frac1n\to0$, $\frac1{n^2}\to0$, so by the theorem on the limit of a quotient: $\frac{3-0+0}{0-2}$.

$-\frac32$

Find $\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left(\sqrt{n^2+4n}-n\right)$.

  1. The form $[\infty-\infty]$; multiply by the conjugate: $$\frac{(n^2+4n)-n^2}{\sqrt{n^2+4n}+n}$$ $$=\frac{4n}{\sqrt{n^2+4n}+n}$$
  2. Divide by $n$ (under the root by $n^2$, since $n=\sqrt{n^2}$ for $n>0$): $$\frac{4}{\sqrt{1+\frac4n}+1}\to\frac{4}{1+1}$$

6. Exercises

  1. easy
    Find $\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{2n^3-n}{4n^3+5n^2+1}$.
    Solution
    Divide by $n^3$: $\frac{2-\frac1{n^2}}{4+\frac5n+\frac1{n^3}}\to\frac24$. $\frac12$
  2. easy
    Find the limits: a) $\lim\frac{n^2+1}{n+3}$, b) $\lim\frac{n+1}{n^2+3}$.
    Solution
    a) Divide by $n$: $\frac{n+\frac1n}{1+\frac3n}$; the numerator $\to+\infty$, the denominator $\to1>0$. $+\infty$
    b) Divide by $n^2$: $\frac{\frac1n+\frac1{n^2}}{1+\frac3{n^2}}\to\frac01$. $0$
  3. medium
    Find $\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{2^{n+1}+5^n}{5^{n+1}-3}$.
    Solution
    Divide by $5^n$: $\frac{2\cdot\left(\frac25\right)^n+1}{5-\frac{3}{5^n}}$. Since $\left(\frac25\right)^n\to0$ and $\frac3{5^n}\to0$: $\frac15$
  4. medium
    Find $\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left(\sqrt{n^2+3n}-\sqrt{n^2-n}\right)$.
    Solution
    Conjugate: $\frac{(n^2+3n)-(n^2-n)}{\sqrt{n^2+3n}+\sqrt{n^2-n}}=\frac{4n}{\sqrt{n^2+3n}+\sqrt{n^2-n}}$. Divide by $n$: $\frac{4}{\sqrt{1+\frac3n}+\sqrt{1-\frac1n}}\to\frac42$. $2$
  5. exam-level
    Find $\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{4^n+7^n}$.
    Solution
    Estimate: $7^n\le4^n+7^n\le2\cdot7^n$, so $7\le\sqrt[n]{4^n+7^n}\le7\sqrt[n]2$. $\lim7=7$ and $\lim7\sqrt[n]2=7\cdot1=7$. By the squeeze theorem: $7$
  6. exam-level
    Find $p$ for which $\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{(p-1)n^2+n}{1+3+5+\ldots+(2n-1)}=3$.
    Solution
    The denominator is the sum of $n$ terms of an arithmetic sequence: $\frac{1+(2n-1)}{2}\cdot n=n^2$. Then $\frac{(p-1)n^2+n}{n^2}=p-1+\frac1n\to p-1$. The condition is $p-1=3$. $p=4$

7. On the exam

  • Justify: write what you divide by and which terms tend to $0$. The bare result without transformations usually does not earn full points.
  • Most common mistakes: $\infty-\infty=0$, $\frac\infty\infty=1$, taking $n$ out from under the root without squaring ($\sqrt{n^2+4n}=n\sqrt{1+\frac4n}$, and not $n\sqrt{n+4}$).
  • Squeeze theorem: write both inequalities, compute the limits of both outer sequences and cite the theorem by name.
  • A limit only makes sense for $n\to\infty$: do not substitute "$n=\infty$", just use $\frac1n\to0$.
