Total probability

When an experiment has two stages (first choosing an urn, then a ball), split the world into cases $B_1, B_2,\ldots$ and add up the contribution of each. $P(A)$ is a weighted average of the probabilities $P(A|B_i)$ with weights $P(B_i)$.

XII.R.1

1. Partition and the theorem

A partition of the sample space (complete system of events) $B_1,\ldots,B_n$: pairwise disjoint events with positive probabilities which together make up $\Omega$. Exactly one of them always occurs (e.g. "urn I was chosen", "urn II was chosen").
Law of total probability on CKE sheet $$P(A)=P(A|B_1)P(B_1)+\ldots$$ $$\ldots+P(A|B_n)P(B_n)$$
Proof
$A=(A\cap B_1)\cup\ldots\cup(A\cap B_n)$, and these sets are pairwise disjoint (because the $B_i$ are). Hence $P(A)=\sum P(A\cap B_i)$, and from the definition of conditional probability $P(A\cap B_i)=P(A|B_i)\,P(B_i)$.
On the tree
$$P(A)=\sum_{\text{branches to }A}\text{product}$$
along
you multiply (this is $P(B_i)\cdot P(A|B_i)$)
across
you add the branches that end in $A$
check
at each node the probabilities of the outgoing branches add up to $1$

2. Interactive: the tree with balls

Three cases $B_1, B_2, B_3$ (e.g. three machines). The thickness of a branch is the probability of reaching it from the root. The red leaves are $A$. Drop the balls: each one randomly chooses a path, and the frequency of hitting $A$ approaches $P(A)$.

3. Animation: $P(A)$ is the leveled height

The width of a bar is $P(B_i)$, the height is $P(A|B_i)$, so the blue area is $P(A)$. "Level out": the heights even out like water in connected vessels, the area does not change. The common level is $P(A)$. The settings are taken from the tree above.

4. Step-by-step example

We roll a die. If $1$ or $2$ comes up, we draw a ball from urn I ($2$ white, $3$ black), otherwise from urn II ($4$ white, $1$ black). Find the probability of drawing a white ball.

  1. Partition: $U_1$ (urn I), $P(U_1)=\frac26=\frac13$; $U_2$, $P(U_2)=\frac23$.
  2. Branches to "white": $\frac13\cdot\frac25=\frac{2}{15}$ and $\frac23\cdot\frac45=\frac{8}{15}$.
  3. $P(A)=\frac{2}{15}+\frac{8}{15}=\frac{10}{15}$.

$P(A)=\frac23$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Urn I: $4$ white and $1$ black, urn II: $2$ white and $3$ black. We toss a coin and draw a ball from urn I (heads) or II (tails). Find the probability of drawing a white ball.
    Solution
    $P(A)=\frac12\cdot\frac45+\frac12\cdot\frac25=\frac{6}{10}$. $P(A)=\frac35$
  2. medium
    Three production lines make $50\%$, $30\%$ and $20\%$ of the products respectively. The defect rates on them are $2\%$, $3\%$ and $5\%$. Find the probability that a randomly chosen product is defective.
    Solution
    $P(W)=0.5\cdot0.02+0.3\cdot0.03+0.2\cdot0.05=0.01+0.009+0.01$. $P(W)=0.029=\frac{29}{1000}$
  3. medium
    Urn I: $3$ white and $2$ black, urn II: $2$ white and $2$ black. We draw one ball from urn I and put it into urn II, then draw a ball from urn II. Find the probability that it is white.
    Solution
    Transferred ball white ($\frac35$): urn II has $3$ white out of $5$. Transferred ball black ($\frac25$): $2$ white out of $5$. $P(A)=\frac35\cdot\frac35+\frac25\cdot\frac25=\frac{9+4}{25}$. $P(A)=\frac{13}{25}$
  4. exam-level
    An urn contains $4$ white and $3$ black balls. Two balls are drawn in succession without replacement. Find the probability that the second one is white.
    Solution
    Partition: first white / first black. $P(A)=\frac47\cdot\frac36+\frac37\cdot\frac46=\frac{12+12}{42}=\frac{24}{42}$. The result equal to $P(\text{first white})$ is no accident: without knowing the first ball, the second is "just as random". $P(A)=\frac47$
  5. exam-level
    We roll a die, and then toss a coin as many times as the number of pips that came up. Find the probability that at least one head comes up.
    Solution
    $B_k$: the die shows $k$, $P(B_k)=\frac16$. $P(A|B_k)=1-\left(\frac12\right)^k$ (complement: all tails). $P(A')=\frac16\left(\frac12+\frac14+\ldots+\frac1{64}\right)=\frac16\cdot\frac{63}{64}=\frac{21}{128}$. $P(A)=\frac{107}{128}$
  6. exam-level
    Urn I contains $n$ white balls and $2$ black ones, urn II: $3$ white and $1$ black. We choose an urn at random (coin toss) and a ball from it. The probability of drawing a white one is $\frac34$. Find $n$.
    Solution
    $\frac12\cdot\frac{n}{n+2}+\frac12\cdot\frac34=\frac34$, so $\frac{n}{n+2}=\frac34$, $4n=3n+6$. $n=6$

6. On the exam

  • Name the partition ("$B_1$: urn I was chosen, $B_2$: …") and write the formula with concrete numbers. A labeled tree (events at the nodes, numbers on the branches) is also a good way to write it.
  • Checking the tree: the branches from one node add up to $1$. A common mistake: the number of balls in the urn after a ball is transferred (the denominator changes!).
  • When after $P(A)$ comes the question "given that $A$ occurred, what is the probability of $B_1$?", that is Bayes' formula: $\frac{P(A|B_1)P(B_1)}{P(A)}$.
  • Give the result as a fraction in lowest terms or an exact decimal.
