Quadrilaterals and regular polygons

Every “special” quadrilateral is a parallelogram, a trapezoid or a kite with extra conditions – and the diagonals tell you almost everything: do they bisect each other, are they equal, are they perpendicular. A regular polygon is $n$ identical isosceles triangles around a center.

VIII.3, VIII.4, VIII.12

1. Quadrilaterals: angles and diagonals

The angles of a quadrilateral add up to $360^\circ$ (a diagonal splits it into two triangles); for an $n$-gon: $(n-2)\cdot180^\circ$.

4. Worked example

A regular hexagon has side $4$. Find its area and the lengths of its diagonals.

  1. The diagonals through the center split it into 6 equilateral triangles with side $4$: $P=6\cdot\frac{16\sqrt3}{4}=24\sqrt3$.
  2. The long diagonal is two sides of such triangles: $2\cdot4=8$.
  3. The short diagonal is two altitudes: $2\cdot\frac{4\sqrt3}{2}=4\sqrt3$.

$P=24\sqrt3$, diagonals $8$ and $4\sqrt3$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Find the interior angle and the number of diagonals of a regular dodecagon.
    Solution
    $\frac{10\cdot180^\circ}{12}=150^\circ$, $\frac{12\cdot9}{2}=54$. $150^\circ$, $54$ diagonals
  2. easy
    The interior angle of a regular polygon is $140^\circ$. How many sides does this polygon have?
    Solution
    The exterior angle is $180^\circ-140^\circ=40^\circ=\frac{360^\circ}{n}$, so $n=9$.
  3. medium
    The diagonals of a rhombus have lengths $10$ and $24$. Find the side, the area and the height of the rhombus.
    Solution
    The diagonals bisect each other at a right angle: $a=\sqrt{5^2+12^2}=13$. $P=\frac12\cdot10\cdot24=120$, $h=\frac{P}{a}=\frac{120}{13}$. $a=13$, $P=120$, $h=\frac{120}{13}$
  4. medium
    An isosceles trapezoid has bases $14$ and $4$ and leg $13$. Find its height, area and the length of its diagonal.
    Solution
    The foot of the altitude cuts off $\frac{14-4}{2}=5$ from the longer base: $h=\sqrt{169-25}=12$. $P=\frac{14+4}{2}\cdot12=108$. The diagonal is the hypotenuse of a triangle with legs $h=12$ and $14-5=9$: $\sqrt{144+81}=15$. $h=12$, $P=108$, $d=15$
  5. exam-level
    A parallelogram has sides $6$ and $10$ and an acute angle $60^\circ$. Find its area and the lengths of its diagonals.
    Solution
    $P=6\cdot10\cdot\sin60^\circ=30\sqrt3$. The shorter diagonal lies opposite the $60^\circ$ angle, the longer opposite $120^\circ$ (law of cosines): $$d_1^2=36+100-120\cdot\tfrac12=76$$ $$d_2^2=36+100+120\cdot\tfrac12=196$$ $P=30\sqrt3$, $d_1=2\sqrt{19}$, $d_2=14$
  6. exam-level
    In trapezoid $ABCD$ ($AB\parallel CD$) the diagonals meet at point $S$. $|AB|=12$, $|CD|=4$, and the area of triangle $CDS$ is $3$. Find the area of the trapezoid.
    Solution
    $\triangle ABS\sim\triangle CDS$ (alternate angles) with scale $k=3$, so $P_{ABS}=9\cdot3=27$ (areas of similar figures). $|AS|=3|SC|$, and triangles $BSC$ and $BSA$ share the altitude from $B$, so $P_{BSC}=\frac{27}{3}=9$; likewise $P_{ASD}=9$. $P=3+27+9+9$. $P=48$

6. On the exam

  • Trapezoid: almost always draw two altitudes from the ends of the shorter base – you get two right triangles and a rectangle.
  • Rhombus: the halves of the diagonals and the side form a right triangle. This is the key to 90% of rhombus problems.
  • Do not assume from the picture that the diagonals are perpendicular or equal – you may use only the properties of the given figure.
  • Regular polygon: join the center to the vertices – you get $n$ isosceles triangles with angle $\frac{360^\circ}{n}$ at the center and legs $R$.
