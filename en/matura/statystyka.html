Statistics: mean, weighted mean, median, mode

The mean is the point at which data lying on a rod would balance. The median is the “middle” element after the data are put in order. A single extreme value moves the mean a lot, and the median hardly at all.

XII.2

1. Four measures and formulas

Arithmetic mean on CKE sheet
$$\bar{x}=\frac{x_1+x_2+\ldots+x_n}{n}$$
$n$
the number of data values
Weighted mean on CKE sheet
$$\bar{x}_w=\frac{w_1x_1+\ldots+w_nx_n}{w_1+\ldots+w_n}$$
$w_i$
the weight of the value $x_i$ (a positive number). For a frequency table the weight is the frequency
Median $Me$: arrange the data in non-decreasing order. For odd $n$ it is the middle term $x_{\frac{n+1}{2}}$, for even $n$ the mean of the two middle ones: $$Me=\frac{x_{n/2}+x_{n/2+1}}{2}$$
Mode $Do$: the value that occurs most often. There may be several. When all values occur the same number of times, we say there is no mode.
The “balance” property The sum of deviations from the mean is zero: $\;(x_1-\bar x)+\ldots+(x_n-\bar x)=0$.
Why?
$\sum (x_i-\bar x)=\sum x_i - n\bar x = n\bar x-n\bar x=0$. Physically: equal weights at the points $x_i$ on a weightless rod balance exactly on a support at $\bar x$ (the torques on the left and right cancel).
Variance and standard deviation were dropped from the 2024 core curriculum. They are not in the 2024 requirements, but the formulas are on the CKE sheet (more: removed from the curriculum).

4. Step-by-step example

$20$ students were asked how many siblings they have. Find the mean, median and mode.

number of siblings01234
number of students49511
  1. The mean is the mean weighted by the frequencies: $\bar x=\frac{0\cdot4+1\cdot9+2\cdot5+3\cdot1+4\cdot1}{20}=\frac{26}{20}=1.3$.
  2. $n=20$ is even, so the median is the mean of the 10th and 11th terms. Cumulative frequencies: $4,\ 13,\ldots$, so terms 5 to 13 equal $1$. Hence $Me=\frac{1+1}{2}=1$.
  3. The most frequent value (9 times) is $1$.

$\bar x=1.3$, $\;Me=1$, $\;Do=1$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Find the mean, median and mode of the data: $7,\ 3,\ 9,\ 3,\ 5,\ 8,\ 3,\ 6$.
    Solution
    The sum is $44$, so $\bar x=\frac{44}{8}=5.5$. Sorted: $3,3,3,5,6,7,8,9$, the middle ones are $5$ and $6$. $\bar x=5.5$, $Me=5.5$, $Do=3$
  2. easy
    The chart shows the grades from a class test. Find the mean and median of the grades and the percentage of students who got at least $4$.
    Solution
    The frequencies are $2,3,8,6,4,2$, in total $n=25$. The sum of the grades is $2+6+24+24+20+12=88$, so $\bar x=\frac{88}{25}=3.52$. The median is the 13th term: cumulative $2,5,13$, so the 13th grade is $3$. At least $4$ was obtained by $6+4+2=12$ people, $\frac{12}{25}=48\%$. $\bar x=3.52$, $Me=3$, $48\%$
  3. medium
    The arithmetic mean of the numbers $2,\ 5,\ x,\ 7,\ 9,\ 3$ is $5$. Find $x$ and the median of these numbers.
    Solution
    $\frac{26+x}{6}=5$, so $x=4$. Sorted: $2,3,4,5,7,9$, the middle ones are $4$ and $5$. $x=4$, $Me=4.5$
  4. medium
    Grades: tests (weight 3): $4,\ 5$; quizzes (weight 2): $3$ and one more; oral answer (weight 1): $5$. What grade (from 1 to 6) must be obtained on the last quiz for the weighted mean to be at least $4.5$?
    Solution
    The sum of the weights is $3+3+2+2+1=11$. The condition: $\frac{12+15+6+2x+5}{11}\ge4.5$, that is $38+2x\ge49.5$, $x\ge5.75$. Check: $x=6$ gives $\frac{50}{11}\approx4.55$, while $x=5$ gives only $\frac{48}{11}\approx4.36$. The student must get $6$.
  5. exam-level
    A set of five natural numbers has mean $6$, median $7$ and exactly one mode, equal to $8$. Find all such sets.
    Solution
    Order them: $a\le b\le 7\le d\le e$, sum $30$. The number $8$ must occur at least twice and is greater than the median, so $d=e=8$. Then $a+b=30-7-16=7$. No other number may occur twice (there would be two modes), so $a<b<7$. The pairs: $(1,6),(2,5),(3,4)$ (the pair $(0,7)$ is out: $7$ would occur twice). $\{1,6,7,8,8\}$, $\{2,5,7,8,8\}$, $\{3,4,7,8,8\}$

6. On the exam

  • Median: always sort the data first. The most common mistake is taking the middle number of an unsorted list.
  • Frequency table / chart: the mean is a weighted mean (you multiply each value by its frequency). Find the median from the cumulative frequencies, without listing all the data.
  • Even $n$: the median is the mean of the two middle values and may not be any of the data.
  • Changing the data: adding a number $x$ changes the sum by $x$ and the number of data values by $1$. Work with the sum: $S=n\cdot\bar x$.
  • There may be several modes. Then list them all.
