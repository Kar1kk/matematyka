Reduction formulas

The point for the angle $90^\circ\pm\alpha$, $180^\circ\pm\alpha$, $270^\circ\pm\alpha$ or $360^\circ-\alpha$ is the image of the point for $\alpha$ under a rotation or a reflection. The triangle underneath is the same, so the coordinates just swap places or change sign. Do not memorize the table – read it off the circle.

VII.R.4

1. The rule in two steps

Rule For an expression $f(\text{angle}\pm\alpha)$, where $f\in\{\sin,\cos,\tan\}$:
  1. Function: at $90^\circ$ and $270^\circ$ ($\frac\pi2$, $\frac{3\pi}2$) swap $\sin\leftrightarrow\cos$, $\;\tan\to\frac{1}{\tan}$. At $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$ ($\pi$, $2\pi$) the function stays.
  2. Sign: pretend that $\alpha$ is small and acute. Determine the quadrant of the angle on the left-hand side and give the sign that the original function $f$ has there.

Example: $\cos(270^\circ+\alpha)$. The angle $270^\circ$ → swap to $\sin$. $270^\circ+\alpha$ lies in quadrant IV, where $\cos>0$. Result: $+\sin\alpha$.

In radians it is the same: $\frac\pi2$, $\pi$, $\frac{3\pi}2$, $2\pi$. The formulas work for every $\alpha$ (in the domain), not only an acute one. $\frac1{\tan\alpha}$ is written $\cot\alpha$ in textbooks. In addition, parity: $\sin(-\alpha)=-\sin\alpha$, $\cos(-\alpha)=\cos\alpha$, $\tan(-\alpha)=-\tan\alpha$.

Reduction formulas are special cases of the formulas for $\sin(\alpha\pm\beta)$, $\cos(\alpha\pm\beta)$ on CKE sheet. If you forget the rule, expand e.g. $\cos(270^\circ+\alpha)=\cos270^\circ\cos\alpha-\sin270^\circ\sin\alpha=\sin\alpha$.

2. Interactive: where it comes from

Drag $P$ (angle $\alpha$) and choose an angle. The green segment has length $|\cos\alpha|$, the red one $|\sin\alpha|$. At $90^\circ$ and $270^\circ$ the colors at point $Q$ swap axes – that is the swap $\sin\leftrightarrow\cos$. "▶ Show the motion" animates the rotation or reflection.

Practicing the rule: draw a random expression and choose an answer. After answering you will see the justification and the angle on the circle (for $\alpha=25^\circ$).

3. Step-by-step example

Simplify the expression $\dfrac{\sin(\pi-\alpha)\cdot\cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}+\alpha\right)}{\tan(\pi+\alpha)\cdot\cos\left(\frac\pi2-\alpha\right)}$.

  1. $\sin(\pi-\alpha)=\sin\alpha$ (quadrant II, $\sin>0$).
  2. $\cos\left(\frac{3\pi}2+\alpha\right)=\sin\alpha$ (swap; quadrant IV, $\cos>0$).
  3. $\tan(\pi+\alpha)=\tan\alpha$ (quadrant III, $\tan>0$), $\;\cos\left(\frac\pi2-\alpha\right)=\sin\alpha$.
  4. $\dfrac{\sin\alpha\cdot\sin\alpha}{\tan\alpha\cdot\sin\alpha}=\dfrac{\sin\alpha}{\sin\alpha/\cos\alpha}=\cos\alpha$.

$\cos\alpha$

4. Tasks

  1. easy
    Compute: a) $\cos300^\circ$, b) $\tan210^\circ$, c) $\sin(-135^\circ)$.
    Solution
    a) $\cos(360^\circ-60^\circ)=\cos60^\circ$; b) $\tan(180^\circ+30^\circ)=\tan30^\circ$; c) $-\sin135^\circ=-\sin(180^\circ-45^\circ)=-\sin45^\circ$. a) $\frac12$, b) $\frac{\sqrt3}{3}$, c) $-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$
  2. easy
    Compute $\sin\frac{11\pi}{6}$.
    Solution
    $\sin\left(2\pi-\frac\pi6\right)=-\sin\frac\pi6$ (quadrant IV, $\sin<0$). $-\frac12$
  3. medium
    Compute $\sin\left(\frac\pi2+\alpha\right)\cos(\pi-\alpha)+\cos\left(\frac\pi2+\alpha\right)\sin(\pi-\alpha)$.
    Solution
    $\cos\alpha\cdot(-\cos\alpha)+(-\sin\alpha)\cdot\sin\alpha=-(\cos^2\alpha+\sin^2\alpha)$. $-1$ (for every $\alpha$)
  4. medium
    $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ are the angles of a triangle. Show that $\sin(\alpha+\beta)=\sin\gamma$ and $\cos(\alpha+\beta)=-\cos\gamma$.
    Solution
    $\alpha+\beta=180^\circ-\gamma$, so $\sin(180^\circ-\gamma)=\sin\gamma$, $\cos(180^\circ-\gamma)=-\cos\gamma$. Both equalities hold.
  5. exam-level
    Compute $\tan1^\circ\cdot\tan2^\circ\cdot\tan3^\circ\cdot\ldots\cdot\tan89^\circ$.
    Solution
    Pair up $k^\circ$ and $(90-k)^\circ$: $\tan k^\circ\cdot\tan(90^\circ-k^\circ)=\tan k^\circ\cdot\frac{1}{\tan k^\circ}=1$. There are $44$ pairs, and $\tan45^\circ=1$ is left in the middle. $1$

5. On the exam

  • You take the sign from the original function (the one before the reduction), not from the result. This is the most common mistake.
  • First shrink a large angle by the period ($-2k\pi$), only then reduce: $\cos\frac{23\pi}{6}=\cos\left(4\pi-\frac\pi6\right)=\cos\frac\pi6$.
  • In a triangle $\alpha+\beta+\gamma=\pi$ – reduction formulas turn $\sin(\alpha+\beta)$ into $\sin\gamma$. This appears in proofs.
  • In closed tasks check the result for $\alpha=30^\circ$: a quick sign test.
