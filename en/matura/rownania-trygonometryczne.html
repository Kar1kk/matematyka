Trigonometric equations

You reduce every equation to $\sin x=m$, $\cos x=m$ or $\tan x=m$. On the unit circle such an equation usually has two points; then you add full turns $+2k\pi$ (for the tangent $+k\pi$).

VII.R.6

1. Elementary equations

$\sin x=m$, $\;|m|\le1$
$$x=x_0+2k\pi\ \vee\ x=\pi-x_0+2k\pi$$
$x_0$
any solution, e.g. from $\left[-\frac\pi2,\frac\pi2\right]$; for $|m|>1$ there are no solutions
$\cos x=m$, $\;|m|\le1$
$$x=x_0+2k\pi\ \vee\ x=-x_0+2k\pi$$
$x_0$
the solution from $[0,\pi]$ (cosine is even, hence $\pm x_0$)
$\tan x=m$, $\;m\in\R$
$$x=x_0+k\pi$$
$x_0$
the solution from $\left(-\frac\pi2,\frac\pi2\right)$; it always exists

Everywhere $k\in\Z$. Special cases (one family): $\sin x=0\iff x=k\pi$, $\;\sin x=\pm1\iff x=\pm\frac\pi2+2k\pi$, $\;\cos x=0\iff x=\frac\pi2+k\pi$.

Equality of the same functions
$$\sin a=\sin b\iff$$ $$a=b+2k\pi\ \vee\ a=\pi-b+2k\pi$$ $$\cos a=\cos b\iff a=\pm b+2k\pi$$ $$\tan a=\tan b\iff a=b+k\pi$$
note
for $\tan$ both arguments must belong to the domain

Move $m$ (it snaps to the values $0,\pm\frac12,\pm\frac{\sqrt2}2,\pm\frac{\sqrt3}2,\pm1$; for the tangent to $\pm\frac{\sqrt3}{3},\pm1,\pm\sqrt3$). The graph shows all the solutions; the red ones lie in the interval $[0,2\pi]$ (shaded). On the circle the same solutions are just two points.

3. Types of equations and the method

  1. Elementary with a composite argument: $\sin\left(2x-\frac\pi3\right)=\frac12$ – substitute $u=2x-\frac\pi3$, solve for $u$, go back to $x$ (the period is also divided by $2$: $+k\pi$).
  2. Reducible to a quadratic: use the Pythagorean identity to change everything into one function, $t=\sin x$, $t\in[-1,1]$, reject roots outside the interval.
  3. Double angle: $\sin2x=2\sin x\cos x$; for $\cos2x$ choose the form with the same function as the rest of the equation.
  4. Factoring: move everything to one side and factor out the common factor. Never divide by $\sin x$ or $\cos x$ – you lose solutions (demo below).
  5. $\sin f=\sin g$, $\cos f=\cos g$: the formulas from the box above. When $\sin f=\cos g$, convert: $\cos g=\sin\left(\frac\pi2-g\right)$.
Trigonometric inequalities were removed from the 2024 core curriculum – on the exam there are only equations.

The equation $\sin2x=\sin x$ in $[0,2\pi]$. Tick “dividing by $\sin x$” and see which solutions disappear (those where $\sin x=0$).

4. Step-by-step example

Solve the equation $2\sin^2x-\sin x-1=0$.

  1. $t=\sin x$, $t\in[-1,1]$: $\;2t^2-t-1=0$, $\Delta=9$, $t=1$ or $t=-\frac12$. Both are in the interval.
  2. $\sin x=1\iff x=\frac\pi2+2k\pi$.
  3. $\sin x=-\frac12$: $x_0=-\frac\pi6$, so $x=-\frac\pi6+2k\pi$ or $x=\pi+\frac\pi6+2k\pi=\frac{7\pi}6+2k\pi$.

$x=\frac\pi2+2k\pi\ \vee\ x=-\frac\pi6+2k\pi\ \vee\ x=\frac{7\pi}6+2k\pi,\ k\in\Z$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Solve the equation $\cos x=-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$.
    Solution
    $x_0=\frac{3\pi}4$ (second quadrant). $x=\pm\frac{3\pi}{4}+2k\pi$, $k\in\Z$
  2. easy
    Solve the equation $\tan x=\sqrt3$ in the interval $[0,2\pi]$.
    Solution
    $x=\frac\pi3+k\pi$; in the interval $k=0,1$. $x\in\left\{\frac\pi3,\frac{4\pi}3\right\}$
  3. medium
    Solve the equation $2\cos^2x+3\sin x-3=0$.
    Solution
    $2(1-\sin^2x)+3\sin x-3=0\iff2t^2-3t+1=0$, $t=\sin x$. $t=1$ or $t=\frac12$. $x=\frac\pi2+2k\pi\ \vee\ x=\frac\pi6+2k\pi\ \vee\ x=\frac{5\pi}6+2k\pi$
  4. medium
    Solve the equation $\sin2x=\cos x$ in the interval $[0,2\pi]$.
    Solution
    $2\sin x\cos x-\cos x=0\iff\cos x(2\sin x-1)=0$. $\cos x=0$: $\frac\pi2,\frac{3\pi}2$; $\sin x=\frac12$: $\frac\pi6,\frac{5\pi}6$. $x\in\left\{\frac\pi6,\frac\pi2,\frac{5\pi}6,\frac{3\pi}2\right\}$
  5. medium
    Solve the equation $\sin3x=\sin x$.
    Solution
    $3x=x+2k\pi$ or $3x=\pi-x+2k\pi$, that is $x=k\pi$ or $4x=\pi+2k\pi$. $x=k\pi\ \vee\ x=\frac\pi4+\frac{k\pi}2$, $k\in\Z$
  6. exam-level
    Find all solutions of the equation $\cos2x+3\cos x+2=0$ that belong to the interval $[0,2\pi]$.
    Solution
    $\cos2x=2\cos^2x-1$: $\;2t^2+3t+1=0$, $t=\cos x\in[-1,1]$. $t=-1$ or $t=-\frac12$. $\cos x=-1$: $x=\pi$. $\cos x=-\frac12$: $x=\frac{2\pi}3$ or $x=\frac{4\pi}3$. $x\in\left\{\frac{2\pi}3,\pi,\frac{4\pi}3\right\}$

6. On the exam

  • Leaving out “$k\in\Z$” in a general solution costs a point. For $\tan$ the period is $\pi$.
  • After the substitution $t=\sin x$ write $t\in[-1,1]$ and explicitly reject e.g. $t=2$.
  • Dividing by an expression containing the unknown loses solutions – factor out instead.
  • For solutions in an interval, list consecutive values of $k$ and check the endpoints (whether $0$ and $2\pi$ belong).
  • When the equation contains $\tan x$, write the domain $x\neq\frac\pi2+k\pi$ and check the result against it.
