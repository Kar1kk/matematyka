Biquadratic equations and equations reducible to quadratics

$ax^4+bx^2+c=0$ is a quadratic equation in disguise: substitute $t=x^2$. Each $t>0$ gives two solutions $x=\pm\sqrt t$, $t=0$ gives one, and $t<0$ gives none.

1. The substitution method

The biquadratic equation
$$ax^4+bx^2+c=0,\quad a\neq0$$ $$t=x^2,\ t\ge0:\quad at^2+bt+c=0$$
$t\ge0$
the substitution condition: a square is not negative

Other substitutions (the same idea: a repeated “block”):

  • $x^6-7x^3-8=0$: $t=x^3$ (here without the condition $t\ge0$, because each $t$ gives one $x=\sqrt[3]{t}$).
  • $(x^2+x)^2-8(x^2+x)+12=0$: $t=x^2+x$.
  • $(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)(x-4)=24$: group as $(x-1)(x-4)\cdot(x-2)(x-3)$ $=(x^2-5x+4)(x^2-5x+6)$, then $t=x^2-5x$.
Finishing the last example

$(t+4)(t+6)=24 \iff t^2+10t=0 \iff t=0$ or $t=-10$. $x^2-5x=0$ gives $x\in\{0,5\}$; $x^2-5x+10=0$ has $\Delta=-15<0$. Answer: $x\in\{0,5\}$.

2. Interactive: the $t$ axis and the $x$ axis

Left: the parabola $y=t^2+bt+c$; the region $t<0$ is forbidden (shaded). Right: $y=x^4+bx^2+c$. Each green root $t>0$ gives two roots $\pm\sqrt t$ on the right. Set $c=0$ and see the root $t=0$.

3. Animation: $t=x^2$ “bends” the axis

We take the graph in the variable $t$ (parameters $b$, $c$ from the demo above). Every point with $t\ge0$ splits in two and travels to $x=\pm\sqrt t$. The part with $t<0$ disappears: no $x$ reaches it. This is how the graph $y=x^4+bx^2+c$ arises.

4. Step-by-step example

Solve the equation $x^4-5x^2+4=0$.

  1. $t=x^2\ge0$: $t^2-5t+4=0$, $\Delta=9$, $t=1$ or $t=4$. Both positive.
  2. $x^2=1$ gives $x=\pm1$; $\;x^2=4$ gives $x=\pm2$.

$x\in\{-2,-1,1,2\}$

This is exactly the demo's starting setting ($b=-5$, $c=4$).

Solve the equation $x^4+3x^2-4=0$.

  1. $t^2+3t-4=0 \iff (t+4)(t-1)=0$, so $t=1$ or $t=-4$.
  2. $t=-4<0$ is rejected. $x^2=1$.

$x\in\{-1,1\}$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Solve the equation $x^4-13x^2+36=0$.
    Solution
    $t^2-13t+36=0$, $t=4$ or $t=9$. $x\in\{-3,-2,2,3\}$
  2. medium
    Solve the equation $2x^4+x^2-3=0$.
    Solution
    $2t^2+t-3=0$, $\Delta=25$, $t=1$ or $t=-\frac32$ (rejected). $x\in\{-1,1\}$
  3. medium
    Solve the equation $x^6-7x^3-8=0$.
    Solution
    $t=x^3$: $t^2-7t-8=(t-8)(t+1)=0$. $x^3=8$ or $x^3=-1$. $x\in\{-1,2\}$
  4. medium
    Solve the inequality $x^4-10x^2+9\le0$.
    Solution
    $(x^2-1)(x^2-9)\le0 \iff 1\le x^2\le9 \iff 1\le|x|\le3$. $x\in[-3,-1]\cup[1,3]$
  5. exam-level
    Solve the equation $(x^2+x)^2-8(x^2+x)+12=0$.
    Solution
    $t=x^2+x$: $t^2-8t+12=0$, $t=2$ or $t=6$. $x^2+x-2=0$: $x=1$, $x=-2$. $\;x^2+x-6=0$: $x=2$, $x=-3$. $x\in\{-3,-2,1,2\}$
  6. exam-level
    For which $m$ does the equation $x^4-2(m+1)x^2+m+7=0$ have four distinct solutions?
    Solution
    We need two distinct positive $t$ for $t^2-2(m+1)t+m+7=0$: $\frac\Delta4=(m+3)(m-2)>0$, $t_1t_2=m+7>0$, $t_1+t_2=2(m+1)>0$. $m\in(2,\infty)$

6. On the exam

  • Write the condition $t\ge0$ with $t=x^2$ and explicitly reject negative $t$ (with the comment “contradiction”).
  • Do not stop at $t$: the answer is in $x$. A common mistake is giving the value of $t$.
  • Translate a question about the number of solutions with a parameter into the signs of the roots in $t$ (the table above, Viète).
  • With $t=x^2+x$ and the like the condition on $t$ is not needed, but each $t$ gives a separate quadratic equation with its own $\Delta$.
