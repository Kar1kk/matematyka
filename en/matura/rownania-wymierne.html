Rational equations and inequalities

A fraction is zero when the numerator is zero and the denominator is not. A fraction is positive when the numerator and denominator have the same sign, that is, exactly when their product is positive. You always start with the domain.

III.R.2, III.R.7, II.4, II.R.6

1. Domain and operations on fractions

Domain of a rational expression $\frac{V(x)}{W(x)}$: all $x$ for which $W(x)\neq0$. Write it down before any transformation, because after cancelling the fraction the information is lost.
  • Multiplication, division: factor and cancel: $\frac{x^2-4}{x}\cdot\frac{x^2}{x+2}=x(x-2)$ for $x\notin\{-2,0\}$.
  • Addition, subtraction: common denominator: $\frac{1}{x+1}-\frac1x=\frac{x-(x+1)}{x(x+1)}=\frac{-1}{x(x+1)}$.

2. Equations

A fraction equal to zero
$$\frac{V(x)}{W(x)}=0\iff V(x)=0\ \wedge\ W(x)\neq0$$
trap
a root of the numerator that also makes the denominator zero is not a solution

The equation $\frac{A}{B}=\frac{C}{D}$: after writing the domain you cross-multiply, $AD=BC$, and solve the linear or quadratic equation. At the end reject results outside the domain.

3. Inequalities: do not multiply by the denominator!

Replacing with a product
$$\frac{V}{W}>0\iff V\cdot W>0$$ $$\frac{V}{W}\ge0\iff V\cdot W\ge0\ \wedge\ W\neq0$$
why
$\frac VW$ and $V\cdot W=\frac VW\cdot W^2$ have the same sign, because $W^2>0$
  1. Domain.
  2. Everything on one side, common denominator: $\frac{V}{W}\;?\;0$.
  3. Replace with $V\cdot W\;?\;0$ and solve (parabola or the wavy-curve sign chart).
  4. Remove the zeros of the denominator from the result.
Multiplying both sides by $W(x)$ is a mistake: you do not know the sign of $W$, so you do not know whether to reverse the inequality. See demo 5.

4. Interactive: the graph and the domain

$f(x)=\frac{(x-p)(x-q)}{x-r}$. The dashed vertical line: $x=r$ is outside the domain (asymptote). Set $p=r$: the fraction cancels, but at $x=r$ a “hole” remains (open dot). Turn on $V\cdot W$: it has the same sign as $f$ everywhere in the domain.

5. Interactive: the mistake of multiplying by the denominator

The inequality $\frac{x}{x-1}\ge k$. Green bar: the correct solution (where the graph is above the line $y=k$). Red: the result of “solving” by multiplying both sides by $x-1$. To the left of $x=1$ the denominator is negative and the wrong method gives something completely different there.

6. Step-by-step example

Solve the equation $\frac{(x-2)(x+3)}{(x+3)(x-1)}=0$.

  1. Domain: $x\neq-3$, $x\neq1$.
  2. The numerator is zero for $x=2$ and $x=-3$.
  3. $x=-3$ does not belong to the domain.

$x=2$

Solve the inequality $\frac{x+1}{x-2}\ge2$.

  1. Domain: $x\neq2$.
  2. $\frac{x+1}{x-2}-2\ge0 \iff \frac{x+1-2x+4}{x-2}\ge0 \iff \frac{5-x}{x-2}\ge0$.
  3. $(5-x)(x-2)\ge0$ and $x\neq2$. A parabola with arms down, roots $2$ and $5$: $x\in[2,5]$, without $2$.

$x\in(2,5]$

7. Tasks

  1. easy
    Solve the equation $\frac{(x-3)(x+1)}{x(x-3)}=0$.
    Solution
    Domain: $x\notin\{0,3\}$. Numerator: $x=3$ (outside the domain) or $x=-1$. $x=-1$
  2. easy
    Solve the equation $\frac{3}{x-1}=\frac{2}{x+2}$.
    Solution
    Domain: $x\notin\{-2,1\}$. Cross-multiplying: $3(x+2)=2(x-1)$, $3x+6=2x-2$. $x=-8$
  3. medium
    Solve the inequality $\frac{2x-1}{x+3}<1$.
    Solution
    $x\neq-3$. $\frac{2x-1-(x+3)}{x+3}<0 \iff \frac{x-4}{x+3}<0 \iff (x-4)(x+3)<0$. $x\in(-3,4)$
  4. medium
    Solve the equation $\frac{x}{x-1}-\frac{3}{x+1}=\frac{6}{x^2-1}$.
    Solution
    Domain: $x\neq\pm1$. Multiply by $(x-1)(x+1)$: $x(x+1)-3(x-1)=6 \iff x^2-2x-3=0$, $x=3$ or $x=-1$. But $-1\notin D$. $x=3$
  5. exam-level
    Solve the inequality $\frac6x\ge x+1$.
    Solution
    $x\neq0$. $\frac{6-x^2-x}{x}\ge0 \iff \frac{-(x+3)(x-2)}{x}\ge0 \iff \frac{(x+3)(x-2)}{x}\le0$. The product $x(x+3)(x-2)\le0$, the wavy curve starting from the upper right, roots $-3,0,2$; remove $0$. $x\in(-\infty,-3]\cup(0,2]$
  6. exam-level
    Solve the equation $\frac1x+\frac1{x+2}=\frac43$.
    Solution
    $x\notin\{-2,0\}$. Times $3x(x+2)$: $3(x+2)+3x=4x(x+2)$, that is $4x^2+2x-6=0$, $2x^2+x-3=0$, $\Delta=25$. $x=1$ or $x=-\frac32$ (both in the domain).

8. On the exam

  • Domain at the beginning, a check against the domain at the end. A solution outside the domain in the answer costs a point.
  • Inequalities: move everything to one side and replace with a product. Multiplying by a denominator of unknown sign is a substantive error.
  • For $\ge$, $\le$: the zeros of the numerator belong to the set, the zeros of the denominator never do.
  • Common denominator: factor the denominators, e.g. $x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)$, instead of multiplying them all.
