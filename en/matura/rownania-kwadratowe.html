Quadratic equations and inequalities

A quadratic inequality asks: where is the parabola above the $Ox$ axis, and where is it below? Two pieces of information are enough: the zeros and the direction of the arms.

2. Inequality: sketching the parabola

  1. Move everything to one side: $ax^2+bx+c \;?\; 0$.
  2. Find the zeros (or state that there are none).
  3. Sketch the parabola: arms up for $a>0$, down for $a<0$.
  4. Read where the graph is above the axis ($>0$) or below it ($<0$). For $\ge$, $\le$ include the zeros.

4. Animation: when $\Delta$ passes through zero

The parabola $y=a(x-1)^2+q$ moves upward. Below it, the solution sets of all four inequalities at once. Stop at $q=0$ ($\Delta=0$): there $\ge$ and $>$ differ by just one point.

5. Step-by-step example

Solve the inequality $-2x^2+5x+3\ge0$.

  1. $\Delta=25+24=49$, $\sqrt\Delta=7$.
  2. $x_1=\frac{-5+7}{-4}=-\frac12$, $\;x_2=\frac{-5-7}{-4}=3$.
  3. $a=-2<0$: the arms point down, so the parabola is above the axis between the roots. The sign $\ge$: the endpoints belong.

$x\in[-\frac12,3]$

Solve $x^2+4>4x$.

  1. $x^2-4x+4>0 \iff (x-2)^2>0$.
  2. A square is positive everywhere except $x=2$, where it is zero.

$x\in\R\setminus\{2\}$

6. Tasks

  1. easy
    Solve the inequality $x^2\ge5x$.
    Solution
    $x^2-5x\ge0 \iff x(x-5)\ge0$. The roots are $0$ and $5$, the arms point up, so we take the outside with the endpoints. $x\in(-\infty,0]\cup[5,\infty)$
  2. easy
    Solve the equation $3x^2-x-2=0$.
    Solution
    $\Delta=1+24=25$, $x=\frac{1\mp5}{6}$. $x=-\frac23$ or $x=1$
  3. medium
    Solve the inequality $(2x-1)^2<(x+2)^2$.
    Solution
    $4x^2-4x+1<x^2+4x+4 \iff 3x^2-8x-3<0$. $\Delta=100$, $x_1=-\frac13$, $x_2=3$, the arms point up. $x\in\left(-\frac13,3\right)$
    Faster: a difference of squares $(2x-1-x-2)(2x-1+x+2)=(x-3)(3x+1)<0$.
  4. medium
    How many integers satisfy the inequality $-x^2+3x+10>0$?
    Solution
    The roots: $\Delta=49$, $x=-2$ and $x=5$; the arms point down, so $x\in(-2,5)$. The integers: $-1,0,1,2,3,4$. There are $6$ of them.
  5. exam-level
    Find the domain of the function $f(x)=\sqrt{6-x-x^2}+\frac1x$.
    Solution
    Conditions: $6-x-x^2\ge0$ and $x\neq0$. The first: $x^2+x-6\le0$, roots $-3$ and $2$, so $x\in[-3,2]$. Remove zero. $D=[-3,0)\cup(0,2]$
  6. exam-level
    The perimeter of a rectangle is $20$. What length can the side $x$ have if the area of the rectangle is at least $21$?
    Solution
    The sides are $x$ and $10-x$, $x\in(0,10)$. $x(10-x)\ge21 \iff x^2-10x+21\le0$, roots $3$ and $7$, so $x\in[3,7]$. It lies within the domain. $x\in[3,7]$

7. On the exam

  • The parabola sketch is part of the solution: usually it is enough to mark the roots and the direction of the arms. Without it it is easy to confuse “between” with “outside”.
  • Interval endpoints: for $\ge$, $\le$ square brackets $[\;]$, for $>$, $<$ round ones. Next to $\infty$ always round.
  • $a<0$: the most common mistake is reading it as for $a>0$. You may multiply by $-1$ and reverse the sign.
  • $\Delta<0$: the answer is $\R$ or $\varnothing$, not just “no solutions” without checking the sign.
  • Word problems: intersect the result with the domain (positive lengths, natural numbers).
