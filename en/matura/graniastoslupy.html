Prisms

A prism is a base “pulled” upward. Almost every problem reduces to one right triangle: the height, a segment in the base (the projection) and the hypotenuse (the diagonal of the solid or of a face).

X.3, X.5

1. Concepts and formulas

A right prism has lateral edges perpendicular to the bases (the lateral faces are rectangles). A regular prism is a right prism whose base is a regular polygon. A cuboid with a square base is a regular quadrilateral prism; a cube: all edges equal.

3. Step-by-step example

The diagonal of a regular quadrilateral prism has length $10$ and makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the plane of the base. Find the volume.

  1. The triangle: diagonal $d=10$, projection $d_p$, height $h$. $h=10\sin60^\circ=5\sqrt3$, $d_p=10\cos60^\circ=5$.
  2. $d_p=a\sqrt2=5$, so $a=\frac{5\sqrt2}{2}$ and $P_p=a^2=\frac{25}{2}$.
  3. $V=P_p\cdot h=\frac{25}{2}\cdot5\sqrt3$.

$V=\frac{125\sqrt3}{2}$

4. Tasks

  1. easy
    The diagonal of a cube has length $6$. Find the volume and the total surface area of the cube.
    Solution
    $a\sqrt3=6\Rightarrow a=2\sqrt3$. $V=(2\sqrt3)^3$, $P_c=6\cdot12$. $V=24\sqrt3$, $P_c=72$
  2. easy
    A regular triangular prism has base edge $6$ and height $8$. Find $V$ and $P_c$.
    Solution
    $P_p=\frac{36\sqrt3}{4}=9\sqrt3$, $V=9\sqrt3\cdot8$, $P_b=3\cdot6\cdot8=144$. $V=72\sqrt3$, $P_c=144+18\sqrt3$
  3. medium
    A regular hexagonal prism has base edge $2$, and its longest diagonal makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the base. Find $V$ and $P_c$.
    Solution
    The projection of the longest diagonal is the longest diagonal of the base: $2a=4$. $h=4\tan60^\circ=4\sqrt3$. $P_p=\frac{3\cdot4\sqrt3}{2}=6\sqrt3$, $V=6\sqrt3\cdot4\sqrt3=72$, $P_c=12\sqrt3+6\cdot2\cdot4\sqrt3$. $V=72$, $P_c=60\sqrt3$
  4. exam-level
    In a regular quadrilateral prism $ABCDA'B'C'D'$ the base edge has length $2$ and the height is $4$. Find the cosine of the angle between the diagonals of the lateral faces $AB'$ and $AD'$.
    Solution
    $|AB'|=|AD'|=\sqrt{4+16}=\sqrt{20}$, $|B'D'|=2\sqrt2$. By the law of cosines in triangle $AB'D'$: $8=20+20-2\cdot20\cos\alpha$, $\cos\alpha=\frac{32}{40}$. $\cos\alpha=\frac45$
  5. exam-level
    The diagonal of a regular quadrilateral prism has length $d$ and makes an angle $\alpha$ with the base. Find the volume in terms of $d$ and $\alpha$.
    Solution
    $h=d\sin\alpha$, $d_p=d\cos\alpha=a\sqrt2$, so $P_p=a^2=\frac{d^2\cos^2\alpha}{2}$. $V=\frac{d^3\sin\alpha\cos^2\alpha}{2}$ (for $d=10$, $\alpha=60^\circ$ this gives the result from the example).

5. On the exam

  • Mark on the figure the angle and the right triangle in which you compute it; name the projection (“$AC$ is the projection of $AG$ onto the plane of the base”).
  • “Regular” gives two things at once: the solid is right and the base is regular. Do not assume this when the text says only “right”.
  • In a hexagon distinguish the diagonals: $2a$ (through the center) and $a\sqrt3$ (the shorter one).
  • Total surface area: do not forget two bases.
