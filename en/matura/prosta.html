Distance between points, equation of a line

The distance between two points is the hypotenuse of a triangle with legs $|\Delta x|$ and $|\Delta y|$: plain Pythagoras. A line is "one step right → $a$ steps up", anchored at the point $(0,b)$.

IX.2, IX.3

1. Distance and the midpoint of a segment

Distance between the points $A=(x_A,y_A)$, $B=(x_B,y_B)$
$$|AB|=\sqrt{(x_B-x_A)^2+(y_B-y_A)^2}$$
why
Pythagoras in the triangle with legs $|x_B-x_A|$ and $|y_B-y_A|$. It is on the formula sheet.
Midpoint of the segment $AB$
$$S=\left(\frac{x_A+x_B}{2},\ \frac{y_A+y_B}{2}\right)$$
$S$
the average of the coordinates on CKE sheet. Conversely: $B=(2x_S-x_A,\ 2y_S-y_A)$.

2. Equation of a line

Slope-intercept form
$$y=ax+b$$
$a$
the slope: $a=\tan\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the angle of inclination to the axis $Ox$; from two points $a=\frac{y_B-y_A}{x_B-x_A}$
$b$
the line meets the axis $Oy$ at $(0,b)$
General form
$$Ax+By+C=0,\quad A^2+B^2\neq0$$
$B\neq0$
you can convert to slope-intercept form: $y=-\frac{A}{B}x-\frac{C}{B}$
$B=0$
a vertical line $x=-\frac{C}{A}$: it has no slope-intercept form
Line through $P=(x_0,y_0)$ with a given $a$ / through $A$ and $B$
$$y-y_0=a(x-x_0)$$ $$\begin{aligned}&(y-y_A)(x_B-x_A)=\\&=(y_B-y_A)(x-x_A)\end{aligned}$$
note
the second formula on CKE sheet also works for a vertical line
The quickest check: substitute both given points into the equation you got. You must get an identity.

5. Worked example

Given $A=(-2,1)$ and $B=(4,9)$. Find $|AB|$, the midpoint $S$ and the equation of the line $AB$.

  1. $\Delta x=6$, $\Delta y=8$, so $|AB|=\sqrt{36+64}=10$.
  2. $S=\left(\frac{-2+4}{2},\frac{1+9}{2}\right)=(1,5)$.
  3. $a=\frac{8}{6}=\frac43$. Substitute $A$: $1=\frac43\cdot(-2)+b$, so $b=\frac{11}{3}$.
  4. Multiply by $3$: $3y=4x+11$.
  5. Check $B$: $4\cdot4-3\cdot9+11=0$ ✓.

$|AB|=10$, $S=(1,5)$, $y=\frac43x+\frac{11}{3}$, i.e. $4x-3y+11=0$

6. Exercises

  1. easy
    Find the length of the segment with endpoints $A=(1,-2)$, $B=(6,10)$ and find its midpoint.
    Solution
    $|AB|=\sqrt{5^2+12^2}=\sqrt{169}=13$, $S=\left(\frac72,4\right)$. $|AB|=13$, $S=(3.5;\ 4)$
  2. easy
    The point $S=(2,-1)$ is the midpoint of the segment $AB$, $A=(-3,4)$. Find $B$.
    Solution
    $x_B=2\cdot2-(-3)=7$, $y_B=2\cdot(-1)-4=-6$. $B=(7,-6)$
  3. medium
    Find the equation of the line through $A=(-1,5)$ and $B=(3,-3)$ in slope-intercept and general form.
    Solution
    $a=\frac{-3-5}{3-(-1)}=-2$. From $A$: $5=2+b$, $b=3$. Check $B$: $-6+3=-3$ ✓. $y=-2x+3$, i.e. $2x+y-3=0$
  4. medium
    The line $3x-2y+6=0$ cuts a triangle off the coordinate axes. Write it in slope-intercept form and find the area of this triangle.
    Solution
    $2y=3x+6$, so $y=\frac32x+3$. Intersection with $Oy$: $(0,3)$; with $Ox$: $\frac32x+3=0$, $x=-2$. A right triangle with legs $2$ and $3$: $P=\frac12\cdot2\cdot3$. $y=\frac32x+3$, area $3$
  5. exam-level
    On the axis $Ox$ find a point $P$ equidistant from $A=(1,4)$ and $B=(7,2)$.
    Solution
    $P=(x,0)$. $|PA|^2=|PB|^2$: $(x-1)^2+16=(x-7)^2+4$, i.e. $-2x+17=-14x+53$, $x=3$. Check: $|PA|^2=4+16=20=16+4=|PB|^2$ ✓. $P=(3,0)$
  6. exam-level
    Show that the triangle with vertices $A=(-2,1)$, $B=(2,-1)$, $C=(4,3)$ is right and isosceles. Find its area and the center of its circumscribed circle.
    Solution
    $|AB|^2=16+4=20$, $|BC|^2=4+16=20$, $|AC|^2=36+4=40$. Since $|AB|^2+|BC|^2=|AC|^2$, by the converse of the Pythagorean theorem the angle at $B$ is right, and $|AB|=|BC|$. Area $\frac12\cdot20=10$. The center of the circle circumscribed on a right triangle is the midpoint of the hypotenuse $AC$. Area $10$, center of the circumscribed circle $(1,2)$

7. On the exam

  • A vertical line $x=c$ has no slope-intercept form. When the points have equal $x$, do not divide by zero, just write $x=c$ at once.
  • In the answer give the equation in the form the problem asks for. The general form preferably with integer coefficients.
  • Simplify the root: $\sqrt{40}=2\sqrt{10}$. Do not round unless told to.
  • "Show that the triangle is right/isosceles" problems you do with squares of lengths: no roots and no mistakes.
