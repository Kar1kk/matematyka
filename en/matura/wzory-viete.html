Vieta's formulas

If you know the roots, you know the coefficients: expand $a(x-x_1)(x-x_2)$ and compare with $ax^2+bx+c$. This way you get the sum and product of the roots without computing the roots themselves.

III.R.3

1. The theorem

Theorem (Vieta's formulas) If $a\neq0$ and $\Delta\ge0$, and $x_1$, $x_2$ are the roots of $ax^2+bx+c=0$, then $$x_1+x_2=\frac{-b}{a},\qquad x_1\cdot x_2=\frac{c}{a}$$ (The formulas are on the formula sheet. For $\Delta=0$ we take $x_1=x_2=x_0$.)
Proof (required on the exam)

From the formulas for the roots, with $\Delta\ge0$:

$$x_1+x_2=\frac{-b-\sqrt\Delta-b+\sqrt\Delta}{2a}$$ $$=\frac{-2b}{2a}=\frac{-b}{a}$$ $$x_1x_2=\frac{(-b-\sqrt\Delta)(-b+\sqrt\Delta)}{4a^2}$$ $$=\frac{b^2-\Delta}{4a^2}=\frac{b^2-b^2+4ac}{4a^2}=\frac{c}{a}$$

In the numerator of the product we used $(u-v)(u+v)=u^2-v^2$. For $\Delta=0$ both equalities hold too ($\sqrt\Delta=0$). $\blacksquare$

Second way: $ax^2+bx+c=a(x-x_1)(x-x_2)=ax^2-a(x_1+x_2)x+ax_1x_2$. Comparing the coefficients of $x$ and the constant terms gives $b=-a(x_1+x_2)$, $c=ax_1x_2$.

First $\Delta\ge0$ (and for "two distinct roots" $\Delta>0$)! When $\Delta<0$, the formulas still give numbers, but there are no roots. This is a favorite trap in problems with a parameter.

2. Symmetric expressions

Every expression that does not change when you swap $x_1\leftrightarrow x_2$ can be written in terms of $s=x_1+x_2$ and $p=x_1x_2$. These formulas are not on the formula sheet, derive them from the algebraic identities:

The most common
$$x_1^2+x_2^2=s^2-2p$$ $$\frac1{x_1}+\frac1{x_2}=\frac{s}{p}$$ $$(x_1-x_2)^2=s^2-4p=\frac{\Delta}{a^2}$$ $$|x_1-x_2|=\frac{\sqrt\Delta}{|a|}$$ $$x_1^3+x_2^3=s^3-3ps$$
$s, p$
$s=-\frac{b}{a}$, $p=\frac{c}{a}$
$\frac{s}{p}$
requires $p\neq0$ (no root is zero)

The signs of the roots can also be read from $s$ and $p$: $p<0$ means roots of different signs; $p>0$ and $s>0$ means both positive; $p>0$ and $s<0$ means both negative (all for $\Delta>0$). More in the topic on the parameter.

4. Animation: where $x_1^2+x_2^2=s^2-2p$ comes from

The square with side $x_1+x_2$ consists of the squares $x_1^2$, $x_2^2$ and two rectangles $x_1x_2$. Take away the rectangles and $x_1^2+x_2^2$ remains.

5. Worked example

For the equation $x^2-5x+3=0$ find $x_1^2+x_2^2$, $\frac1{x_1}+\frac1{x_2}$ and $|x_1-x_2|$ without finding the roots.

  1. $\Delta=25-12=13>0$: the roots exist.
  2. $s=5$, $p=3$.
  3. $x_1^2+x_2^2=25-6=19$; $\;\frac1{x_1}+\frac1{x_2}=\frac53$.
  4. $|x_1-x_2|=\frac{\sqrt{13}}{1}=\sqrt{13}$.

$19,\ \frac53,\ \sqrt{13}$

6. Exercises

  1. easy
    Find the sum and product of the roots of the equation $2x^2+6x-5=0$.
    Solution
    $\Delta=36+40=76>0$. $x_1+x_2=-3$, $x_1x_2=-\frac52$
  2. medium
    Write a quadratic equation ($a=1$) whose roots are $2-\sqrt3$ and $2+\sqrt3$.
    Solution
    $s=4$, $p=(2-\sqrt3)(2+\sqrt3)=4-3=1$. The equation $x^2-sx+p=0$. $x^2-4x+1=0$
  3. medium
    $x_1$, $x_2$ are the roots of the equation $x^2-3x-1=0$. Find $x_1^3+x_2^3$.
    Solution
    $\Delta=13>0$, $s=3$, $p=-1$. $x_1^3+x_2^3=s^3-3ps=27+9$. $36$
  4. exam-level
    Find $m$ for which the roots of the equation $x^2-2mx+m^2-1=0$ satisfy $x_1^2+x_2^2=10$.
    Solution
    $\Delta=4m^2-4m^2+4=4>0$ for every $m$. $s=2m$, $p=m^2-1$, so $x_1^2+x_2^2=4m^2-2m^2+2=2m^2+2=10$, $m^2=4$. $m=-2$ or $m=2$
  5. exam-level
    Find $m$ for which the equation $x^2+2mx+m+2=0$ has roots satisfying $x_1^2+x_2^2=2$.
    Solution
    Condition for existence: $\frac{\Delta}4=m^2-m-2=(m-2)(m+1)\ge0$, i.e. $m\in(-\infty,-1]\cup[2,\infty)$.
    $s^2-2p=4m^2-2m-4=2 \iff 2m^2-m-3=0 \iff m=-1$ or $m=\frac32$.
    $m=\frac32$ is rejected ($\Delta<0$, then "$x_1^2+x_2^2$" does not exist). For $m=-1$: $(x-1)^2=0$, $1+1=2$. $m=-1$

7. On the exam

  • The condition on $\Delta$ always at the start, and at the end the intersection of the results. Without it you usually lose 1–2 points.
  • The condition $a\neq0$, if the coefficient of $x^2$ depends on the parameter.
  • Transform the expression so that it contains only $x_1+x_2$ and $x_1x_2$, and write down this transformation (e.g. $x_1^2+x_2^2=(x_1+x_2)^2-2x_1x_2$).
  • With $\frac1{x_1}+\frac1{x_2}$ add $x_1x_2\neq0$, i.e. $c\neq0$.
