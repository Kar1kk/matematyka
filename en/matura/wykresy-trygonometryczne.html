Graphs and periodicity of trigonometric functions

The sine wave is the height of a point travelling around the unit circle, unrolled in time. After a full turn ($2\pi$) the point returns to its place, so the graph repeats: this is periodicity.

VII.R.2, VII.R.3, V.12, V.R.1

1. Animation: from the circle to the graph

A point travels around the circle; its height (the sine) is carried horizontally onto the graph. Switch to tangent: we read the value on the tangent line $x=1$ to the circle and also project it horizontally. Cosine is the $x$-coordinate of the point: the same green segment, just laid horizontally.

Everywhere $k\in\Z$. The tangent has vertical asymptotes $x=\frac\pi2+k\pi$ (where $\cos x=0$) and is increasing on every interval $\left(-\frac\pi2+k\pi,\frac\pi2+k\pi\right)$.

Definition A function $f$ is periodic if there is $T>0$ such that for every $x$ in the domain $x\pm T$ also belongs to the domain and $f(x+T)=f(x)$. The smallest such $T$ (if it exists) is the fundamental period.
Periodicity and parity
$$\sin(x+2k\pi)=\sin x,\quad \cos(x+2k\pi)=\cos x$$ $$\tan(x+k\pi)=\tan x$$ $$\sin(-x)=-\sin x,\quad \cos(-x)=\cos x$$
why
$x+2k\pi$: the same point of the circle; $-x$: the point reflected in the axis $Ox$; $x+\pi$: the point symmetric about $O$, so both coordinates change sign, while their quotient does not
$\cos x=\sin\left(x+\frac\pi2\right)$: the graph of cosine is the sine wave shifted $\frac\pi2$ to the left. For $\sin(kx)$ the period is $\frac{2\pi}{|k|}$, because $\sin\left(k\left(x+\frac{2\pi}{k}\right)\right)=\sin(kx+2\pi)$.

3. Transformations of graphs

From the core curriculum: the shift $y=f(x-p)+q$ (by the vector $[p,q]$), reflection in $Ox$: $y=-f(x)$, reflection in $Oy$: $y=f(-x)$. The slider $k$ only illustrates how the period of $\sin(kx)$ changes.

Dashed: the original graph. The red arrow is the shift vector, the purple segment has the length of one period. Notice: for sine $f(-x)$ gives the same as $-f(x)$ (oddness), while for cosine $f(-x)$ changes nothing.

4. Worked example

Sketch $f(x)=\sin\left(x-\frac\pi3\right)+1$, and give the range, the period and the zeros.

  1. The graph of $y=\sin x$ shifted by the vector $\left[\frac\pi3,1\right]$ (set $p=\frac\pi3$, $q=1$ in the demo).
  2. The shift does not change the period: $T=2\pi$. Range: $[-1+1,\,1+1]=[0,2]$.
  3. $f(x)=0\iff\sin\left(x-\frac\pi3\right)=-1\iff x-\frac\pi3=\frac{3\pi}2+2k\pi$.

$\text{range}=[0,2]$, $T=2\pi$, $x=\frac{11\pi}{6}+2k\pi,\ k\in\Z$

5. Exercises

  1. easy
    Find the range of the function $f(x)=-\cos x+2$.
    Solution
    $-\cos x\in[-1,1]$, so $f(x)\in[1,3]$. $[1,3]$
  2. easy
    Find $\sin\frac{2026\pi}{3}$.
    Solution
    $\frac{2026\pi}{3}=674\pi+\frac{4\pi}{3}=337\cdot2\pi+\frac{4\pi}3$, so this is $\sin\frac{4\pi}3$ (third quadrant, reference angle $\frac\pi3$). $-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}$
  3. medium
    The graph of $y=\sin x$ is shifted by the vector $\left[\frac\pi2,-1\right]$. Give the formula of the resulting function and its zeros.
    Solution
    $y=\sin\left(x-\frac\pi2\right)-1=-\cos x-1$. Zero: $\cos x=-1$. $y=-\cos x-1$, $\;x=\pi+2k\pi$, $k\in\Z$
  4. medium
    How many solutions does the equation $\tan x=2$ have in the interval $(0,3\pi)$? Read it off the graph.
    Solution
    Each branch of the tangent takes the value $2$ exactly once. In $(0,3\pi)$ there fit: part of the branch $(0,\frac\pi2)$, the branches $(\frac\pi2,\frac{3\pi}2)$, $(\frac{3\pi}2,\frac{5\pi}2)$ and part of $(\frac{5\pi}2,3\pi)$, where $\tan x<0$. 3 solutions
  5. medium
    Find the fundamental period of the function $f(x)=\sin3x$ and justify it.
    Solution
    $\sin\left(3\left(x+\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)\right)=\sin(3x+2\pi)=\sin3x$. If the period were $T<\frac{2\pi}{3}$, then $3T<2\pi$ would be a period of the sine (substitute $u=3x$), but the smallest period of the sine is $2\pi$. $T=\frac{2\pi}{3}$
  6. exam-level
    Find the greatest and the smallest value of the function $f(x)=\cos^2x-\sin x+1$.
    Solution
    $\cos^2x=1-\sin^2x$, so for $t=\sin x\in[-1,1]$: $g(t)=-t^2-t+2$. The vertex $t=-\frac12\in[-1,1]$: $g\left(-\frac12\right)=\frac94$. Endpoints: $g(-1)=2$, $g(1)=0$. Greatest $\frac94$, smallest $0$.

6. On the exam

  • After substituting $t=\sin x$ or $t=\cos x$ always add $t\in[-1,1]$. This is the most common omission in problems about values and equations.
  • Write general solutions with $k\in\Z$. For $\tan$ the period is $\pi$, not $2\pi$.
  • $f(x-p)$ shifts to the right by $p$. In a sketch mark the characteristic points (zeros, maxima) and the asymptotes of the tangent.
  • "How many solutions in an interval" – the safest way is to sketch the graph and the line $y=m$, then count the intersections.
