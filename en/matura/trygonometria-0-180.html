Trigonometric functions of angles 0°–180°, the Pythagorean identity

The arm of the angle meets the semicircle of radius $1$ at the point $P=(x,y)$. Then simply $\cos\alpha=x$, $\sin\alpha=y$. For an obtuse angle $P$ is to the left of the $Oy$ axis, so the cosine and tangent are negative.

VII.1, VII.2

1. Definition and values

Definition The angle $\alpha\in[0^\circ,180^\circ]$ has its vertex at $O=(0,0)$ and its first arm on the positive $Ox$ half-axis. If $P=(x,y)\neq O$ lies on the second arm and $r=|OP|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, then $$\sin\alpha=\frac{y}{r},\quad \cos\alpha=\frac{x}{r}$$ $$\tan\alpha=\frac{y}{x}\quad(\alpha\neq 90^\circ)$$ For an acute angle this is the same definition as in a right triangle. When $r=1$: $\;P=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$.

The tables give values for $0^\circ$–$90^\circ$. You read the obtuse angles off by symmetry (formulas below). Signs: $\sin\alpha\ge0$ always; $\cos\alpha$ and $\tan\alpha$ are negative for $\alpha\in(90^\circ,180^\circ)$.

2. Identities

The Pythagorean identity and the tangent on CKE sheet
$$\sin^2\alpha+\cos^2\alpha=1$$ $$\tan\alpha=\frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}$$
proof
$\frac{y^2}{r^2}+\frac{x^2}{r^2}=\frac{x^2+y^2}{r^2}=1$; $\;\frac{y/r}{x/r}=\frac{y}{x}$
An angle and its supplement to $180^\circ$
$$\sin(180^\circ-\alpha)=\sin\alpha$$ $$\cos(180^\circ-\alpha)=-\cos\alpha$$ $$\tan(180^\circ-\alpha)=-\tan\alpha$$
why
the points $(x,y)$ and $(-x,y)$ are symmetric about the $Oy$ axis: the same height, the opposite $x$-coordinate
From $\sin\alpha$ alone you cannot read off the angle: $\sin\alpha=\frac12$ gives $\alpha=30^\circ$ or $150^\circ$. The cosine decides uniquely (each value in $(-1,1)$ corresponds to exactly one angle in $(0^\circ,180^\circ)$).

4. Step-by-step example

The angle $\alpha\in(90^\circ,180^\circ)$ and $\tan\alpha=-2$. Find $\sin\alpha$ and $\cos\alpha$.

  1. $\sin\alpha=-2\cos\alpha$. Substitute into the identity: $4\cos^2\alpha+\cos^2\alpha=1$, so $\cos^2\alpha=\frac15$.
  2. The angle is obtuse, so $\cos\alpha<0$: $\cos\alpha=-\frac{1}{\sqrt5}=-\frac{\sqrt5}{5}$.
  3. $\sin\alpha=-2\cos\alpha=\frac{2\sqrt5}{5}$ (positive, consistent).

$\sin\alpha=\frac{2\sqrt5}{5},\ \cos\alpha=-\frac{\sqrt5}{5}$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Compute $\sin120^\circ\cdot\cos150^\circ+\tan135^\circ$.
    Solution
    $\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot\left(-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)+(-1)=-\frac34-1$. $-\frac74$
  2. medium
    The angle $\alpha$ is obtuse and $\tan\alpha=-\frac34$. Find $\sin\alpha$ and $\cos\alpha$.
    Solution
    $\sin\alpha=-\frac34\cos\alpha$, so $\frac{9}{16}\cos^2\alpha+\cos^2\alpha=1$, $\cos^2\alpha=\frac{16}{25}$. The angle is obtuse: $\cos\alpha=-\frac45$, $\sin\alpha=\frac35$.
  3. medium
    It is known that $\sin\alpha+\cos\alpha=\frac75$. Find $\sin\alpha\cdot\cos\alpha$.
    Solution
    Square it: $\sin^2\alpha+2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha+\cos^2\alpha=\frac{49}{25}$, i.e. $1+2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha=\frac{49}{25}$. $\sin\alpha\cos\alpha=\frac{12}{25}$
  4. medium
    Show that for every acute angle $\alpha$: $\;\tan\alpha+\frac{1}{\tan\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}$.
    Solution
    $L=\frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}+\frac{\cos\alpha}{\sin\alpha}=\frac{\sin^2\alpha+\cos^2\alpha}{\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}=R$. The identity holds (the denominators are nonzero because the angle is acute).
  5. exam-level
    The angle $\alpha\in(0^\circ,180^\circ)$ satisfies $\sin\alpha-\cos\alpha=\frac12$. Find $\sin\alpha\cos\alpha$ and $\sin\alpha+\cos\alpha$.
    Solution
    Square: $1-2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha=\frac14$, so $\sin\alpha\cos\alpha=\frac38$. Then $(\sin\alpha+\cos\alpha)^2=1+2\cdot\frac38=\frac74$. The product is positive and $\sin\alpha>0$, so $\cos\alpha>0$ and the sum is positive. $\sin\alpha\cos\alpha=\frac38$, $\;\sin\alpha+\cos\alpha=\frac{\sqrt7}{2}$

6. On the exam

  • The sign from the square root: after using the identity always write why you choose $+$ or $-$ (acute / obtuse angle). Without it you lose a point.
  • Reduce an obtuse angle to an acute one: $\cos135^\circ=-\cos45^\circ$, $\sin150^\circ=\sin30^\circ$.
  • A typical trick: given $\sin\alpha\pm\cos\alpha$ → square it and use the identity, you get $\sin\alpha\cos\alpha$.
  • In a triangle an angle found from a sine can have two values ($\alpha$ and $180^\circ-\alpha$). Check whether both fit the angle sum.
