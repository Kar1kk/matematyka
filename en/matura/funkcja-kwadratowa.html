Quadratic function

The graph is always the same parabola $y=x^2$, just stretched and shifted. The whole trick is to find the vertex and the direction of the arms.

V.7–V.11, XIII (optimization)

1. Three forms and formulas

General form
$$f(x)=ax^2+bx+c,\quad a\neq 0$$
$a$
$a>0$: arms up, $a<0$: down. The larger $|a|$, the narrower the parabola
$c$
the intersection with the $Oy$ axis, because $f(0)=c$
Vertex form
$$f(x)=a(x-p)^2+q$$ $$p=\frac{-b}{2a},\qquad q=\frac{-\Delta}{4a}$$
$(p,q)$
the vertex: the parabola $y=ax^2$ translated by the vector $[p,q]$
$x=p$
the axis of symmetry
Factored form (when $\Delta\ge 0$)
$$f(x)=a(x-x_1)(x-x_2)$$ $$x_{1,2}=\frac{-b\mp\sqrt{\Delta}}{2a},\qquad \Delta=b^2-4ac$$
$\Delta$
$\Delta>0$: two zeros, $\Delta=0$: one, $\Delta<0$: none
$p$
exactly halfway between the roots: $p=\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}$
$q=f(p)$. Instead of computing $-\Delta/4a$, it is usually faster to substitute $p$ into the formula.
Where does the formula for $x_{1,2}$ come from? (proof from the core curriculum)

Factor out $a$ and complete the square:

$$ax^2+bx+c=a\left[x^2+\frac{b}{a}x+\frac{c}{a}\right]$$ $$=a\left[\left(x+\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2-\frac{\Delta}{4a^2}\right]$$

When $\Delta\ge0$, the bracket is a difference of squares, so $f(x)=0 \iff x+\frac{b}{2a}=\pm\frac{\sqrt\Delta}{2a}$. When $\Delta<0$, the bracket is always positive, so there are no roots. On the way we see the vertex form: $p=-\frac{b}{2a}$, $q=-\frac{\Delta}{4a}$.

4. Step-by-step example

Write $f(x)=x^2-2x-3$ in vertex form and in factored form.

  1. $\Delta=(-2)^2-4\cdot1\cdot(-3)=16$, so $\sqrt\Delta=4$.
  2. $x_1=\frac{2-4}{2}=-1$, $\;x_2=\frac{2+4}{2}=3$.
  3. $p=\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}=1$, $\;q=f(1)=1-2-3=-4$.

$f(x)=(x-1)^2-4=(x+1)(x-3)$

This is exactly the parabola from the graph in section 2 (the starting settings).

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Find the vertex of the parabola $f(x)=2x^2-8x+5$.
    Solution
    $p=\frac{8}{4}=2$, $q=f(2)=8-16+5=-3$. $W=(2,-3)$
  2. medium
    Find the formula of the quadratic function whose graph has the vertex $W=(1,-4)$ and passes through the point $(3,4)$.
    Solution
    $f(x)=a(x-1)^2-4$. Substitute $(3,4)$: $4=4a-4$, so $a=2$. $f(x)=2(x-1)^2-4=2x^2-4x-2$
  3. medium
    Find the least and the greatest value of $f(x)=-x^2+4x+1$ in the interval $[-1,3]$.
    Solution
    $p=2\in[-1,3]$ and $a<0$, so the greatest value is $f(2)=5$. The least is at one of the endpoints: $f(-1)=-4$, $f(3)=4$. Greatest $5$, least $-4$.
  4. exam-level
    The sum of two real numbers is $10$. Find the numbers for which the sum of their squares is the smallest, and compute that sum.
    Solution
    The numbers $x$ and $10-x$. $S(x)=x^2+(10-x)^2=2x^2-20x+100$, $x\in\R$. The parabola has its arms up, so the minimum is at the vertex: $p=5$, $S(5)=50$. The numbers $5$ and $5$, the least sum of squares is $50$.

6. On the exam

  • Greatest/least value in an interval: first check whether $p$ lies in the interval. If not, you compute only the endpoints.
  • Optimization: always state the domain of the function (e.g. a length $>0$). The vertex must belong to it.
  • Quadratic inequality: sketch the parabola instead of a “sign table”. Watch the endpoints of the intervals with $\ge$ and $>$.
  • At the extended level the most points come from tasks with a parameter (conditions on $\Delta$, $a$, Vieta's formulas).
