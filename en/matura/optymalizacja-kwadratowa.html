Optimization with a quadratic function

“Largest area”, “smallest cost”: the quantity you are optimizing is written as a function of one variable. If it turns out to be a parabola, the answer sits at the vertex, provided the vertex belongs to the domain.

XIII (basic level), V.10, V.11

1. The 5-step scheme

  1. Variable: call one quantity $x$ (e.g. the width). Express the others through $x$ using the condition of the problem.
  2. Domain: lengths $>0$, constraints from the statement. Write it out explicitly, e.g. $x\in(0,10)$.
  3. Objective function: $P(x)=ax^2+bx+c$.
  4. Vertex: $p=\frac{-b}{2a}$. Check whether $p$ belongs to the domain and what the sign of $a$ is.
  5. Answer the question from the statement: the dimensions and the value (area, cost).
Greatest/least value on $[ \alpha,\beta]$ (V.10)
$$p\in[ \alpha,\beta]:\ f(p),\ f(\alpha),\ f(\beta)$$ $$p\notin[ \alpha,\beta]:\ f(\alpha),\ f(\beta)$$

You compare only these candidates: the largest of them is the maximum, the smallest is the minimum.

$a<0$
arms down, the vertex is a maximum
$a>0$
arms up, the vertex is a minimum
When the domain is an open interval (e.g. $x\in(0,10)$), the values at the endpoints are not attained. Then for $a<0$ (arms down) a greatest value exists only if the vertex is inside the interval; for $a>0$ the vertex gives the least value (and there is no greatest one).

2. Interactive: a fence against a wall

You have $20$ m of fencing for three sides of a rectangle; the fourth side is a wall. Drag the corner (or the slider $x$) and watch the point travel along the parabola $P(x)=x(20-2x)$. Shorten the wall and the vertex falls out of the domain.

3. Interactive: is the vertex in the interval?

Drag the ends of the interval $\alpha$, $\beta$ along the $Ox$ axis. When the vertex falls outside the interval, both extrema land at the endpoints.

4. Worked example

With $20$ m of fencing we enclose a rectangular pen against a wall (fencing on three sides). Which dimensions give the largest area?

  1. $x$: the side perpendicular to the wall. The side parallel to it: $20-2x$.
  2. Domain: $x>0$ and $20-2x>0$, that is $x\in(0,10)$.
  3. $P(x)=x(20-2x)=-2x^2+20x$, $a=-2<0$.
  4. $p=\frac{-20}{-4}=5\in(0,10)$, so the maximum is at $x=5$: $P(5)=5\cdot10=50$.

Dimensions $5$ m × $10$ m, area $50\ \text{m}^2$.

The same, but the wall is only $8$ m long.

  1. New condition: $20-2x\le 8$, that is $x\ge6$. Domain: $x\in[6,10)$.
  2. $p=5\notin[6,10)$, and on this domain the parabola is decreasing.
  3. The greatest value is at the left end: $P(6)=6\cdot8=48$.

Dimensions $6$ m × $8$ m, area $48\ \text{m}^2$ (check in the demo: wall $8$).

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    The sum of two numbers is $12$. For which numbers is their product the greatest?
    Solution
    $f(x)=x(12-x)=-x^2+12x$, $x\in\R$. $p=6$, $a<0$. The numbers $6$ and $6$, product $36$.
  2. easy
    The difference of two numbers is $8$. For which numbers is their product the least?
    Solution
    The numbers $x$ and $x-8$: $f(x)=x^2-8x$, $a>0$, $p=4$. The numbers $4$ and $-4$, product $-16$.
  3. medium
    At a cinema ticket price of $20$ PLN, $300$ people come. Each price increase of $1$ PLN reduces the audience by $10$. Which price gives the greatest revenue?
    Solution
    An increase of $k$ PLN, $k\in[-20,30]$: $$R(k)=(20+k)(300-10k)$$ $$=-10k^2+100k+6000$$ $p=5$ belongs to the domain, $a<0$. Price $25$ PLN, $250$ viewers, revenue $6250$ PLN.
  4. medium
    A rectangle with two sides lying on the legs is inscribed in a right triangle with legs $6$ and $8$. Find the greatest possible area of the rectangle.
    Solution
    Setup: the right-angle vertex at $(0,0)$, the hypotenuse $\frac{x}{6}+\frac{y}{8}=1$. The vertex $(x,y)$ of the rectangle lies on it: $y=8-\frac43x$, $x\in(0,6)$. $$P(x)=x\left(8-\tfrac43x\right)$$ $p=3$, $y=4$. The greatest area is $12$ (half the area of the triangle).
  5. exam-level
    On the line $y=2x-1$ find the point $P$ closest to the point $A=(4,1)$.
    Solution
    $P=(x,2x-1)$. Minimize the squared distance (it has its minimum at the same $x$): $$d^2(x)=(x-4)^2+(2x-2)^2$$ $$=5x^2-16x+20$$ $p=\frac{16}{10}=\frac85$, $d^2=7.2$. $P=\left(\frac85,\frac{11}{5}\right)$, $|AP|=\frac{6\sqrt5}{5}$ (agrees with the formula for the distance from a point to a line).

6. On the exam

  • The domain is a separate point in the marking scheme. Without it you do not know whether the vertex “counts”.
  • They ask for the dimensions and you give only $x$? Lost point. Answer in a full sentence: the dimensions and the value.
  • Vertex outside the domain: the function is monotonic on the domain, and the extremum is at an endpoint (provided the endpoint belongs to the domain).
  • Distance: optimize $d^2$ instead of $d$. The square root is increasing, so the minimum is at the same place.
  • If the objective function turns out cubic or rational, you need the derivative.
