Sum and difference formulas, double angle

$\sin(\alpha+\beta)\neq\sin\alpha+\sin\beta$. The true formula follows from a simple observation: a chord of a circle depends only on the angle between the radii, so rotating both points does not change it. The rest (double angle, tangent) is just substitution.

VII.R.5

1. Formulas

Theorem For any $\alpha,\beta\in\R$ on CKE sheet: $$\sin(\alpha\pm\beta)=\sin\alpha\cos\beta\pm\cos\alpha\sin\beta$$ $$\cos(\alpha\pm\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta\mp\sin\alpha\sin\beta$$
Proof (required on the exam)

1. A chord in two ways. On the unit circle take $A=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$ and $B=(\cos\beta,\sin\beta)$. From the distance formula and the Pythagorean identity:

$$|AB|^2=(\cos\alpha-\cos\beta)^2$$ $$+\,(\sin\alpha-\sin\beta)^2$$ $$=2-2(\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta)$$

2. Rotation. Rotate both points about $O$ by the angle $-\beta$. You get $A'=(\cos(\alpha-\beta),\sin(\alpha-\beta))$ and $B'=(1,0)$. A rotation preserves distances, so $|A'B'|=|AB|$, and

$$|A'B'|^2=(\cos(\alpha-\beta)-1)^2$$ $$+\,\sin^2(\alpha-\beta)=2-2\cos(\alpha-\beta)$$

3. Compare the two results:

$$\cos(\alpha-\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta$$

4. The sum. Put $-\beta$ in place of $\beta$; $\cos(-\beta)=\cos\beta$, $\sin(-\beta)=-\sin\beta$: $\;\cos(\alpha+\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta-\sin\alpha\sin\beta$.

5. Sine. From $\sin x=\cos\left(\frac\pi2-x\right)$ and the formula from step 3:

$$\sin(\alpha+\beta)=\cos\left(\left(\tfrac\pi2-\alpha\right)-\beta\right)$$ $$=\cos\left(\tfrac\pi2-\alpha\right)\cos\beta$$ $$+\,\sin\left(\tfrac\pi2-\alpha\right)\sin\beta$$ $$=\sin\alpha\cos\beta+\cos\alpha\sin\beta$$

6. $\sin(\alpha-\beta)$: again $\beta\to-\beta$. $\blacksquare$

2. Interactive: the proof on a circle

Set $\alpha$ and $\beta$, then “▶ Rotate by −β”. The chord $AB$ (red) rotates together with the points and does not change length. After the rotation $B'=(1,0)$, and $A'$ has coordinates $(\cos(\alpha-\beta),\sin(\alpha-\beta))$.

3. Interactive: $\sin(\alpha+\beta)$ as a sum of two heights

The segment $OQ$ has length $1$ and angle $\alpha+\beta$, so its height is $\sin(\alpha+\beta)$. Split it into $OP=\cos\beta$ (at the angle $\alpha$) and $PQ=\sin\beta$ (perpendicular). The height of $P$ is $\sin\alpha\cos\beta$ (red), and $PQ$ adds $\cos\alpha\sin\beta$ vertically (green).

4. Step-by-step example

Compute $\sin75^\circ$ and $\tan15^\circ$.

  1. $\sin(45^\circ+30^\circ)=\frac{\sqrt2}2\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}2+\frac{\sqrt2}2\cdot\frac12=\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}$.
  2. $\tan(45^\circ-30^\circ)=\dfrac{1-\frac{\sqrt3}3}{1+\frac{\sqrt3}3}=\dfrac{3-\sqrt3}{3+\sqrt3}$.
  3. Rationalize: $\dfrac{(3-\sqrt3)^2}{9-3}=\dfrac{12-6\sqrt3}{6}=2-\sqrt3$.

$\sin75^\circ=\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4},\quad \tan15^\circ=2-\sqrt3$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Compute $\cos105^\circ$.
    Solution
    $\cos(60^\circ+45^\circ)=\frac12\cdot\frac{\sqrt2}2-\frac{\sqrt3}2\cdot\frac{\sqrt2}2$. $\frac{\sqrt2-\sqrt6}{4}$
  2. medium
    $\sin\alpha=\frac35$ and $\alpha\in\left(\frac\pi2,\pi\right)$. Compute $\sin2\alpha$ and $\cos2\alpha$.
    Solution
    Second quadrant: $\cos\alpha=-\frac45$. $\sin2\alpha=2\cdot\frac35\cdot\left(-\frac45\right)$, $\cos2\alpha=1-2\cdot\frac{9}{25}$. $\sin2\alpha=-\frac{24}{25}$, $\cos2\alpha=\frac{7}{25}$
  3. medium
    The angles $\alpha,\beta$ are acute, $\tan\alpha=2$, $\tan\beta=3$. Compute $\alpha+\beta$.
    Solution
    $\tan(\alpha+\beta)=\frac{2+3}{1-6}=-1$, and $\alpha+\beta\in(0,\pi)$. The only angle in this interval with tangent $-1$: $\alpha+\beta=\frac{3\pi}{4}$
  4. medium
    It is known that $\tan x=\frac12$. Compute $\cos2x$.
    Solution
    $\cos2x=\frac{\cos^2x-\sin^2x}{\cos^2x+\sin^2x}$; divide the numerator and denominator by $\cos^2x$: $\frac{1-\tan^2x}{1+\tan^2x}=\frac{3/4}{5/4}$. $\frac35$
  5. exam-level
    Prove that $\dfrac{\sin3\alpha}{\sin\alpha}-\dfrac{\cos3\alpha}{\cos\alpha}=2$ for $\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\neq0$.
    Solution
    Common denominator: $\frac{\sin3\alpha\cos\alpha-\cos3\alpha\sin\alpha}{\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}=\frac{\sin(3\alpha-\alpha)}{\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}=\frac{2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}{\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}$. $=2$
  6. exam-level
    Prove that if $\alpha+\beta=\frac\pi4$ (and $\tan\alpha$, $\tan\beta$ exist), then $(1+\tan\alpha)(1+\tan\beta)=2$.
    Solution
    $1=\tan\frac\pi4=\frac{\tan\alpha+\tan\beta}{1-\tan\alpha\tan\beta}$, so $\tan\alpha+\tan\beta=1-\tan\alpha\tan\beta$. Hence $(1+\tan\alpha)(1+\tan\beta)=1+\tan\alpha+\tan\beta+\tan\alpha\tan\beta$ $=2$.

6. On the exam

  • The proof of the formula for $\cos(\alpha-\beta)$ can be a task in itself. Remember three steps: the chord from coordinates, rotation by $-\beta$, comparison.
  • Recognize the formulas “backwards”: $\sin3\alpha\cos\alpha-\cos3\alpha\sin\alpha=\sin2\alpha$, $\;2\sin x\cos x=\sin2x$, $\;\cos^2x-\sin^2x=\cos2x$.
  • When finding an angle from a tangent, determine the interval in which the angle lies (e.g. $\alpha+\beta\in(0,\pi)$), otherwise the answer is not unique.
  • Determine the sign of $\cos\alpha$ from the quadrant – before using the formula for $\sin2\alpha$.
