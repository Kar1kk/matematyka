Similar solids

Enlarge a solid $2$ times: every length grows $2$ times, the area $4$ times (because it has two dimensions), and the volume $8$ times (because it has three). This one observation solves a whole class of tasks.

X.6

1. The scale k, k², k³

Similar solids with scale $k>0$: every segment of one solid is $k$ times longer than the corresponding segment of the other (the angles do not change). All spheres are similar, and so are all cubes.
Similar solids with scale $k$ (to remember)
$$\frac{P_2}{P_1}=k^2,\qquad \frac{V_2}{V_1}=k^3$$
$k$
the ratio of corresponding lengths: edges, heights, radii, diagonals
$P$
any area: total surface area, lateral area, base, cross-section
A cross-section parallel to the base A plane parallel to the base of a pyramid (cone) cuts off a pyramid (cone) similar to the original with scale $k=\frac{h}{H}$, where $h$ is the distance of the plane from the apex.
Why $k^3$?
With scale $k$ every base area grows $k^2$ times (similar figures), and the height $k$ times. For a prism, pyramid, cylinder and cone $V=c\cdot P_p\cdot H$, so $V$ grows $k^2\cdot k=k^3$ times. For a sphere: $V=\frac43\pi r^3$, and $r$ grows $k$ times.

2. Interactive: scaling

Move $k$ or press ▶ ($k$ from $1$ to $2$). Green: the original solid ($k=1$), blue: the scaled one. See how fast $k^3$ grows compared with $k$.

3. Interactive: a cut-off pyramid

Move the plane parallel to the base. The cut-off pyramid (red) is similar to the whole one with scale $k=\frac hH$, so its volume is $k^3$ of the whole.

4. Step-by-step example

A pyramid of volume $54$ was cut by a plane parallel to the base, at a distance of $\frac13$ of the height from the apex. Find the volumes of the two resulting solids.

  1. The cut-off pyramid is similar with scale $k=\frac13$.
  2. $V_{\text{small}}=k^3\cdot54=\frac{54}{27}=2$.
  3. The other solid (a frustum): $54-2=52$.

$2$ and $52$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Spheres have radii $2$ and $6$. Give the ratio of their surface areas and of their volumes.
    Solution
    $k=3$. Areas $1:9$, volumes $1:27$
  2. easy
    Cube $A$ has surface area $24$, cube $B$ has area $96$. How many times is the volume of $B$ larger than the volume of $A$?
    Solution
    $k^2=4$, so $k=2$ and $k^3=8$ (check: $V_A=8$, $V_B=64$). 8 times
  3. medium
    The ratio of the volumes of two similar solids is $8:125$. Find the ratio of their surface areas.
    Solution
    $k^3=\frac{8}{125}\Rightarrow k=\frac25$, $k^2=\frac{4}{25}$. $4:25$
  4. medium
    A cone of volume $64$ was cut by a plane parallel to the base at half of the height. Find the volumes of the resulting solids.
    Solution
    The small cone: $k=\frac12$, $V=\frac{64}{8}=8$. The rest: $64-8$. $8$ and $56$
  5. exam-level
    A glass has the shape of a cone (apex down). Water was poured into it up to half of the height. What fraction of the glass's capacity does the water occupy? To what height must water be poured to fill half of the capacity?
    Solution
    The water forms a cone similar with scale $\frac12$: $\left(\frac12\right)^3=\frac18$. Half of the volume: $k^3=\frac12$, $k=\frac{1}{\sqrt[3]2}\approx0.79$. $\frac18$ of the capacity; up to $\frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{2}}=\frac{\sqrt[3]{4}}{2}$ of the height ($\approx79\%$)
  6. exam-level
    A pyramid has height $10$. At what distance from the base should it be cut by a plane parallel to the base to divide it into two solids of equal volumes? Give the exact and the approximate result.
    Solution
    The cut-off pyramid: $k^3=\frac12$, its height $\frac{10}{\sqrt[3]2}=5\sqrt[3]4\approx7.94$. Distance from the base: $10-5\sqrt[3]4$. $10-5\sqrt[3]{4}\approx2.06$

6. On the exam

  • First determine $k$ from lengths. If areas are given, $k=\sqrt{\text{ratio of areas}}$; if volumes, $k=\sqrt[3]{\text{ratio of volumes}}$.
  • For a parallel cross-section you measure $k$ from the apex, not from the base.
  • A frustum of a pyramid (cone) is a difference: $V_{\text{frustum}}=V(1-k^3)$.
  • Trap: a cone “$2$ times taller” with the same radius is not similar. Similarity requires scaling all dimensions.
