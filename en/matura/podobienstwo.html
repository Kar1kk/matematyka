Similarity of figures

Similar figures are the same figure at a different scale: angles unchanged, all lengths multiplied by $k$. Area grows faster – by a factor of $k^2$, because you multiply two lengths at once.

VIII.8, VIII.9

1. Similarity and the similarity criteria for triangles

Definition Figure $\mathcal B$ is similar to figure $\mathcal A$ with scale $k>0$ if the distance between any two points of $\mathcal B$ is $k$ times the distance between the corresponding points of $\mathcal A$. We write $\triangle ABC\sim\triangle KLM$ – the order of the letters tells which vertices correspond to each other.
Similarity criteria for triangles on CKE sheet
  • SSS: three pairs of proportional sides: $\frac{|KL|}{|AB|}=\frac{|LM|}{|BC|}=\frac{|MK|}{|CA|}$.
  • SAS: two pairs of proportional sides and equal angles between them.
  • AA: equal angles. Two pairs are enough – the third follows from the sum $180^\circ$.
In the SAS criterion the angle must lie between those sides. Two proportional sides and an angle “next to them” are not enough.

The most common sources of similarity: parallel lines (corresponding/alternate angles – Thales), a common angle + a right angle (altitude in a right triangle), inscribed angles subtending the same arc.

2. Perimeters and areas of similar figures

Similar figures with scale $k$
$$\frac{\text{Perim}_{\mathcal B}}{\text{Perim}_{\mathcal A}}=k$$ $$\frac{P_{\mathcal B}}{P_{\mathcal A}}=k^2$$
$k$
all “lengths” scale the same way: altitudes, medians, diagonals, radii $R$ and $r$
$k^2$
is in the tables (“Areas of similar figures”). For a triangle: $\frac12(ka)(kh)=k^2\cdot\frac12ah$
When you know the ratio of the areas, the scale is its square root: areas $1:4$ mean sides $1:2$, not $1:4$.

3. Interactive: scale $k$

Move the $k$ slider and drag the vertices and the centre $S$. Triangle $A'B'C'$ is made by “stretching” every point away from $S$ by a factor of $k$. The perimeter grows $k$ times, the area $k^2$ times.

Why $k^2$? A triangle enlarged $k$ times can be cut into $k^2$ copies of the original (red). Change $k$ and the shape of the triangle.

4. Step-by-step example

Triangle $ABC$ has sides $4$, $6$, $8$ and area $P$. Triangle $KLM$ is similar to it, and its shortest side has length $6$. Find the remaining sides of $KLM$, its perimeter and its area (in terms of $P$).

  1. The shortest corresponds to the shortest: $k=\frac64=\frac32$.
  2. Sides: $6\cdot\frac32=9$, $8\cdot\frac32=12$. Perimeter: $18\cdot\frac32=27$.
  3. Area: $k^2P=\frac94P$.

Sides $6$, $9$, $12$; perimeter $27$; area $\frac94P$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    The areas of two similar triangles are $12$ and $75$. Find the ratio of their perimeters (larger to smaller).
    Solution
    $k^2=\frac{75}{12}=\frac{25}{4}$, so $k=\frac52$.
  2. easy
    Are the triangles with sides a) $3, 4, 6$ and $4.5;\ 6;\ 9$; b) $6, 8, 12$ and $9, 12, 16$ similar?
    Solution
    Arrange the sides in increasing order and divide corresponding ones. a) $\frac{4.5}{3}=\frac64=\frac96=1.5$ – yes (SSS). b) $\frac96=\frac{12}{8}=1.5$, but $\frac{16}{12}=\frac43$ – no.
  3. medium
    In right triangle $ABC$ ($|\angle C|=90^\circ$) $|AC|=6$, $|BC|=8$. On the hypotenuse a point $D$ is chosen so that $|BD|=4$, and the line perpendicular to $AB$ at $D$ meets $BC$ at point $E$. Find $|DE|$ and $|BE|$.
    Solution
    $|AB|=10$. $\triangle BDE\sim\triangle BCA$ (AA: common angle $B$ and right angles at $D$ and $C$), so $\frac{|BD|}{|BC|}=\frac{|DE|}{|CA|}=\frac{|BE|}{|BA|}$, that is $\frac48=\frac{|DE|}{6}=\frac{|BE|}{10}$. $|DE|=3$, $|BE|=5$
  4. medium
    A triangle has base $12$ and the altitude to it equals $8$. A line parallel to the base divides it into two figures of equal areas. At what distance from the vertex opposite the base does this line lie?
    Solution
    The cut-off triangle is similar to the whole one (parallel lines) and has half its area: $k^2=\frac12$, $k=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$. The altitude also scales by $k$: $8\cdot\frac{\sqrt2}2$. $4\sqrt2$
  5. exam-level
    In triangle $ABC$: $|AB|=9$, $|AC|=12$, $|BC|=10$. A point $D$ is chosen on side $AC$ so that $|AD|=6.75$. Show that $\triangle ABD\sim\triangle ACB$, and find $|BD|$.
    Solution
    $\frac{|AD|}{|AB|}=\frac{6.75}{9}=\frac34$ and $\frac{|AB|}{|AC|}=\frac{9}{12}=\frac34$, and angle $A$ is common and lies between these sides – the SAS criterion. The correspondence is $A\to A$, $B\to C$, $D\to B$, so $\frac{|BD|}{|CB|}=\frac34$. $|BD|=7.5$
  6. exam-level
    The perimeters of two similar triangles are $18$ and $30$, and the area of the larger one is $75$. Find the area of the smaller one.
    Solution
    The scale (smaller to larger) is $k=\frac{18}{30}=\frac35$, so $P=75\cdot\frac{9}{25}$. $P=27$

6. On the exam

  • Write similarity with the correct order of vertices and the name of the criterion (e.g. “AA: common angle $B$ and right angles”). Without justifying the criterion you lose a point.
  • Establish the correspondence of sides from the angles: the side opposite equal angles corresponds. Do not match “by eye” from the drawing.
  • Areas $\to$ scale: square root. Scale $\to$ areas: square. A typical mistake is mixing up these directions.
  • Cutting off with a line parallel to a side always gives a triangle similar to the original.
