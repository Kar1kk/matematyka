Vectors

A vector is a "recipe for a translation": how far right and how far up. It has no fixed place, so you can attach it anywhere, and that is exactly why vectors are so easy to add.

IX.R.3

1. Coordinates and length

The vector from $A=(x_A,y_A)$ to $B=(x_B,y_B)$
$$\vec{AB}=[x_B-x_A,\ y_B-y_A]$$ $$|\vec{u}|=\sqrt{u_1^2+u_2^2}\quad\text{for }\vec u=[u_1,u_2]$$
order
always end minus start; $\vec{BA}=-\vec{AB}$
equality
vectors are equal $\iff$ they have equal coordinates (the same length, direction and sense)

2. Addition and multiplication by a number

Analytically: $\vec u=[u_1,u_2]$, $\vec v=[v_1,v_2]$
$$\vec u+\vec v=[u_1+v_1,\ u_2+v_2]$$ $$k\cdot\vec u=[k u_1,\ k u_2]$$
formula sheet
the coordinates of a vector and both operations are on the formula sheet
Addition geometrically Triangle method: attach $\vec v$ at the end of $\vec u$; the sum goes from the start of $\vec u$ to the end of $\vec v$. Parallelogram method: attach both at one point; the sum is the diagonal. Hence the rule $\vec{AB}+\vec{BC}=\vec{AC}$.
Multiplication geometrically $k\cdot\vec u$ has length $|k|\cdot|\vec u|$, the same direction; the same sense for $k>0$, the opposite for $k<0$. Subtraction: $\vec u-\vec v=\vec u+(-\vec v)$.

Drag $A$ and $B$, change $k$. The point $P$ satisfies $\vec{AP}=k\cdot\vec{AB}$. For $0\le k\le1$ it lies on the segment $AB$ and divides it in the ratio $k:(1-k)$; for $k<0$ the sense reverses.

5. Worked example

Given $A=(-1,2)$, $B=(3,-1)$, $C=(4,5)$. Find $D$ so that $ABCD$ is a parallelogram, and find $|\vec{AB}|$.

  1. $\vec{AB}=[3-(-1),\ -1-2]=[4,-3]$.
  2. $D=(x,y)$: $\vec{DC}=[4-x,\ 5-y]$. From $\vec{AB}=\vec{DC}$: $4-x=4$, $5-y=-3$.
  3. $D=(0,8)$. Check: $\vec{AD}=[1,6]=\vec{BC}$ ✓.
  4. $|\vec{AB}|=\sqrt{16+9}=5$.

$D=(0,8)$, $|\vec{AB}|=5$

6. Exercises

  1. easy
    $\vec u=[3,-1]$, $\vec v=[-2,4]$. Find $2\vec u-\vec v$ and its length.
    Solution
    $2\vec u=[6,-2]$, $2\vec u-\vec v=[6+2,\ -2-4]=[8,-6]$, length $\sqrt{64+36}=10$. $[8,-6]$, length $10$
  2. easy
    $\vec{AB}=[-3,5]$ and $B=(1,2)$. Find $A$.
    Solution
    $1-x_A=-3$, $2-y_A=5$. $A=(4,-3)$
  3. medium
    For which $m$ are the vectors $\vec u=[m,4]$ and $\vec v=[2,m-2]$ parallel?
    Solution
    $m(m-2)-4\cdot2=0$, $m^2-2m-8=0$, $(m-4)(m+2)=0$. Check $m=4$: $[4,4]=2\cdot[2,2]$ ✓. $m=4$ or $m=-2$
  4. medium
    The point $P$ lies on the segment $AB$, $A=(-2,1)$, $B=(7,7)$, and $|AP|:|PB|=1:2$. Find $P$.
    Solution
    $\vec{AB}=[9,6]$, $\vec{AP}=\frac13\vec{AB}=[3,2]$, $P=(-2+3,\ 1+2)$. $P=(1,3)$
  5. exam-level
    The points $A=(-1,-4)$, $B=(2,2)$, $C=(m,8)$ are collinear. Find $m$.
    Solution
    $\vec{AB}=[3,6]$, $\vec{AC}=[m+1,12]$. Parallelism: $3\cdot12-6(m+1)=0$, $m+1=6$. Check: $\vec{AC}=[6,12]=2\vec{AB}$ ✓. $m=5$
  6. exam-level
    A triangle $A=(-2,-1)$, $B=(4,1)$, $C=(1,6)$. $S$ is the midpoint of $BC$, and the point $G$ satisfies $\vec{AG}=\frac23\vec{AS}$. Find $G$ and compare it with the average of the vertices' coordinates.
    Solution
    $S=\left(\frac52,\frac72\right)$, $\vec{AS}=\left[\frac92,\frac92\right]$, $\vec{AG}=[3,3]$, $G=(1,2)$. Average: $\left(\frac{-2+4+1}{3},\frac{-1+1+6}{3}\right)=(1,2)$, the same point: it is the centroid (the intersection of the medians). $G=(1,2)$

7. On the exam

  • $\vec{AB}$ is end minus start. The reverse order gives the opposite vector and a wrong result.
  • Parallelogram $ABCD$: the vertices go in order, so $\vec{AB}=\vec{DC}$, and not $\vec{AB}=\vec{CD}$.
  • Do not confuse the vector $[x,y]$ with the point $(x,y)$. A point is a place, a vector is a translation.
  • Check parallelism of vectors with $u_1v_2-u_2v_1=0$: it also works when some coordinate is zero (division would fail here).
