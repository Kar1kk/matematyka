Arithmetic sequence

At each step you add the same number $r$, so the points $(n,a_n)$ lie on one line. You compute the sum with Gauss's trick: two copies of the "staircase" fit together into a rectangle.

VI.4, VI.5, VI.7

1. Definition and formulas

Definition A sequence $(a_n)$ is arithmetic when the difference $a_{n+1}-a_n=r$ is constant (the same for every $n$). The number $r$ is called the common difference of the sequence.
The $n$-th term on CKE sheet
$$a_n=a_1+(n-1)r$$ $$a_n=a_k+(n-k)r$$
$r$
$r>0$: increasing, $r<0$: decreasing, $r=0$: constant
graph
points on the line $y=rx+(a_1-r)$: an arithmetic sequence is a "linear function on $\N_+$"
Sum of the first $n$ terms on CKE sheet
$$S_n=\frac{a_1+a_n}{2}\cdot n$$ $$S_n=\frac{2a_1+(n-1)r}{2}\cdot n$$
meaning
the average of the first and last term times the number of terms
Arithmetic mean of the neighbors on CKE sheet
$$a_n=\frac{a_{n-1}+a_{n+1}}{2}$$
$x,y,z$
form (in this order) an arithmetic sequence $\iff 2y=x+z$
Theorem In an arithmetic sequence $a_n=a_1+(n-1)r$ and $S_n=\frac{a_1+a_n}{2}\cdot n$.
Proof (required on the exam)

The formula for $a_n$. By definition $a_{k+1}-a_k=r$ for $k=1,2,\ldots,n-1$. Add these $n-1$ equalities side by side:

$$(a_2-a_1)+(a_3-a_2)+\ldots$$ $$\ldots+(a_n-a_{n-1})=(n-1)r$$

On the left everything cancels except $a_n-a_1$, so $a_n=a_1+(n-1)r$.

The formula for $S_n$ (Gauss's method). Write the sum twice, once in reverse:

$$\begin{aligned}S_n&=a_1+a_2+\ldots+a_n\\ S_n&=a_n+a_{n-1}+\ldots+a_1\end{aligned}$$

The terms standing one under the other are $a_k$ and $a_{n+1-k}$. Their sum is always the same:

$$a_k+a_{n+1-k}=$$ $$=a_1+(k-1)r+a_1+(n-k)r$$ $$=2a_1+(n-1)r=a_1+a_n$$

Adding both rows, you get $n$ pairs, each equal to $a_1+a_n$: $2S_n=n(a_1+a_n)$, so $S_n=\frac{a_1+a_n}{2}\cdot n$. Substituting $a_n=a_1+(n-1)r$, you get the second form.

This is exactly what the animation in section 3 shows: the reversed copy of the "staircase" completes it to the rectangle $n\times(a_1+a_n)$.

2. Points on a line

Change $a_1$ and $r$. Each step to the right by $1$ is a step up by $r$ (red arrows), so all the points lie on a line with slope $r$.

3. Animation: the sum by Gauss's method

Bars of heights $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ (blue) make up $S_n$. Click ▶: the red copy rotates by $180^\circ$ and is placed on top. The result is the rectangle $n\times(a_1+a_n)$, i.e. $2S_n$.

4. Step-by-step example

In an arithmetic sequence $a_2=5$ and $a_6=17$. Find $S_{10}$.

  1. $a_6=a_2+4r$, so $17=5+4r$ and $r=3$.
  2. $a_1=a_2-r=2$.
  3. $S_{10}=\frac{2a_1+9r}{2}\cdot10=\frac{4+27}{2}\cdot10=155$.

$S_{10}=155$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Show that the sequence $a_n=5-3n$ is arithmetic, and find the sum of its first $20$ terms.
    Solution
    $a_{n+1}-a_n=5-3(n+1)-(5-3n)=-3$, constant, so the sequence is arithmetic, $r=-3$. $a_1=2$, $a_{20}=-55$, $S_{20}=\frac{2-55}{2}\cdot20$. $S_{20}=-530$
  2. medium
    The numbers $x-1$, $x^2$, $4x-1$ form (in this order) an arithmetic sequence. Find $x$.
    Solution
    $2x^2=(x-1)+(4x-1)$, i.e. $2x^2-5x+2=0$, $\Delta=9$, $x=\frac{5\mp3}{4}$. Check: $x=2$: $1,4,7$; $x=\frac12$: $-\frac12,\frac14,1$. $x=2$ or $x=\frac12$
  3. medium
    Find the sum of all natural numbers in the interval $[100, 500]$ that are divisible by $7$.
    Solution
    The smallest is $105=7\cdot15$, the largest $497=7\cdot71$. An arithmetic sequence, $r=7$, number of terms $n=71-15+1=57$. $S=\frac{105+497}{2}\cdot57=301\cdot57$. $17\,157$
  4. medium
    The sum of the first $n$ terms of the sequence $(a_n)$ is $S_n=n^2+2n$. Find $a_n$ and show that the sequence is arithmetic.
    Solution
    $a_1=S_1=3$. For $n\ge2$: $a_n=S_n-S_{n-1}=n^2+2n-(n-1)^2-2(n-1)=2n+1$; the formula also works for $n=1$. $a_{n+1}-a_n=2$. $a_n=2n+1$, an arithmetic sequence with common difference $r=2$.
  5. exam-level
    In an arithmetic sequence $a_1+a_5=14$ and $a_2\cdot a_4=45$. Find $S_{10}$.
    Solution
    $a_1+a_5=2a_3$, so $a_3=7$. Then $a_2=7-r$, $a_4=7+r$, $49-r^2=45$, $r=\pm2$. For $r=2$: $a_1=3$, $S_{10}=\frac{6+18}{2}\cdot10=120$. For $r=-2$: $a_1=11$, $S_{10}=\frac{22-18}{2}\cdot10=20$. $S_{10}=120$ or $S_{10}=20$
  6. exam-level
    The first row of a hall has $18$ seats, and each next row has $2$ more than the previous one. The hall has $630$ seats in total. How many rows are there and how many seats in the last one?
    Solution
    $S_n=\frac{36+2(n-1)}{2}\cdot n=n(n+17)=630$, so $n^2+17n-630=0$, $\Delta=2809=53^2$, $n=\frac{-17+53}{2}=18$ (the other root is negative). $a_{18}=18+17\cdot2=52$. $18$ rows, $52$ seats in the last one.

6. On the exam

  • Two given terms ($a_k$, $a_m$): immediately $a_m-a_k=(m-k)r$. You do not need to set up a system with $a_1$.
  • Three numbers in an arithmetic sequence: conveniently $a-r,\ a,\ a+r$ (sum $3a$). With the condition $2y=x+z$, check the numbers you obtain.
  • Number of terms: from $a_k$ to $a_m$ there are $m-k+1$ terms, not $m-k$. This is the most common mistake in tasks like "the sum of numbers divisible by 7".
  • $S_n$ given by a formula: $a_n=S_n-S_{n-1}$ only for $n\ge2$; check $a_1=S_1$ separately.
  • The proof of the formula for $S_n$ is on the list of theorems from the curriculum: be able to write it (summing forward and backward).
