Polynomial equations and inequalities

Factor the polynomial and the rest is easy: a product is zero when one of its factors is zero, and the sign changes only at the roots, and only for odd multiplicity. Hence the “wavy curve”.

III.5, III.R.1, II.R.1, II.R.2, II.R.3, II.R.5

1. Factoring

MethodExample
factoring out a common factor$x^4-3x^3=x^3(x-3)$
grouping$x^3+2x^2-x-2=x^2(x+2)-(x+2)$ $=(x+2)(x-1)(x+1)$
special product formulas$x^3-1=(x-1)(x^2+x+1)$
integer root + divisionsee the theorem below
Theorem (integer roots) If a polynomial with integer coefficients and constant term $a_0\neq0$ has an integer root $k$, then $k$ is a divisor of $a_0$.
Why?

$W(k)=a_nk^n+\ldots+a_1k+a_0=0$, so $a_0=-k\,(a_nk^{n-1}+\ldots+a_1)$. The bracket is an integer, hence $k\mid a_0$.

If $W(k)=0$, then $W(x)=(x-k)\cdot Q(x)$ (the factor theorem, Bézout); you find $Q$ by dividing by $x-k$ (Horner's scheme). Rational roots $\frac{p}{q}$ are outside the core curriculum.

Definition $k$ is a root of multiplicity $r$ of the polynomial $W$ if $W(x)=(x-k)^r\cdot Q(x)$ and $Q(k)\neq0$.

2. Inequality: the wavy-curve method

  1. Move everything to one side and factor: $W(x)\;?\;0$.
  2. Mark the roots on the axis, with their multiplicities.
  3. Start on the right: above the axis if the leading coefficient is positive (below the axis if negative).
  4. Go left: at a root of odd multiplicity cross the axis, at even multiplicity bounce off it.
  5. Read off the set. For $\ge$, $\le$ include all the roots, also the “bounced” ones.
A factor with no roots (e.g. $x^2+x+1$, $\Delta<0$) has a constant sign: it does not change the wavy curve, it only flips it if it is negative.

3. Interactive: the wavy curve

Drag the roots along the axis and change their multiplicities. Notice: odd multiplicity means passing through the axis, even means bouncing. The graph is rescaled vertically, only the sign matters.

4. Interactive: where the sign of a product comes from

The same polynomial split into factors: each row is the sign of one factor (green $+$, red $-$). Drag the purple point at the bottom. The sign of $W(x)$ is “minus” when the number of negative factors is odd. A factor raised to an even power is never negative.

5. Worked example

Solve the inequality $x^3-2x^2-9x+18\ge0$.

  1. Grouping: $x^2(x-2)-9(x-2)=(x-2)(x^2-9)$ $=(x-2)(x-3)(x+3)$.
  2. Roots $-3,\,2,\,3$, all of multiplicity 1; $a=1>0$.
  3. Wavy curve from the top right: $+$ on $(3,\infty)$, $-$ on $(2,3)$, $+$ on $(-3,2)$, $-$ on $(-\infty,-3)$.

$x\in[-3,2]\cup[3,\infty)$

Solve the inequality $x^3-3x-2<0$.

  1. Candidates for integer roots: divisors of $-2$, that is $\pm1,\pm2$. $W(-1)=-1+3-2=0$.
  2. Dividing by $x+1$: $x^3-3x-2=(x+1)(x^2-x-2)$ $=(x+1)^2(x-2)$.
  3. $-1$ has multiplicity 2 (bounce), $2$ has multiplicity 1. Negative only to the left of $2$, but without $-1$.

$x\in(-\infty,-1)\cup(-1,2)$

6. Tasks

  1. easy
    Solve the equation $x(x-3)(2x+1)=0$.
    Solution
    A product is zero when one of its factors is zero. $x\in\left\{-\frac12,0,3\right\}$
  2. easy
    Solve the equation $x^3-5x^2+6x=0$.
    Solution
    $x(x^2-5x+6)=x(x-2)(x-3)=0$. $x\in\{0,2,3\}$ (do not divide by $x$!)
  3. medium
    Solve the inequality $x^3+2x^2-x-2<0$.
    Solution
    $(x+2)(x-1)(x+1)<0$; roots $-2,-1,1$, wavy curve from the top right. $x\in(-\infty,-2)\cup(-1,1)$
  4. medium
    Solve the inequality $(x-1)^2(x+2)^3(3-x)\ge0$.
    Solution
    The coefficient of $x^6$: $1\cdot1\cdot(-1)=-1<0$, so we start on the right below the axis. At $3$ we cross (multiplicity 1), at $1$ we bounce (2), at $-2$ we cross (3). Signs: $(3,\infty)$: $-$; $(1,3)$: $+$; $(-2,1)$: $+$; $(-\infty,-2)$: $-$. $x\in[-2,3]$
  5. exam-level
    Solve the inequality $2x^3-3x^2-11x+6>0$.
    Solution
    Divisors of $6$: $W(3)=54-27-33+6=0$. Dividing by $x-3$: $2x^3-3x^2-11x+6=(x-3)(2x^2+3x-2)$. For $2x^2+3x-2$: $\Delta=25$, $x=\frac12$, $x=-2$. $W(x)=2(x-3)\left(x-\frac12\right)(x+2)$. $x\in\left(-2,\frac12\right)\cup(3,\infty)$
  6. exam-level
    Solve the inequality $x^4+x^3\le x+1$.
    Solution
    $x^3(x+1)-(x+1)=(x+1)(x^3-1)$ $=(x+1)(x-1)(x^2+x+1)\le0$. The last factor has $\Delta=-3<0$, so it is always positive. What remains is $(x+1)(x-1)\le0$. $x\in[-1,1]$

7. On the exam

  • Do not divide by $x$ or by a factor containing $x$: you lose solutions. Factor out instead.
  • The sign of the leading coefficient comes from the product of the factors (e.g. $(3-x)$ gives $-1$). The start of the wavy curve depends on it.
  • Roots of even multiplicity belong to the set for $\ge$, $\le$, and must be thrown out for $>$.
  • Show the factorization and a sketch of the wavy curve with the roots marked: it is safer to show them, because they are usually scored separately.
