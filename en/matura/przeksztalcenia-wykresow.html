Graph transformations: f(x−a)+b, −f(x), f(−x)

A change outside $f$ moves the graph vertically, a change inside (next to $x$) moves it horizontally, and “the other way round”: $f(x-3)$ goes right. A minus outside reflects in $Ox$, a minus next to $x$ reflects in $Oy$.

V.12, V.R.1

Why is $f(x-a)$ a translation to the right?

Let $g(x)=f(x-a)$. Then $g(x_0+a)=f(x_0)$: the value that $f$ has at $x_0$, the function $g$ attains only at $x_0+a$, that is, $a$ units further to the right. Every point $(x_0,y_0)$ of the graph of $f$ goes to $(x_0+a,\,y_0)$. See the demo in section 3.

The order matters. $-f(x)+b$: first reflect, then translate. $f(-x+a)=f(-(x-a))$: first reflect in $Oy$, then translate by $[a,0]$ (not by $[-a,0]$!).

The graphs $y=|f(x)|$ and $y=f(|x|)$ are outside the core curriculum of 2024 (|f(x)| was removed from the requirements), but they are easy to understand as a reflection of part of the graph.

3. Why a “minus” shifts to the right

Drag a point on the blue graph of $f(x)=\frac12x^2$. The red graph is $g(x)=f(x-a)$. The same height appears on $g$ $a$ units further to the right.

4. Step-by-step example

Sketch $g(x)=-\sqrt{x+2}+3$ based on $f(x)=\sqrt x$. Give the domain, the range and the zero.

  1. $\sqrt{x+2}=f(x+2)$: translation by $[-2,0]$, the starting point at $(-2,0)$.
  2. $-\sqrt{x+2}$: reflection in $Ox$, the graph goes down.
  3. $+3$: translation by $[0,3]$, the starting point at $(-2,3)$.
  4. $D=[-2,\infty)$, $\text{range}=(-\infty,3]$. Zero: $\sqrt{x+2}=3\iff x=7$.

$D_g=[-2,\infty)$, $\;\operatorname{range} g=(-\infty,3]$, $\;x_0=7$

Check in the demo: $\sqrt x$, $a=-2$, $b=3$, tick $-f(x)$.

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    The graph of $f(x)=x^2$ was translated by the vector $[-3,2]$. Give the formula of the resulting function.
    Solution
    $f(x-(-3))+2$. $g(x)=(x+3)^2+2$
  2. easy
    A function $f$ has domain $[-2,5]$, range $[-1,4]$ and zeros $-1$ and $3$. Give the domain, the range and the zeros of the function $g(x)=f(x-3)$ and the range of $h(x)=-f(x)$.
    Solution
    $g$: everything shifted $3$ to the right, values unchanged. $h$: values with the opposite sign. $D_g=[1,8]$, $\operatorname{range} g=[-1,4]$, zeros of $g$: $2$ and $6$; $\;\operatorname{range} h=[-4,1]$
  3. medium
    Give the formulas of the functions whose graphs are symmetric to the graph of $f(x)=2^x$ with respect to the $Oy$ axis and with respect to the $Ox$ axis. Give their ranges.
    Solution
    In $Oy$: $f(-x)=2^{-x}=\left(\frac12\right)^x$, $\text{range}=(0,\infty)$. In $Ox$: $-f(x)=-2^x$, $\text{range}=(-\infty,0)$. $y=\left(\frac12\right)^x$, $\text{range}=(0,\infty)$; $\;y=-2^x$, $\text{range}=(-\infty,0)$
  4. medium
    For $f(x)=x^2-4x$ find the formula $g(x)=f(-x)$ and the interval in which $g$ is increasing.
    Solution
    $g(x)=(-x)^2-4(-x)=x^2+4x$, vertex $p=-2$ (the reflection of $p_f=2$), arms upward. $g(x)=x^2+4x$, increasing in $[-2,\infty)$
  5. exam-level
    The graph of $f(x)=\log_2 x$ was translated by the vector $[a,b]$, giving the graph of a function $g$ that has the asymptote $x=-1$ and passes through the point $A=(3,1)$. Find $a$, $b$ and the zero of $g$.
    Solution
    $g(x)=\log_2(x-a)+b$, the asymptote is $x=a$, so $a=-1$. $g(3)=\log_2 4+b=2+b=1$, so $b=-1$. $\log_2(x+1)=1\iff x+1=2$. $a=-1$, $b=-1$, zero $x=1$

6. On the exam

  • $f(x+3)$ is a translation to the left by $3$. Write the vector explicitly: $[-3,0]$.
  • When sketching, move the characteristic points (vertex, starting point, the point $(0,1)$ for $a^x$) and the asymptotes.
  • $-f(x)$: the zeros do not change. $f(-x)$: the point where the graph crosses $Oy$ does not change.
  • At the extended level the formula is sometimes “hidden”: $f(2-x)=f(-(x-2))$ is a reflection in $Oy$, then a translation by $[2,0]$.
