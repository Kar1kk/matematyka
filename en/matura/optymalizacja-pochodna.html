Optimization problems with derivatives

The same as optimization with a parabola, except that the objective function is cubic, rational or contains a root. There is no vertex, so you look for the maximum where the derivative changes sign.

XIII.R.6, XIII.R.5

1. The exam template

The typical solution (and marking) scheme has three stages: the model (function and domain), the derivative with the extremum, the answer. The stages are usually marked separately, so it is worth writing each of them down even if you do not reach the final result.

  1. Model: a variable $x$, the other quantities in terms of $x$, the objective function $f(x)$ and its domain (from geometric conditions, e.g. $x>0$, $12-2x>0$).
  2. Derivative: $f'(x)$ simplified to a product form and its zeros within the domain.
  3. Justification and answer: the sign of $f'$ (increasing, then decreasing), so at $x_0$ there is the largest value. Compute it and answer the question in the text.
“$f'(x_0)=0$, so a maximum” is not enough: the justification is missing. Write: “$f'>0$ on $(0,x_0)$ and $f'<0$ on $(x_0,b)$, so $f$ increases on $(0,x_0]$ and decreases on $[x_0,b)$, hence $f(x_0)$ is the largest value”.

2. Interactive: a box from cardboard

From a $12\times12$ square you cut squares of side $x$ out of the corners and fold up the sides. Small $x$: a flat box. Large $x$: tall but narrow. Somewhere in between the volume is the largest.

3. Interactive: a can of fixed volume

The can has volume $V=16\pi$. Change the radius $r$: the height adjusts by itself ($h=\frac{16}{r^2}$). The can that uses the least sheet metal has height equal to its diameter.

4. Step-by-step example

From a square piece of cardboard of side $12$ we cut squares of side $x$ out of the corners and fold an open-topped box. For which $x$ is the volume the largest?

  1. Model: the base is $(12-2x)\times(12-2x)$, the height is $x$. $V(x)=x(12-2x)^2$, domain $x\in(0,6)$.
  2. Derivative (product and composition): $$V'(x)=(12-2x)^2-4x(12-2x)$$ $$=(12-2x)(12-6x)$$ In the domain $12-2x>0$, so $V'(x)=0\iff x=2$.
  3. Sign: $12-6x>0$ for $x<2$, $<0$ for $x>2$. $V$ increases on $(0,2]$, decreases on $[2,6)$, so $V(2)$ is the largest: $V(2)=2\cdot8^2=128$.

$x=2$, the largest volume $128$.

A trapezoid is inscribed in a semicircle of radius $2$ so that its longer base is the diameter. Find the largest area of the trapezoid.

  1. Model: the shorter base is $2x$ (endpoints on the circle), the height is $\sqrt{4-x^2}$, domain $x\in(0,2)$. $$P(x)=(2+x)\sqrt{4-x^2}$$
  2. Derivative: $$P'(x)=\sqrt{4-x^2}-\frac{x(2+x)}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}$$ $$=\frac{-2(x+2)(x-1)}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}$$ In the domain $P'(x)=0\iff x=1$.
  3. Sign: $P'>0$ on $(0,1)$, $P'<0$ on $(1,2)$, so the maximum is at $x=1$: $P(1)=3\sqrt3$.

The largest area is $3\sqrt3$ (the shorter base is $2$, equal to the radius: half of a regular hexagon).

5. Tasks

  1. medium
    Positive numbers $x$, $y$ satisfy $x+y=12$. For which $x$, $y$ is the product $x\cdot y^2$ the largest?
    Solution
    $f(y)=(12-y)y^2=12y^2-y^3$, $y\in(0,12)$. $f'(y)=24y-3y^2=3y(8-y)$: $+$ on $(0,8)$, $-$ on $(8,12)$. $y=8$, $x=4$, product $256$.
  2. medium
    A rectangle has two vertices on the $Ox$ axis and two on the parabola $y=12-x^2$ (above the axis). Find the largest area of the rectangle.
    Solution
    The vertices are $(\pm x,0)$, $(\pm x,12-x^2)$, $x\in(0,2\sqrt3)$. $P(x)=2x(12-x^2)=24x-2x^3$, $P'(x)=24-6x^2=6(2-x)(2+x)$: $+$ on $(0,2)$, $-$ afterwards. $P(2)=32$
  3. medium
    An open box (no lid) with a square base has volume $32\ \text{dm}^3$. What dimensions minimize the amount of material used?
    Solution
    The base is $a$, the height is $h=\frac{32}{a^2}$. $P(a)=a^2+4ah=a^2+\frac{128}{a}$, $a>0$. $$P'(a)=2a-\frac{128}{a^2}=\frac{2(a^3-64)}{a^2}$$ $-$ on $(0,4)$, $+$ on $(4,\infty)$: the minimum is at $a=4$. $a=4$ dm, $h=2$ dm, area $48\ \text{dm}^2$.
  4. exam-level
    A cylinder is inscribed in a sphere of radius $\sqrt3$. Find the largest volume of the cylinder.
    Solution
    Height $h$, radius $r$: $r^2+\left(\frac h2\right)^2=3$, $h\in(0,2\sqrt3)$. $$V(h)=\pi\left(3-\tfrac{h^2}{4}\right)h$$ $V'(h)=\pi\left(3-\frac{3h^2}{4}\right)=\frac{3\pi}{4}(2-h)(2+h)$: $+$ on $(0,2)$, $-$ on $(2,2\sqrt3)$. For $h=2$: $r^2=2$. $V_{\max}=4\pi$
  5. exam-level
    The slant height of a cone is $3\sqrt3$. What height gives the largest volume? Find that volume.
    Solution
    $r^2=27-h^2$, $h\in(0,3\sqrt3)$. $$V(h)=\tfrac{\pi}{3}(27h-h^3)$$ $V'(h)=\frac\pi3(27-3h^2)=\pi(3-h)(3+h)$: $+$ on $(0,3)$, $-$ afterwards. $h=3$, $r^2=18$. $h=3$, $V_{\max}=18\pi$

6. On the exam

  • The domain is a separate point. It comes from the geometry: positive lengths, a point on a circle, $h<2R$, etc.
  • Reduce the derivative to a product. Then you can read the sign at once (a denominator such as $a^2$ or $\sqrt{\ }$ is positive).
  • Justifying a maximum/minimum: monotonicity on the whole domain, not just $f'=0$.
  • Answer the question: if they ask for the dimensions, give all the dimensions; if for the volume, compute it.
  • With a root you may optimize the square (e.g. $d^2$ instead of $d$), but you must say so and justify it (the root is increasing).
