Line and circle, two circles, tangent to a circle

Common points are the solutions of a system of equations: substitute the line into the circle and you get a quadratic equation, and $\Delta$ tells you how many points there are. For two circles it is enough to compare the distance between the centers with $r_1+r_2$ and $|r_1-r_2|$.

$d$ is the distance from the center $S$ to the line, i.e. $|SF|$, where $F$ is the projection of $S$ onto the line (perpendicular line + system). Faster: for the line $Ax+By+C=0$ and center $S=(x_0,y_0)$ $$d=\frac{|Ax_0+By_0+C|}{\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}$$ (the formula is on the CKE formula sheet, but it is not in the core curriculum requirements). The projection method is an alternative.

2. Tangent to a circle

Theorem A tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency.
Tangent at the point $P=(x_P,y_P)$ of the circle with center $S=(a,b)$
$$\vec{SP}=[x_P-a,\ y_P-b]$$ $$(x_P-a)(x-x_P)+(y_P-b)(y-y_P)=0$$
in plain words
the line through $P$ perpendicular to $SP$; in slope form $a_{\text{tan}}=-\frac{1}{a_{SP}}$

4. Worked example

Find the common points of the line $y=x+1$ and the circle $(x-2)^2+(y-1)^2=10$, and the tangent to the circle at the point with positive $x$-coordinate.

  1. Substitute $y=x+1$: $(x-2)^2+x^2=10$, so $2x^2-4x-6=0$, $x^2-2x-3=0$.
  2. $x=3$ or $x=-1$, so the points are $(3,4)$ and $(-1,0)$.
  3. Tangent at $P=(3,4)$: $\vec{SP}=[1,3]$, so $1\cdot(x-3)+3(y-4)=0$.
  4. Simplify: $x+3y-15=0$, i.e. $y=-\frac13x+5$. Check: $a_{SP}=3$, $3\cdot\left(-\frac13\right)=-1$ ✓.

$(3,4)$, $(-1,0)$; tangent $x+3y-15=0$

5. Exercises

  1. easy
    Determine the relative position of the circles $(x-1)^2+(y+2)^2=4$ and $(x-4)^2+(y-2)^2=9$. If they are tangent, find the point of tangency.
    Solution
    $d=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5=2+3=r_1+r_2$: externally tangent. The point of tangency lies on $S_1S_2$ at distance $r_1=2$ from $S_1$: $T=S_1+\frac25\vec{S_1S_2}=(1,-2)+\frac25[3,4]$. Externally tangent at $T=\left(\frac{11}{5},-\frac25\right)$
  2. medium
    For which $m$ does the line $y=x+m$ have two common points with the circle $x^2+y^2=8$?
    Solution
    $x^2+(x+m)^2=8$, $2x^2+2mx+m^2-8=0$, $\Delta=4m^2-8(m^2-8)=64-4m^2>0$, i.e. $m^2<16$. $m\in(-4,4)$
  3. medium
    Find the tangent to the circle $(x+1)^2+(y-3)^2=25$ at the point $P=(2,7)$.
    Solution
    $P$ lies on the circle: $9+16=25$ ✓. $\vec{SP}=[3,4]$, tangent: $3(x-2)+4(y-7)=0$. $3x+4y-34=0$
  4. exam-level
    From the point $Q=(0,-5)$ tangents are drawn to the circle $x^2+y^2=5$. Find their equations, the points of tangency and the length of the tangent segment.
    Solution
    The vertical line $x=0$ meets the circle at two points, so it is not tangent. $y=mx-5$: $(1+m^2)x^2-10mx+20=0$, $\Delta=100m^2-80(1+m^2)=20m^2-80=0$, $m=\pm2$. Then $x=\frac{10m}{2(1+m^2)}=m$, the points are $(2,-1)$ and $(-2,-1)$. $|QT|=\sqrt{25-5}=2\sqrt5$. $y=2x-5$, $y=-2x-5$; $T_1=(2,-1)$, $T_2=(-2,-1)$; $|QT|=2\sqrt5$
  5. exam-level
    Find the common points of the circles $x^2+y^2=25$ and $(x-7)^2+(y-1)^2=25$.
    Solution
    Expand the second: $x^2+y^2-14x-2y+50=25$. Subtract it from the first: $14x+2y-50=0$, $y=25-7x$. Substitute: $x^2+(25-7x)^2=25$, $50x^2-350x+600=0$, $x^2-7x+12=0$, $x\in\{3,4\}$. Check $(4,-3)$ in the second: $9+16=25$ ✓. $(3,4)$ and $(4,-3)$
  6. exam-level
    For which $r>0$ are the circles $x^2+y^2=4$ and $(x-3)^2+(y-4)^2=r^2$ tangent?
    Solution
    $d=5$. Externally: $r+2=5$, $r=3$. Internally: $|r-2|=5$, $r=7$ (or $r=-3$, which we reject). $r=3$ or $r=7$

6. On the exam

  • Tangent through an external point: writing $y=m(x-x_Q)+y_Q$ loses the vertical line. Always check $x=x_Q$ separately (sometimes it is one of the tangents).
  • "Tangent circles" with no qualifier means both cases: external and internal. Reject a negative radius.
  • In problems with a parameter, write the condition on $\Delta$ explicitly and solve the inequality to the end (an interval, not "$m\ne\dots$").
  • Tangent at a known point: do not compute $\Delta$, just use perpendicularity to the radius. Faster and with no risk of error.
