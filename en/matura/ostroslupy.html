Pyramids

Everything revolves around the height $H$ and its foot $O$. From $H$ and a segment in the base ($R$ or $r$) you build two right triangles: one gives the lateral edge, the other the height of a lateral face.

X.3, X.5

1. The foot of the height and two triangles

A regular pyramid has a regular polygon as its base, and the foot of the height at the center of that base. A regular tetrahedron: all $6$ edges equal.
Where is the foot of the height?
  • All lateral edges equal (or equally inclined to the base) $\Rightarrow$ $O$ is the center of the circumscribed circle of the base.
  • All lateral faces equally inclined to the base (foot inside the base) $\Rightarrow$ $O$ is the center of the inscribed circle.
Why?
The triangles $SOA_i$ are right triangles with a common leg $SO=H$. Equal hypotenuses $|SA_i|$ (or equal angles at $A_i$) give equal $|OA_i|$, so $O$ is equidistant from the vertices. For the faces: by the three perpendiculars theorem, the feet of the altitudes of the faces project onto segments $OM_i\perp$ the edge, and equal angles give equal $|OM_i|$, so $O$ is equidistant from the sides.

3. Step-by-step example

A regular quadrilateral pyramid has a base edge of $6$, and a lateral face is inclined to the base at an angle of $60^\circ$. Find $V$ and $P_c$.

  1. Triangle $SOM$: $r=|OM|=3$, the angle at $M$ is $60^\circ$.
  2. $H=3\tan60^\circ=3\sqrt3$, $\;h_b=\frac{3}{\cos60^\circ}=6$.
  3. $V=\frac13\cdot36\cdot3\sqrt3=36\sqrt3$, $\;P_c=36+4\cdot\frac12\cdot6\cdot6=36+72$.

$V=36\sqrt3$, $P_c=108$

4. Tasks

  1. easy
    A regular quadrilateral pyramid has a base edge of $8$ and a height of $3$. Find $V$ and $P_c$.
    Solution
    $V=\frac13\cdot64\cdot3=64$. $r=4$, $h_b=\sqrt{9+16}=5$, $P_c=64+4\cdot\frac12\cdot8\cdot5$. $V=64$, $P_c=144$
  2. medium
    Find the height and the volume of a regular tetrahedron with edge $6$.
    Solution
    The foot is the center of an equilateral triangle: $R=\frac{6\sqrt3}{3}=2\sqrt3$. $H=\sqrt{36-12}=\sqrt{24}=2\sqrt6$. $V=\frac13\cdot\frac{36\sqrt3}{4}\cdot2\sqrt6=6\sqrt{18}$. $H=2\sqrt6$, $V=18\sqrt2$ (in general $H=\frac{a\sqrt6}{3}$, $V=\frac{a^3\sqrt2}{12}$).
  3. medium
    In a regular triangular pyramid the base edge has length $6$, and a lateral edge forms an angle of $45^\circ$ with the base. Find the volume.
    Solution
    $R=2\sqrt3$, the angle is $45^\circ$, so $H=R=2\sqrt3$. $P_p=9\sqrt3$, $V=\frac13\cdot9\sqrt3\cdot2\sqrt3$. $V=18$
  4. exam-level
    A regular hexagonal pyramid has a lateral edge of $10$ and a height of $8$. Find the volume and the lateral surface area.
    Solution
    $R=\sqrt{100-64}=6$, and in a regular hexagon $R=a$, so $a=6$. $P_p=\frac{3\cdot36\sqrt3}{2}=54\sqrt3$, $V=\frac13\cdot54\sqrt3\cdot8=144\sqrt3$. The height of a face from the triangle $b$, $\frac a2$: $h_b=\sqrt{100-9}=\sqrt{91}$, $P_b=6\cdot\frac12\cdot6\sqrt{91}$. $V=144\sqrt3$, $P_b=18\sqrt{91}$
  5. exam-level
    The base of a pyramid is a right triangle with legs $6$ and $8$. a) Each lateral edge has length $13$. b) Each lateral face is inclined to the base at an angle of $60^\circ$. Find the volume in each case.
    Solution
    $P_p=24$, the hypotenuse is $10$. a) The foot = the center of the circumscribed circle = the midpoint of the hypotenuse, $R=5$, $H=\sqrt{169-25}=12$, $V=\frac13\cdot24\cdot12=96$. b) The foot = the center of the inscribed circle, $r=\frac{6+8-10}{2}=2$, $H=2\tan60^\circ=2\sqrt3$, $V=\frac13\cdot24\cdot2\sqrt3$. a) $96$, b) $16\sqrt3$

5. On the exam

  • Always justify the position of the foot of the height (regular, equal edges, equal face angles).
  • Do not confuse $h_b$ (height of a face, used for $P_b$) with $H$ (height of the solid, used for $V$), nor $\alpha$ (edge) with $\beta$ (face).
  • The angle between a face and the base: arms perpendicular to the base edge, i.e. $OM$ and $SM$, where $M$ is the midpoint of the edge (in a regular one).
  • Do not forget the $\frac13$ in the volume.
