Special points of a triangle, the angle bisector theorem

Four “centers” of a triangle: equally far from the vertices (circumcircle), equally far from the sides (incircle), the intersection of the altitudes, and the centroid. The fact that three lines always meet at one point is not obvious at all – and you must be able to prove it.

3. Why do these lines meet?

The perpendicular bisectors of the sides meet at one point
Proof (required on the exam)

The perpendicular bisector of a segment is the set of points equally distant from its endpoints.

  1. The perpendicular bisectors of $AB$ and $BC$ are not parallel (because $AB$ and $BC$ are not), so they meet at a point $O$.
  2. $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$: $|OA|=|OB|$. $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$: $|OB|=|OC|$.
  3. Hence $|OA|=|OC|$, so $O$ also lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AC$. $\blacksquare$

Incidentally: $O$ is the center of the circumcircle. In the same way (an angle bisector = the points equally distant from the arms of the angle) one proves that the angle bisectors meet at one point.

Corollary: the lines containing the altitudes meet at one point
Proof (required on the exam)

Through each vertex of triangle $ABC$ draw a line parallel to the opposite side. This produces a triangle $A'B'C'$ ($A'$ opposite $A$, etc.).

  1. $ABA'C$ and $AC'BC$ are parallelograms (opposite sides parallel), so $|BA'|=|AC|=|BC'|$. Hence $B$ is the midpoint of $A'C'$.
  2. The altitude from $B$ is perpendicular to $AC$, hence also to $A'C'\parallel AC$, and passes through the midpoint $B$ of the segment $A'C'$ – so it is its perpendicular bisector.
  3. Likewise the other altitudes are perpendicular bisectors of the sides of $A'B'C'$. The perpendicular bisectors meet at one point, so the altitudes do too. $\blacksquare$
The medians meet at one point and divide each other in the ratio $2:1$
Proof (required on the exam)

$K$ – midpoint of $BC$, $L$ – midpoint of $AC$; the medians $AK$ and $BL$ meet at $G$.

  1. $LK$ is a midsegment: $LK\parallel AB$, $|LK|=\frac12|AB|$ (proof from Thales).
  2. $\triangle GAB\sim\triangle GKL$ (AAA: vertical angles at $G$ and alternate angles at the parallel lines $AB$, $KL$) with scale $\frac{|AB|}{|KL|}=2$. Hence $|AG|=2|GK|$ and $|BG|=2|GL|$.
  3. So the median $BL$ meets $AK$ at a point dividing $AK$ in the ratio $2:1$ from $A$. The same argument for the medians $AK$ and $CM$ ($M$ – midpoint of $AB$) shows that $CM$ meets $AK$ at a point dividing $AK$ in the ratio $2:1$ from $A$, that is, at the same point $G$. So all three medians pass through $G$. $\blacksquare$

4. The angle bisector theorem

The angle bisector theorem on CKE sheet If $CD$ is the bisector of angle $ACB$ in triangle $ABC$ and $D$ lies on side $AB$, then $$\frac{|AD|}{|BD|}=\frac{|AC|}{|BC|}$$ The bisector divides the opposite side in the ratio of the adjacent sides.
Proof (required on the exam)

Compare the areas of triangles $ACD$ and $BCD$ in two ways. Let $|\angle ACD|=|\angle DCB|=\varphi$.

  1. Common altitude from $C$ to line $AB$: $\frac{P_{ACD}}{P_{BCD}}=\frac{|AD|}{|BD|}$.
  2. Formula $P=\frac12ab\sin\gamma$: $\frac{P_{ACD}}{P_{BCD}}=\frac{\frac12|AC|\cdot|CD|\sin\varphi}{\frac12|BC|\cdot|CD|\sin\varphi}=\frac{|AC|}{|BC|}$.

The left-hand sides are equal, so the right-hand sides are too. $\blacksquare$

Drag the vertices. The bisector of angle $C$ always divides $AB$ in the ratio $|AC|:|BC|$. The green and purple triangles have areas in the same ratio – that is the whole proof.

5. Step-by-step example

A triangle has sides $13$, $14$, $15$. Find the radii of the incircle and circumcircle.

  1. $p=21$, Heron's formula: $P=\sqrt{21\cdot8\cdot7\cdot6}=\sqrt{7056}=84$.
  2. $r=\frac{P}{p}=\frac{84}{21}=4$.
  3. $R=\frac{abc}{4P}=\frac{13\cdot14\cdot15}{336}=\frac{2730}{336}=\frac{65}{8}$.

$r=4$, $R=\frac{65}{8}=8.125$

6. Tasks

  1. easy
    In triangle $ABC$: $|AC|=8$, $|BC|=12$, $|AB|=15$. The bisector of angle $C$ meets $AB$ at $D$. Find $|AD|$ and $|BD|$.
    Solution
    $|AD|:|BD|=8:12=2:3$, so $|AD|=\frac25\cdot15$. $|AD|=6$, $|BD|=9$
  2. easy
    Find the radii of the incircle and circumcircle of an equilateral triangle with side $6$.
    Solution
    $h=3\sqrt3$, $r=\frac13h$, $R=\frac23h$. $r=\sqrt3$, $R=2\sqrt3$
  3. medium
    The legs of a right triangle have lengths $6$ and $8$. Find $R$, $r$, and the distance from the centroid to the right-angle vertex.
    Solution
    $c=10$, $R=\frac c2=5$, $r=\frac{6+8-10}{2}=2$. The median from the right angle goes to the midpoint of the hypotenuse (the circumcenter), so its length is $R=5$; $G$ divides it $2:1$. $R=5$, $r=2$, $|CG|=\frac{10}{3}$
  4. medium
    An isosceles triangle has base $10$ and legs $13$. Find $r$ and $R$.
    Solution
    $h=\sqrt{169-25}=12$, $P=60$, $p=18$. $r=\frac{60}{18}=\frac{10}{3}$, $R=\frac{10\cdot13\cdot13}{4\cdot60}=\frac{1690}{240}$. $r=\frac{10}{3}$, $R=\frac{169}{24}$
  5. exam-level
    The medians $AK$ and $BL$ of triangle $ABC$ are perpendicular, $|AK|=9$, $|BL|=12$. Find $|AB|$.
    Solution
    $G$ divides the medians $2:1$: $|AG|=6$, $|BG|=8$, and angle $AGB$ is right. $|AB|=\sqrt{36+64}$. $|AB|=10$
  6. exam-level
    Prove that if the centroid of a triangle coincides with the incenter, then the triangle is equilateral.
    Solution
    Line $CG$ contains the median from $C$, and line $CI$ the bisector of angle $C$. Since $G=I$, the median and the bisector from $C$ reach the same point $D$ of side $AB$. By the angle bisector theorem $\frac{|AC|}{|BC|}=\frac{|AD|}{|BD|}=1$, so $|AC|=|BC|$. In the same way from vertex $A$: $|AB|=|AC|$. The triangle is equilateral.

7. On the exam

  • Do not mix up the points: the circumcircle – perpendicular bisectors, the incircle – angle bisectors. This is the most common mistake.
  • Medians are divided $2:1$ counting from the vertex; in an equilateral triangle this gives $r=\frac13h$, $R=\frac23h$.
  • Angle bisector theorem: the ratio of the segments on the side = the ratio of the adjacent sides (the ones next to those segments).
  • When a task involves $P$, $r$, $R$ – write down all area formulas from the formula sheet and combine them: $P=pr=\frac{abc}{4R}$.
