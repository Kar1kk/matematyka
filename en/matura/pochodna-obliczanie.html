Computing derivatives (product, quotient, composition)

You compute a derivative from the definition once, to understand it. After that you use a few rules: power, sum, product, quotient, composition. Every derivative on the exam is a combination of them.

Quotient: in the numerator there is a minus, and the order matters ($f'g$ first). For $x^r$ with non-integer $r$ we assume $x>0$ (e.g. $(x^{\sqrt2})'=\sqrt2\,x^{\sqrt2-1}$).
Composition: “outer times inner”
$$\left[(2x+1)^5\right]'=5(2x+1)^4\cdot2$$ $$\left[\sqrt{u(x)}\right]'=\frac{u'(x)}{2\sqrt{u(x)}}$$
$f$
the outer function (power, root), evaluated at $g(x)$
$g'$
the derivative of the inside: this multiplication is the one most often forgotten
Where does the product rule come from?
$$f(x+h)g(x+h)-f(x)g(x)$$ $$=\big[f(x+h)-f(x)\big]g(x+h)$$ $$+f(x)\big[g(x+h)-g(x)\big]$$

We divide by $h$ and pass to the limit: $g(x+h)\to g(x)$ (because $g$ is differentiable, hence continuous). What remains is $f'g+fg'$. The demo in section 3 shows it geometrically.

Drag a point along the graph of $f$ (or ▶). The slope of the red tangent is the height of the point on the lower graph. The trace of these points is the graph of $f'$. Where the tangent is horizontal, $f'$ crosses the axis.

3. Interactive: why $(fg)'=f'g+fg'$

The product $f\cdot g$ is the area of a rectangle with sides $f(x)=x$ and $g(x)=\frac{x^2}{3}$. When $x$ grows by $\Delta x$, the area grows by two strips (red $\Delta f\cdot g$, green $f\cdot\Delta g$) and the corner $\Delta f\cdot\Delta g$. As $\Delta x\to0$ the corner vanishes faster than the strips.

4. Step-by-step example

Compute the derivative of $f(x)=\frac{2x-1}{x^2+1}$.

  1. Quotient: numerator $u=2x-1$, $u'=2$; denominator $w=x^2+1$, $w'=2x$.
  2. $f'(x)=\frac{2(x^2+1)-(2x-1)\cdot2x}{(x^2+1)^2}$.
  3. Numerator: $2x^2+2-4x^2+2x=-2x^2+2x+2$.

$f'(x)=\frac{-2x^2+2x+2}{(x^2+1)^2}$

Compute the derivatives of $g(x)=\sqrt{x^2+9}$ and $h(x)=x\sqrt x$.

  1. $g$: outer $\sqrt{\ }$, inner $x^2+9$: $g'(x)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x^2+9}}\cdot2x=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+9}}$. E.g. $g'(4)=\frac45$.
  2. $h$: instead of a product write a power $h(x)=x^{3/2}$, so $h'(x)=\frac32x^{1/2}$.

$g'(x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+9}}$, $\;h'(x)=\frac32\sqrt x$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Compute the derivative of $f(x)=4x^3-5x^2+7x-2$.
    Solution
    $f'(x)=12x^2-10x+7$
  2. easy
    Compute the derivative of $f(x)=4\sqrt x+\frac2x$, $x>0$.
    Solution
    $4\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt x}+2\cdot\left(-\frac1{x^2}\right)$. $f'(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt x}-\frac{2}{x^2}$
  3. medium
    Compute the derivative of $f(x)=(x^2+1)(3x-2)$ in two ways: with the product rule and after expanding.
    Solution
    Product: $2x(3x-2)+3(x^2+1)=9x^2-4x+3$. After expanding: $(3x^3-2x^2+3x-2)'=9x^2-4x+3$. $f'(x)=9x^2-4x+3$
  4. medium
    Compute $f'(0)$ for $f(x)=\frac{x^2-3}{x+2}$.
    Solution
    $$f'(x)=\frac{2x(x+2)-(x^2-3)}{(x+2)^2}$$ $$=\frac{x^2+4x+3}{(x+2)^2}$$ $f'(0)=\frac34$
  5. medium
    Compute the derivative of $f(x)=(2x-1)^5$.
    Solution
    Composition: $5(2x-1)^4\cdot(2x-1)'$. $f'(x)=10(2x-1)^4$
  6. exam-level
    Compute the derivative of $f(x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}$ and write it in the simplest form.
    Solution
    Quotient and composition (in the denominator $\left(\sqrt{x^2+1}\right)'=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}$): $$f'(x)=\frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}-x\cdot\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}}{x^2+1}$$ Multiply the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{x^2+1}$: the numerator is $x^2+1-x^2=1$. $f'(x)=\frac{1}{(x^2+1)\sqrt{x^2+1}}$, e.g. $f'(0)=1$.

6. On the exam

  • Before computing, simplify: $\frac{x^2+1}{x}=x+\frac1x$, $x\sqrt x=x^{3/2}$. Less calculation, fewer errors.
  • Quotient: do not expand the denominator $(x^2+1)^2$. Leave it, because you only need the sign of the derivative, and the denominator is positive.
  • Composition: always multiply by the derivative of the inside (e.g. $\cdot2$ in $(2x-1)^5$).
  • The derivatives of $\sin x$, $a^x$, $\ln x$ are not required here. On the exam, powers, roots and rational functions are enough.
