Operations on polynomials

A polynomial is a list of coefficients of consecutive powers of $x$. Adding means adding the lists “position by position”, multiplying means “everything by everything”, and two polynomials are equal exactly when they have the same lists.

II.2, II.3

1. Concepts

Definition A polynomial of degree $n$ in the variable $x$ is $$W(x)=a_nx^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\ldots+a_1x+a_0,$$ where $a_n\neq0$. The numbers $a_n,\ldots,a_0$ are the coefficients, $a_n$ is the coefficient of the highest power, and $a_0$ is the constant term. The zero polynomial $W(x)=0$ has no defined degree.
Constant term
$$W(0)=a_0$$
Sum of coefficients
$$W(1)=a_n+\ldots+a_1+a_0$$

A polynomial in several variables, e.g. $3x^2y-xy^3+5$, consists of monomials. Like terms differ only in the coefficient ($2x^2y$ and $-7x^2y$); only those may be added.

2. Adding, multiplying, factoring out

  • Adding / subtracting: you combine like terms. When subtracting, put the whole subtrahend in brackets: $P-Q=P+(-1)\cdot Q$.
  • Multiplying: each term of the first bracket by each term of the second, then combine (demo below).
  • Factoring out: you factor out the GCD of the coefficients and each variable in the lowest power in which it occurs: $$6x^3y-9x^2y^2=3x^2y(2x-3y)$$
Degrees For nonzero $P$, $Q$: $$\deg(P\cdot Q)=\deg P+\deg Q$$ $$\deg(P+Q)\le\max(\deg P,\ \deg Q)$$ For a sum it can be smaller, because the leading terms may cancel: $(x^2+x)+(-x^2+1)=x+1$.

3. Equality of polynomials

Theorem Two polynomials are equal (have equal values for every $x\in\R$) if and only if they have the same degree and equal coefficients at the same powers.

Hence the method of comparing coefficients: when a task has unknowns $a$, $b$, …, expand one side, arrange by powers and equate the coefficients. You get a system of equations. See the example below.

Equality of polynomials is an identity (for every $x$), not an equation to solve. A missing power has coefficient $0$ and also gives an equation.

6. Step-by-step example

Find $a$ and $b$ such that $(x^2+ax+2)(x-b)=x^3-x^2-4x+4$ for every $x$.

  1. Expand the left side and arrange it: $x^3+(a-b)x^2+(2-ab)x-2b$.
  2. Compare the coefficients: $a-b=-1$, $\;2-ab=-4$, $\;-2b=4$.
  3. From the last one $b=-2$, from the first one $a=-3$.
  4. Check the middle equation: $2-(-3)(-2)=-4$. It holds. Without this check, a system of three equations with two unknowns could be inconsistent.

$a=-3$, $b=-2$, i.e. $x^3-x^2-4x+4=(x^2-3x+2)(x+2)$

Two variables: $(2x-y)(x+3y)$.

  1. $2x\cdot x+2x\cdot3y-y\cdot x-y\cdot3y=2x^2+6xy-xy-3y^2$.

$2x^2+5xy-3y^2$

7. Tasks

  1. easy
    $P(x)=2x^3-x+1$, $Q(x)=x^2+3x$. Compute $P(x)-2Q(x)$ and give the degree of the polynomial $P\cdot Q$.
    Solution
    $2x^3-x+1-2x^2-6x$. $P-2Q=2x^3-2x^2-7x+1$, $\;\deg(P\cdot Q)=3+2=5$
  2. easy
    Factor out as much as possible: $12a^3b^2-18a^2b^3+6a^2b$.
    Solution
    GCD$(12,18,6)=6$, lowest powers: $a^2$, $b^1$. $6a^2b(2ab-3b^2+1)$
  3. medium
    Find the sum of coefficients and the constant term of the polynomial $W(x)=(2x-1)^5(x+3)^2$ (without expanding).
    Solution
    The sum of coefficients is $W(1)=1^5\cdot4^2$, the constant term is $W(0)=(-1)^5\cdot3^2$. Sum $16$, constant term $-9$.
  4. medium
    For which $a$, $b$ are the polynomials $W(x)=x^3+(a+1)x^2+bx$ and $P(x)=x^3+3x^2+(a-b)x$ equal?
    Solution
    At $x^2$: $a+1=3$, at $x$: $b=a-b$. Hence $a=2$, $2b=2$. $a=2$, $b=1$
  5. exam-level
    Find $a$ and $b$ for which the polynomial $x^4+2x^3+ax^2+bx+1$ is the square of a trinomial $x^2+px+q$.
    Solution
    $(x^2+px+q)^2=x^4+2px^3+(p^2+2q)x^2+2pqx+q^2$. Comparison: $2p=2$, $q^2=1$, so $p=1$, $q=\pm1$.
    $q=1$: $a=p^2+2q=3$, $b=2pq=2$. $\;q=-1$: $a=-1$, $b=-2$. $(a,b)=(3,2)$ or $(a,b)=(-1,-2)$
  6. exam-level
    Give the degree of the polynomial $W(x)=(m^2-4)x^3+(m+2)x^2-x$ depending on the parameter $m$.
    Solution
    For $m\ne\pm2$ the coefficient of $x^3$ is nonzero. For $m=2$: $W(x)=4x^2-x$. For $m=-2$: $W(x)=-x$. Degree $3$ for $m\ne\pm2$, degree $2$ for $m=2$, degree $1$ for $m=-2$.

8. On the exam

  • Comparing coefficients: write the equations for all powers, including those with coefficient $0$. When there are more equations than unknowns, check the remaining ones.
  • Degree depending on a parameter: first examine when the coefficient of the highest power vanishes.
  • Quick tricks: $W(0)$ is the constant term, $W(1)$ is the sum of coefficients, $W(-1)$ is the difference between the sums at even and odd powers.
  • A minus before a bracket changes the sign of every term of the polynomial being subtracted.
