Equations and inequalities with a parameter

The parameter $m$ is a knob: every setting gives a different equation, i.e. a different parabola. The question “for which $m$…” always has an answer in the form of a set on the $m$-axis, assembled from a few simple conditions.

III.R.5

1. Solution outline

  1. Does the coefficient of $x^2$ depend on $m$? If so, treat the case $a=0$ separately: a linear equation. Check whether the condition of the task holds then.
  2. Case $a\neq0$: translate the statement into the conditions from the table below ($\Delta$, Vieta's formulas, $f(k)$, vertex).
  3. Solve each condition separately: you get sets on the $m$-axis.
  4. Take the intersection of the conditions ($\cap$) and add ($\cup$) the result from the case $a=0$.

For “two distinct” replace $\Delta\ge0$ with $\Delta>0$. The condition “both $>k$” can also be written using Vieta (when $\Delta\ge0$): $(x_1-k)+(x_2-k)>0$ and $(x_1-k)(x_2-k)>0$, i.e. $s-2k>0$ and $p-ks+k^2>0$.
Where does $a\cdot f(k)<0$ come from?

$a\cdot f(k)=a^2(k-x_1)(k-x_2)$. Since $a^2>0$, the sign depends on $(k-x_1)(k-x_2)$: it is negative exactly when $k$ is between the roots. If there are no roots, $a\cdot f(k)>0$ for every $k$, so the condition by itself forces $\Delta>0$.

3. Linear equations with a parameter

The form $A(m)\cdot x=B(m)$. Consider when $A(m)=0$:

$(m^2-1)x=m+1$

  1. $m=1$: $0\cdot x=2$, contradiction: $\varnothing$.
  2. $m=-1$: $0\cdot x=0$, an identity: $\R$.
  3. $m\neq\pm1$: $x=\frac{m+1}{(m-1)(m+1)}=\frac{1}{m-1}$.

For the inequality $A(m)\cdot x>B(m)$: treat $A>0$ (sign unchanged), $A<0$ (sign reversed) and $A=0$ separately.

The family $f(x)=x^2-2mx+m+2$. Drag the endpoints $k_1$, $k_2$ (green) and move $m$. Try to find an $m$ for which $f(k_1)>0$, $f(k_2)>0$ and $\Delta>0$, and yet the roots lie outside the interval: this is why the condition on the vertex is needed.

6. Step-by-step example

For which $m$ does the equation $(m-1)x^2+2mx+m+2=0$ have two distinct negative roots?

  1. $m=1$: $2x+3=0$, one solution, so the condition fails. From now on $m\neq1$.
  2. $\Delta=4m^2-4(m-1)(m+2)=8-4m>0 \iff m<2$.
  3. $x_1x_2=\frac{m+2}{m-1}>0 \iff (m+2)(m-1)>0 \iff m\in(-\infty,-2)\cup(1,\infty)$.
  4. $x_1+x_2=\frac{-2m}{m-1}<0 \iff m(m-1)>0 \iff m\in(-\infty,0)\cup(1,\infty)$.
  5. Intersection: $m<-2$ or $1<m<2$.

$m\in(-\infty,-2)\cup(1,2)$

Check it on the map in demo 4 (the family $(m-1)x^2\ldots$, “two distinct negative”).

For which $m$ are both roots of $x^2-2mx+m+2=0$ distinct and lie in $(0,4)$?

  1. $\frac{\Delta}{4}=m^2-m-2=(m-2)(m+1)>0 \iff m\in(-\infty,-1)\cup(2,\infty)$.
  2. $f(0)=m+2>0 \iff m>-2$.
  3. $f(4)=18-7m>0 \iff m<\frac{18}{7}$.
  4. $x_w=m\in(0,4)$.
  5. Intersection: $2<m<\frac{18}7$.

$m\in\left(2,\frac{18}{7}\right)$

7. Tasks

  1. easy
    For which $m$ does the equation $x^2+4x+m=0$ have two distinct solutions?
    Solution
    $\Delta=16-4m>0$. $m<4$
  2. medium
    Solve the equation $mx-2=2x+m^2-6$ depending on $m$.
    Solution
    $(m-2)x=m^2-4=(m-2)(m+2)$. For $m=2$: $0=0$. For $m\neq2$ we divide by $m-2$. $m=2$: $x\in\R$; $\;m\neq2$: $x=m+2$.
  3. medium
    For which $m$ does the inequality $mx^2+4x+m>0$ hold for every real number?
    Solution
    $m=0$: $4x>0$ does not hold for every $x$. For $m\neq0$ the parabola must lie entirely above the axis: $m>0$ and $\Delta=16-4m^2<0$, i.e. $m^2>4$. Together: $m>2$
  4. exam-level
    For which $m$ does the equation $x^2-2(m+1)x+m+7=0$ have two distinct positive roots?
    Solution
    $\frac\Delta4=(m+1)^2-(m+7)=m^2+m-6=(m+3)(m-2)>0$: $m<-3$ or $m>2$.
    $x_1x_2=m+7>0$: $m>-7$. $\;x_1+x_2=2(m+1)>0$: $m>-1$. $m\in(2,\infty)$
  5. exam-level
    For which $m$ does the equation $(m+1)x^2-4x+m-2=0$ have exactly one solution?
    Solution
    $m=-1$: $-4x-3=0$, one solution $x=-\frac34$. ✓
    $m\neq-1$: $\Delta=16-4(m+1)(m-2)=-4(m-3)(m+2)=0 \iff m=3$ or $m=-2$. $m\in\{-2,-1,3\}$ (a forgotten case $a=0$ is a typical lost point).
  6. exam-level
    For which $m$ does the equation $x^2-2mx+m+6=0$ have two distinct roots, both greater than $1$?
    Solution
    $\frac\Delta4=m^2-m-6=(m-3)(m+2)>0$: $m<-2$ or $m>3$.
    $f(1)=7-m>0$: $m<7$. $\;x_w=m>1$.
    Intersection: $m\in(3,7)$

8. On the exam

  • Treat the case $a=0$ explicitly and write down the conclusion, even when it gives nothing. A point is usually lost for omitting it.
  • List the conditions in points (1°, 2°, 3°) before calculating. Correct conditions and solving some of them earn partial points.
  • “Two solutions” in task statements usually means two distinct ones, i.e. $\Delta>0$. “Two roots (not necessarily distinct)” means $\Delta\ge0$.
  • At the end, the intersection on the $m$-axis: draw it. Watch the endpoints (strict or non-strict inequalities).
  • Turn conditions with $\frac{c}{a}$ into a product: $\frac{m+2}{m-1}>0 \iff (m+2)(m-1)>0$.
