Exponential and logarithmic functions

$y=a^x$ and $y=\log_a x$ are the same relationship read in two directions: $\log_a b=c\iff a^c=b$. Swapping the roles of $x$ and $y$ is a reflection of the graph in the line $y=x$.

V.14, I.5, I.9, I.R (change of base), I.8

1. Definitions and formulas

Logarithm on CKE sheet
$$\log_a b=c\iff a^c=b$$
conditions
$a>0$, $a\neq1$, $b>0$
Operations on logarithms on CKE sheet
$$\log_a(xy)=\log_a x+\log_a y$$ $$\log_a\frac xy=\log_a x-\log_a y$$ $$\log_a x^r=r\log_a x$$ $$\log_a b=\frac{\log_c b}{\log_c a}$$
assumptions
$a,c>0$, $a,c\neq1$, $x,y,b>0$; the last formula (change of base) is extended level
Proof (required on the exam): the logarithm of a product and change of base

Product. Let $u=\log_a x$, $v=\log_a y$, that is $a^u=x$, $a^v=y$. Then $xy=a^u a^v=a^{u+v}$, so by the definition $\log_a(xy)=u+v$. The quotient and the power are proved in the same way ($a^u/a^v=a^{u-v}$, $(a^u)^r=a^{ur}$).

Change of base. Let $t=\log_a b$, that is $a^t=b$. Take the logarithm of both sides with base $c$: $\log_c a^t=\log_c b$, that is $t\log_c a=\log_c b$. Since $a\neq1$, $\log_c a\neq0$, so $t=\frac{\log_c b}{\log_c a}$.

Monotonicity of exponentiation (I.5): for $a>1$, $x<y$ implies $a^x<a^y$, but for $0<a<1$ the inequality changes direction: $\left(\frac12\right)^x>\left(\frac12\right)^3\iff x<3$.

4. Worked example

$10\,000$ PLN was deposited in an account paying $5\%$ per year (annual compounding). After how many full years will the amount exceed $15\,000$ PLN?

  1. $10\,000\cdot1.05^n>15\,000\iff 1.05^n>1.5$.
  2. Take logarithms ($\log$ is increasing, so the sign stays): $n\log1.05>\log1.5$.
  3. $n>\frac{\log1.5}{\log1.05}\approx8.31$, so the smallest integer is $n=9$.
  4. Check: $1.05^8\cdot10\,000\approx14\,775$ PLN, $\;1.05^9\cdot10\,000\approx15\,513$ PLN.

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    The graph of $f(x)=a^x$ passes through the point $(-2,9)$. Find $a$ and determine the monotonicity of $f$.
    Solution
    $a^{-2}=9\iff a^2=\frac19$, and $a>0$, so $a=\frac13<1$. $a=\frac13$, the function is decreasing
  2. easy
    Compute $\log_3 54-\log_3 2$ and $2\log_5 10-\log_5 4$.
    Solution
    $\log_3\frac{54}{2}=\log_3 27=3$. $\;\log_5 100-\log_5 4=\log_5 25=2$. $3$ and $2$
  3. medium
    Given that $\log_2 3=a$, express $\log_{12}18$ in terms of $a$.
    Solution
    Change of base to $2$: $\log_{12}18=\frac{\log_2 18}{\log_2 12}=\frac{\log_2(2\cdot 9)}{\log_2(4\cdot3)}$. $\log_{12}18=\frac{1+2a}{2+a}$
  4. medium
    Find the domain of the function $f(x)=\log_x(6-x)$.
    Solution
    Base: $x>0$ and $x\neq1$. The argument of the logarithm: $6-x>0\iff x<6$. $D=(0,1)\cup(1,6)$
  5. exam-level
    The half-life of iodine-131 is $8$ days. After how many days will $25$ mg remain from $200$ mg? How much will remain after $20$ days?
    Solution
    $200\cdot\left(\frac12\right)^{t/8}=25\iff\left(\frac12\right)^{t/8}=\frac18=\left(\frac12\right)^3$, so $t=24$. After $20$ days: $200\cdot2^{-20/8}=200\cdot2^{-5/2}=\frac{200}{4\sqrt2}=25\sqrt2$. After $24$ days; after $20$ days $25\sqrt2\approx35.36$ mg

6. On the exam

  • The domain of a logarithm is two conditions on the base ($>0$, $\neq1$) and one on the argument ($>0$). Write them down before solving.
  • For a base $0<a<1$ the inequality changes direction when you drop the power or the logarithm.
  • Change of base: most often you reduce everything to one “convenient” base (e.g. $2$ or $10$).
  • Deposit problems: the question “after how many years” needs an integer; check both neighboring values.
