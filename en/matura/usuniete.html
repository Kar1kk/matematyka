Removed from the core curriculum

Topics that were in the 2018 curriculum (and on the 2015–2024 exams) but are not in the 2024 curriculum (matura from 2025). They are not required on the 2025+ exam. They are useful for olympiads, university, and older exam papers and problem books.

Some of this material is no longer required, but its formulas are still on the CKE formula sheet (2023 edition, also valid for the 2026–2027 exams), so you may use them on the exam: distance from a point to a line, variance and standard deviation, expected value, the tangent–chord angle theorem, the general form of the circle equation, and the table of trigonometric function values.

Sources: the 2018 curriculum (Dz.U. 2018 item 467) and the 2024 curriculum (Dz.U. 2024 item 1019). Detailed comparison with quotations: docs/usuniete-z-podstawy.md.

1. Analytic geometry

Distance from a point to a line on CKE sheet

This formula is on the CKE formula sheet — you may use it on the exam.

A ready-made formula for the distance of a point $P=(x_0,y_0)$ from a line in general form. In 2024 it is not among the requirements (neither basic nor extended level), but it is on the CKE formula sheet, so you may use it. You can also derive it in a few lines (below). Alternatively, the same result comes from methods in the current curriculum: the perpendicular line through $P$, the intersection point, the length of a segment.

Distance from a point to the line $Ax+By+C=0$
$$d=\frac{|Ax_0+By_0+C|}{\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}$$
$[A,B]$
a vector perpendicular to the line
numerator
the left-hand side of the line's equation after substituting $P$; it equals $0$ exactly when $P$ lies on the line
Derivation

The line through $P$ perpendicular to $Ax+By+C=0$ has direction $[A,B]$, so its points are $(x_0+At,\;y_0+Bt)$. Substitute into the line's equation:

$$A(x_0+At)+B(y_0+Bt)+C=0$$ $$t=-\frac{Ax_0+By_0+C}{A^2+B^2}$$

The projection $H$ corresponds to this $t$, and $|PH|=|t|\cdot|[A,B]|=|t|\sqrt{A^2+B^2}$. After simplifying we get $d=\frac{|Ax_0+By_0+C|}{\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}$.

Example

Distance of $P=(3,4)$ from the line $3x+4y-5=0$:

$$d=\frac{|9+16-5|}{\sqrt{9+16}}=\frac{20}{5}$$

$d=4$

General form of the circle equation on CKE sheet

This formula is on the CKE formula sheet — you may use it on the exam.

Expanding $(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2$ gives the general form. In 2024 only the standard form is required, but the general form (with the condition $a^2+b^2-c>0$) is on the CKE formula sheet. From the general form you go back to the standard one anyway by completing the square.

General form of a circle
$$x^2+y^2-2ax-2by+c=0$$ $$S=(a,b),\quad r=\sqrt{a^2+b^2-c}$$
condition
$a^2+b^2-c>0$; otherwise the equation does not describe a circle
Example

$x^2+y^2-6x+4y-12=0$: $-2a=-6$, $-2b=4$, so $a=3$, $b=-2$, $c=-12$.

$r^2=9+4+12=25$. $S=(3,-2)$, $r=5$

Common points of a line and a parabola

The analytic-geometry requirement is gone, but the technique lives on: it is a system of equations, one of which is quadratic (IV.R in 2024). Substitute $y$ from the line into the parabola and look at $\Delta$: $\Delta>0$ two points, $\Delta=0$ the line is tangent, $\Delta<0$ none.

Example

$y=x+1$ and $y=x^2-1$: $x^2-1=x+1$, i.e. $x^2-x-2=0$, $x=2$ or $x=-1$.

$(2,3)$ and $(-1,0)$

2. Polynomials and functions

Rational roots of a polynomial

In 2024 only integer roots remain (divisors of the constant term). The rational root theorem gives a finite list of candidates for fractions too.

Theorem If a polynomial $W(x)=a_nx^n+\ldots+a_1x+a_0$ has integer coefficients and the fraction in lowest terms $\frac{p}{q}$ is its root, then $p\mid a_0$ and $q\mid a_n$.
Proof

Substitute $\frac pq$ and multiply by $q^n$:

$$a_np^n+a_{n-1}p^{n-1}q+\ldots+a_0q^n=0$$

All terms except $a_0q^n$ are divisible by $p$, so $p\mid a_0q^n$. Since $p$ and $q$ are coprime, $p\mid a_0$. Likewise, all terms except $a_np^n$ are divisible by $q$, so $q\mid a_n$.

Example

$W(x)=2x^3+x^2+x-1$. Candidates: $p\in\{\pm1\}$, $q\in\{1,2\}$, i.e. $\pm1,\pm\frac12$.

$W(1)=3$, $W(-1)=-3$, $W(-\frac12)=-\frac32$, $W(\frac12)=0$. Division: $W(x)=(2x-1)(x^2+x+1)$, and $x^2+x+1$ has $\Delta=-3<0$.

The only root: $x=\frac12$ (there are no integer roots, so the 2024 method would find nothing).

Graph of $y=|f(x)|$ (and $y=f(|x|)$)

$|f(x)|$: the part of the graph above the $Ox$ axis stays, the part below the axis is reflected across $Ox$. $f(|x|)$: the part for $x\ge0$ stays, and the left half is replaced by its reflection across $Oy$. Formally, in 2018 only $|f(x)|$ was listed; $f(|x|)$ goes hand in hand with it in problem books.

Two transformations
$$|f(x)|=\begin{cases}f(x), & f(x)\ge0\\-f(x), & f(x)<0\end{cases}$$ $$f(|x|)=\begin{cases}f(x), & x\ge0\\f(-x), & x<0\end{cases}$$
Example: number of solutions of $|x^2-4|=m$

The graph of $|x^2-4|$ is a parabola with a "flipped" dip: minimum $0$ at $x=\pm2$, local maximum $4$ at $x=0$. The line $y=m$ intersects it:

$m<0$: 0 solutions; $m=0$: 2; $0<m<4$: 4; $m=4$: 3; $m>4$: 2.

Example: $x^2-4|x|+3=0$

This is $f(|x|)=0$ for $f(x)=x^2-4x+3=(x-1)(x-3)$. So $|x|=1$ or $|x|=3$.

$x\in\{-3,-1,1,3\}$

3. Trigonometry

Trigonometric inequalities

In 2024 only trigonometric equations remain. You solve an inequality the same way and then read the intervals off the graph or the unit circle. Remember the period: a solution in $\R$ is a union of intervals "$+2k\pi$" (or "$+k\pi$").

Scheme (e.g. $\sin x>a$, $|a|<1$)
$$\sin x=a\ \Rightarrow\ x_1,\ x_2\in[0,2\pi)$$
1.
solve the equation within one period
2.
from the graph pick the intervals where $\sin x$ is above the line $y=a$
3.
add the period $2k\pi$, $k\in\Z$
Example: $2\sin^2x\le1$ in $[0,2\pi]$

$\sin^2x\le\frac12 \iff |\sin x|\le\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$. Equality for $x=\frac\pi4,\frac{3\pi}4,\frac{5\pi}4,\frac{7\pi}4$; between them the graph of $|\sin x|$ is low near $0$, $\pi$, $2\pi$.

$x\in[0,\frac\pi4]\cup[\frac{3\pi}4,\frac{5\pi}4]\cup[\frac{7\pi}4,2\pi]$

Check by another method: $2\sin^2x=1-\cos2x$, so the inequality is $\cos2x\ge0$, i.e. in $\R$: $x\in[-\frac\pi4+k\pi,\frac\pi4+k\pi]$.

Tables and calculator on CKE sheet

This formula is on the CKE formula sheet — you may use it on the exam.

Reading approximate values of $\sin$, $\cos$, $\tan$ from tables (e.g. $\sin 37^\circ\approx0.6018$) and the reverse: finding the angle from the function value. The 2024 curriculum has no such requirement, but the CKE formula sheet still contains a table of trigonometric function values every $1^\circ$, so you may use it.

4. Plane and solid geometry

Angle between a tangent and a chord on CKE sheet

This formula is on the CKE formula sheet — you may use it on the exam.

The angle between the tangent at point $A$ and the chord $AB$ equals the inscribed angle subtending the arc $AB$ (lying inside that angle). In 2024 it is neither among the requirements nor on the list of proofs, but it is easy to justify from the central angle.

Tangent–chord angle
$$\angle(\text{tangent},AB)=\tfrac12\angle AOB$$
$O$
the center of the circle; $\angle AOB$ is the central angle, so the angle in question equals every inscribed angle $\angle ACB$ subtending the arc $AB$
Justification and example

Let the angle between the tangent and $AB$ be $\alpha$ (acute). $OA\perp$ tangent, so $\angle OAB=90^\circ-\alpha$. Triangle $AOB$ is isosceles, so $\angle AOB=180^\circ-2(90^\circ-\alpha)=2\alpha$. The inscribed angle on this arc is half of that: $\alpha$.

Example: the tangent forms an angle of $50^\circ$ with the chord $AB$. Then $\angle AOB=100^\circ$ and the inscribed angle $\angle ACB=50^\circ$.

Cross-section of a cuboid by a plane

Recognizing which shape (triangle, quadrilateral, pentagon, hexagon) a plane section of a cuboid is. In 2024 only sections of the cube and of regular pyramids remain.

5. Statistics and probability

Variance and standard deviation on CKE sheet

This formula is on the CKE formula sheet — you may use it on the exam.

The mean tells you where the data are; the standard deviation tells you how spread out they are around the mean. In 2024 only the mean (also weighted), the median and the mode remain.

Variance and standard deviation
$$\sigma^2=\frac{(x_1-\bar x)^2+\ldots+(x_n-\bar x)^2}{n}$$ $$\sigma=\sqrt{\sigma^2}$$
more convenient
$\sigma^2=\overline{x^2}-\bar x^2$ (mean of the squares minus the square of the mean)
grouped data
value $x_i$ with frequency $n_i$: multiply each squared deviation by $n_i$ (weighted mean)
Example

Data: $2,4,4,4,5,5,7,9$. Mean $\bar x=\frac{40}{8}=5$.

Squared deviations: $9,1,1,1,0,0,4,16$, sum $32$, so $\sigma^2=\frac{32}{8}=4$. (Alternatively: $\overline{x^2}=\frac{232}{8}=29$, $29-25=4$.)

$\sigma^2=4$, $\sigma=2$

Expected value on CKE sheet

This formula is on the CKE formula sheet — you may use it on the exam.

The average winnings "in the long run": multiply each possible value by its probability and add up. A game is fair when the expected value of the profit is $0$.

Expected value
$$E=p_1x_1+p_2x_2+\ldots+p_nx_n$$
$x_i$
possible outcomes (e.g. winnings in PLN)
$p_i$
their probabilities, $p_1+\ldots+p_n=1$
Example

A roll of a die costs 3 PLN. For a six you get 12 PLN, for a five 3 PLN, otherwise nothing.

Average payout: $\frac16\cdot12+\frac16\cdot3=2.5$ PLN. Profit: $2.5-3$.

$E=-0.5$ PLN per game (fair price of the game: 2.50 PLN).

Percentile scale

The $k$-th percentile is the value below which $k\%$ of the data lie (e.g. children's height percentile charts: the "50th percentile" is the median). Removed.

8. How to use this

  • Older papers (2015–2024) and older editions of problem books: you can skip problems on these topics, you will not get them on the 2025+ exam.
  • Olympiad and university: rational roots, distance from a point to a line, the tangent–chord angle, expected value and variance are standard tools, worth knowing.
  • On the exam you may use formulas from the CKE sheet (e.g. the point–line distance shortens line–circle and distance problems). However, problems will not require material from outside the curriculum, so they can always be solved with the methods of the current curriculum too.
