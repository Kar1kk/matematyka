Limit of a function (including one-sided)

A limit tells you what the values $f(x)$ approach as $x$ gets closer and closer to $a$, not what $f(a)$ is. The function does not even have to be defined at $a$.

XIII.R.1

1. Definitions

Definition (Heine) $\lim\limits_{x\to a} f(x)=g$ if for every sequence $(x_n)$ in the domain, $x_n\neq a$, $x_n\to a$, we have $f(x_n)\to g$.
Left-hand limit
$$\lim_{x\to a^-} f(x)$$
$x<a$
approaching from the left
Right-hand limit
$$\lim_{x\to a^+} f(x)$$
$x>a$
approaching from the right
Theorem $\lim\limits_{x\to a} f(x)=g$ exists $\iff$ both one-sided limits exist and are equal to $g$.
  • Improper: $f(x)\to+\infty$ or $-\infty$. E.g. $\lim\limits_{x\to0}\frac1{x^2}=+\infty$. Then the line $x=a$ is a vertical asymptote.
  • At infinity: $\lim\limits_{x\to\pm\infty} f(x)=g$. Then the line $y=g$ is a horizontal asymptote.

3. How to compute limits

When the function is "nice" at $a$ (a polynomial, a rational function with denominator $\neq0$, a square root), just substitute: $\lim\limits_{x\to2}(x^2+1)=5$. The limit of a sum, product, quotient is the sum, product, quotient of the limits (if they are finite and the denominator $\neq0$).

Indeterminate forms: $\frac00$, $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$, $\infty-\infty$, $0\cdot\infty$. The result can be anything, you have to transform the expression.
  • $\frac00$ for a rational function: factor and cancel the factor $(x-a)$.
  • $\frac00$ with a square root: multiply by the conjugate, e.g. $\sqrt{x+4}+2$.
  • $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$: divide the numerator and the denominator by the highest power in the denominator.
  • $\infty-\infty$ with a square root: the conjugate, then as for $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$.

5. Step-by-step example

Find $\lim\limits_{x\to2}\frac{x^2-4}{x^2-3x+2}$.

  1. Substitution gives $\frac00$: an indeterminate form.
  2. Factoring: $\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{(x-2)(x-1)}=\frac{x+2}{x-1}$ for $x\neq2$.
  3. Now substitute: $\frac{2+2}{2-1}=4$.

The limit is $4$ (the graph has a "hole" at $(2,4)$).

Find $\lim\limits_{x\to+\infty}\frac{3x^2-x}{2x^2+5}$ and the one-sided limits of $\frac{x+1}{x-1}$ at $1$.

  1. Divide by $x^2$: $\frac{3-\frac1x}{2+\frac{5}{x^2}}\to\frac{3-0}{2+0}=\frac32$.
  2. $x\to1^-$: numerator $\to2$, denominator $\to0^-$, so $\frac{x+1}{x-1}\to-\infty$.
  3. $x\to1^+$: denominator $\to0^+$, so $\to+\infty$.

$\frac32$; one-sided limits $-\infty$ and $+\infty$, the limit at $1$ does not exist, $x=1$ is a vertical asymptote.

6. Tasks

  1. easy
    Find $\lim\limits_{x\to3}\frac{x^2-9}{x-3}$.
    Solution
    $\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}=x+3\to6$. $6$
  2. easy
    Find $\lim\limits_{x\to-\infty}\frac{4x^3-x}{1-2x^3}$.
    Solution
    Divide by $x^3$: $\frac{4-\frac1{x^2}}{\frac1{x^3}-2}\to\frac{4}{-2}$. $-2$
  3. medium
    Find the one-sided limits of the function $f(x)=\frac{x+1}{2-x}$ at the point $2$. Does $\lim\limits_{x\to2}f(x)$ exist?
    Solution
    The numerator $\to3$. For $x\to2^-$: $2-x\to0^+$, so $f\to+\infty$. For $x\to2^+$: $2-x\to0^-$, so $f\to-\infty$. $+\infty$ and $-\infty$; the limit does not exist, $x=2$ is a vertical asymptote.
  4. medium
    Find $\lim\limits_{x\to0}\frac{\sqrt{x+4}-2}{x}$.
    Solution
    Multiply by the conjugate: $$\frac{(x+4)-4}{x\left(\sqrt{x+4}+2\right)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+4}+2}$$ $\to\frac1{2+2}$. $\frac14$
  5. exam-level
    Find $a$ and $b$ such that $\lim\limits_{x\to1}\frac{x^2+ax+b}{x-1}=5$.
    Solution
    The denominator $\to0$ and the limit is finite, so the numerator must also $\to0$: $1+a+b=0$. Then $1$ is a root of the numerator, and the other root is $b$ (Vieta's formulas: $x_1x_2=b$), i.e. $x^2+ax+b=(x-1)(x-b)$. The limit: $1-b=5$, so $b=-4$, $a=3$. $a=3$, $b=-4$ (check: $\frac{(x-1)(x+4)}{x-1}\to5$).
  6. exam-level
    Find $\lim\limits_{x\to+\infty}\left(\sqrt{x^2+4x}-x\right)$.
    Solution
    The form $\infty-\infty$. Conjugate: $$\frac{x^2+4x-x^2}{\sqrt{x^2+4x}+x}=\frac{4x}{\sqrt{x^2+4x}+x}$$ Divide by $x$ (for $x>0$: $\sqrt{x^2}=x$): $\frac{4}{\sqrt{1+\frac4x}+1}\to\frac42$. $2$

7. On the exam

  • Write down which form comes out of the substitution ($\frac00$, $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$), and then the transformation. A bare "$=4$" without cancelling is a lack of justification.
  • $\frac{c}{0}$: always compute the one-sided limits and check the sign of the denominator ($0^+$ or $0^-$).
  • Dividing by $x$ under a square root: for $x\to-\infty$ we have $\sqrt{x^2}=|x|=-x$. A classic trap.
  • The limit at $a$ does not depend on $f(a)$. The relation between the value and the limit is continuity.
