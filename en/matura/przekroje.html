Cross-sections of a cube and regular pyramids

A plane cuts a solid along a polygon. Its sides lie on the faces of the solid, so you build the section face by face: you join points lying on the same face, and on parallel faces you draw parallel segments.

X.R.2 (the cross-section of a cuboid was removed from the core curriculum; the cube and regular pyramids remain)

1. How to find a cross-section

  1. Two points of the plane lying on the same face are joined by a segment: it is a side of the section.
  2. The plane cuts parallel faces along parallel segments.
  3. When a point is missing, extend a side of the section until it meets the line of an edge of the same face (a point on the extension of the edge, the so-called trace), then join it to a point on the adjacent face.
  4. A section has at most as many sides as the solid has faces: in a cube from $3$ to $6$, in a square pyramid from $3$ to $5$.

4. Step-by-step example

A regular square pyramid $ABCDS$ has base edge $6$ and height $3\sqrt3$. A plane passes through edge $AB$ and makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the base. Find the area of the cross-section.

  1. $M$, $N$ are the midpoints of $AB$ and $CD$. In triangle $MNS$: $|MN|=6$, $\tan\sphericalangle SMN=\frac{3\sqrt3}{3}=\sqrt3$, so the angle at $M$ is $60^\circ$ and triangle $MNS$ is equilateral.
  2. $AB\parallel CD$, so $AB$ is parallel to plane $CDS$ and the cutting plane meets face $CDS$ along $KL\parallel CD$. The section is the trapezoid $ABLK$.
  3. The line from $M$ at $30^\circ$ is the bisector of the $60^\circ$ angle, so it hits the midpoint $P$ of segment $SN$; $|MP|=\frac{6\sqrt3}{2}=3\sqrt3$ (the altitude of the equilateral triangle) and this is the height of the trapezoid.
  4. $P$ is the midpoint of $SN$, so $KL$ is a midsegment of triangle $CDS$: $|KL|=3$.

$P=\frac{6+3}{2}\cdot3\sqrt3=\frac{27\sqrt3}{2}$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    A cube has edge $4$. Find the area of the cross-section by the plane through the base diagonal $AC$ and vertex $G$.
    Solution
    The section is the rectangle $ACGE$ with sides $4\sqrt2$ and $4$. $16\sqrt2$
  2. easy
    A cube has edge $2$. Find the area of the cross-section by plane $BDE$ and the tangent of the angle between this plane and the base.
    Solution
    The sides $BD$, $DE$, $EB$ are face diagonals: an equilateral triangle with side $2\sqrt2$, $P=\frac{8\sqrt3}{4}$. The angle: $\tan=\frac{|AE|}{|AO|}=\frac{2}{\sqrt2}$. $P=2\sqrt3$, $\tan\alpha=\sqrt2$
  3. medium
    A plane passes through the midpoints of edges $BC$, $DH$ and $EF$ of a cube with edge $4$. Determine the shape and area of the cross-section.
    Solution
    The plane passes through the midpoints of six edges (also $CD$, $HE$, $BF$): a regular hexagon with side $\frac{4\sqrt2}{2}=2\sqrt2$. $P=6\cdot\frac{8\sqrt3}{4}$. Regular hexagon, $P=12\sqrt3$
  4. medium
    A regular square pyramid has base edge $6$ and height $4$. Find the area of the cross-section by the plane through the apex $S$ and a diagonal of the base.
    Solution
    Triangle $ACS$: base $6\sqrt2$, height $H=4$. $12\sqrt2$
  5. exam-level
    A cube has edge $4$. A plane passes through vertices $B$, $D$ and the midpoint $M$ of edge $EF$. Determine the cross-section and find its area.
    Solution
    Faces $ABCD$ and $EFGH$ are parallel, so in the top face the side of the section is $MN\parallel BD$; $N$ is the midpoint of $EH$. The section is the isosceles trapezoid $BDNM$, $|BD|=4\sqrt2$, $|MN|=2\sqrt2$, leg $|BM|=\sqrt{16+4}=2\sqrt5$. Height: $\sqrt{20-(\sqrt2)^2}=\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt2$. $P=\frac{4\sqrt2+2\sqrt2}{2}\cdot3\sqrt2$. Trapezoid, $P=18$
  6. exam-level
    All edges of the regular square pyramid $ABCDS$ have length $4$. Find the area of the cross-section by the plane through edge $AB$ and the midpoint of edge $SC$.
    Solution
    $AB\parallel CD$, so the plane cuts face $CDS$ along $KL\parallel CD$, where $K$, $L$ are the midpoints of $SC$, $SD$: $|KL|=2$. Trapezoid $ABKL$. $H=\sqrt{16-8}=2\sqrt2$. The height of the trapezoid in the plane of symmetry: $M=(0,-2,0)$, midpoint of $KL$: $P=(0,1,\sqrt2)$, $|MP|=\sqrt{9+2}=\sqrt{11}$. $P=\frac{4+2}{2}\cdot\sqrt{11}$. $3\sqrt{11}$

6. On the exam

  • First justify the shape of the section (parallel segments on parallel faces, equal sides from symmetry), then compute the area.
  • Area of a trapezoid: its height usually lies in a plane of symmetry of the solid; compute it from a triangle in that plane.
  • Area of a section and an angle: $P_{\text{projection}}=P\cdot\cos\varphi$, where $\varphi$ is the angle between the cutting plane and the base (worth remembering, handy for checking).
  • A plane through a base edge of a pyramid: when it is inclined to the base exactly like a lateral face, the section is that face (a triangle); when it is inclined more steeply, it does not enter the pyramid (only the edge remains).
