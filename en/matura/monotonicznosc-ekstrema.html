Monotonicity and extrema

The sign of the derivative tells you whether the graph goes up or down. Where the derivative changes sign, the function turns around: there is a peak (maximum) or a valley (minimum).

XIII.R.5, XIII.R.6

1. Theorems

Monotonicity Let $f$ be differentiable on an interval $I$. $$f'(x)>0 \text{ on } I\ \Rightarrow\ f \text{ is increasing on } I$$ $$f'(x)<0 \text{ on } I\ \Rightarrow\ f \text{ is decreasing on } I$$

Conversely: if $f$ is differentiable and increasing on an interval $I$, then $f'(x)\ge0$ on $I$ (similarly: decreasing $\Rightarrow f'(x)\le0$).

$f'\ge0$ is also enough if $f'=0$ only at isolated points (e.g. $x^3$ is increasing on $\R$, although $f'(0)=0$).

Extremum Necessary condition: if $f$ has an extremum at $x_0$ and is differentiable there, then $f'(x_0)=0$.
Sufficient condition: if $f'(x_0)=0$ and $f'$ changes sign at $x_0$, then there is an extremum at $x_0$:
  • from $+$ to $-$: a local maximum,
  • from $-$ to $+$: a local minimum.
$f'(x_0)=0$ alone is not enough: $f(x)=x^3$ has $f'(0)=0$ but no extremum (the derivative does not change sign).

4. Step-by-step example

Investigate the monotonicity and find the extrema of $f(x)=x^3-3x^2-9x+5$.

  1. $D=\R$, $f'(x)=3x^2-6x-9=3(x+1)(x-3)$.
  2. $f'(x)=0 \iff x=-1$ or $x=3$. The parabola $f'$ opens upwards:
$x$$(-\infty,-1)$$-1$$(-1,3)$$3$$(3,\infty)$
$f'$$+$$0$$-$$0$$+$
$f$↗max↘min↗
  1. $f(-1)=-1-3+9+5=10$, $\;f(3)=27-27-27+5=-22$.

Increasing on $(-\infty,-1]$ and on $[3,\infty)$, decreasing on $[-1,3]$. Local maximum $f(-1)=10$, local minimum $f(3)=-22$.

The same for the rational function $f(x)=\frac{x^2+3}{x-1}$.

  1. $D=\R\setminus\{1\}$. $f'(x)=\frac{2x(x-1)-(x^2+3)}{(x-1)^2}=\frac{(x+1)(x-3)}{(x-1)^2}$.
  2. The denominator is $>0$, so the sign of $f'$ is the sign of $(x+1)(x-3)$: $+$, $-$, $-$, $+$ on $(-\infty,-1)$, $(-1,1)$, $(1,3)$, $(3,\infty)$.
  3. $f(-1)=\frac{4}{-2}=-2$, $\;f(3)=\frac{12}{2}=6$.

Increasing on $(-\infty,-1]$ and on $[3,\infty)$, decreasing on $[-1,1)$ and on $(1,3]$. Local maximum $-2$, local minimum $6$ (a local maximum smaller than a local minimum is possible: the asymptote separates them).

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Find the intervals of monotonicity and the extrema of $f(x)=x^3-12x$.
    Solution
    $f'(x)=3x^2-12=3(x-2)(x+2)$. Increasing on $(-\infty,-2]$ and $[2,\infty)$, decreasing on $[-2,2]$; local maximum $f(-2)=16$, local minimum $f(2)=-16$.
  2. medium
    Find the extrema of $f(x)=x^4-8x^2+3$.
    Solution
    $f'(x)=4x^3-16x=4x(x-2)(x+2)$. Signs: $-,+,-,+$ on consecutive intervals. Local minima $f(-2)=f(2)=-13$, local maximum $f(0)=3$.
  3. medium
    Find the greatest and least value of $f(x)=2x^3-3x^2-12x+1$ on the interval $[-2,3]$.
    Solution
    $f'(x)=6(x-2)(x+1)$, both points are in the interval. Candidates: $f(-2)=-3$, $f(-1)=8$, $f(2)=-19$, $f(3)=-8$. Greatest $8$, least $-19$.
  4. medium
    Investigate the monotonicity of $f(x)=\frac{x}{x^2+4}$.
    Solution
    $f'(x)=\frac{x^2+4-2x^2}{(x^2+4)^2}=\frac{(2-x)(2+x)}{(x^2+4)^2}$. Increasing on $[-2,2]$, decreasing on $(-\infty,-2]$ and on $[2,\infty)$; local minimum $-\frac14$, local maximum $\frac14$.
  5. exam-level
    For what $m$ is the function $f(x)=x^3+mx^2+3x$ increasing on $\R$?
    Solution
    $f'(x)=3x^2+2mx+3\ge0$ for every $x$ (zeros only at isolated points) $\iff\Delta\le0$: $4m^2-36\le0$. $m\in[-3,3]$
  6. exam-level
    For what $m$ does the equation $x^3-3x=m$ have three solutions?
    Solution
    The number of solutions is the number of common points of the graph of $f(x)=x^3-3x$ and the line $y=m$. $f'=3(x-1)(x+1)$: local maximum $f(-1)=2$, local minimum $f(1)=-2$, and $f\to\pm\infty$ at the ends. A horizontal line meets the graph three times when it lies strictly between the local minimum and the local maximum. $m\in(-2,2)$ (see the graph in the demo).

6. On the exam

  • Start with the domain. Intervals of monotonicity cannot pass through a point outside the domain.
  • Do not write “decreasing on $(-\infty,0)\cup(0,\infty)$”. $\frac1x$ is decreasing on each of these intervals separately, but not on their union ($-1<1$, yet $f(-1)<f(1)$).
  • Extremum: show the sign change of $f'$ (a table or a sketch of the parabola). $f'=0$ alone is not enough.
  • Greatest value on $[a,b]$: compare with the values at the endpoints. A local maximum need not be the greatest value.
  • The endpoints of intervals of monotonicity may be closed (if they belong to the domain). Both notations are usually accepted; it is safest to close the endpoints where the function is defined and continuous.
