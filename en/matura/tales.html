Thales' theorem (intercept theorem) and its converse

Parallel lines cut the arms of an angle "proportionally": if the segments on one arm are in the ratio $2:3$, so they are on the other. The converse works the other way: equal ratios guarantee parallelism.

VIII.7, VIII.R.2

1. Thales' theorem

The lines $AB$ and $CD$ meet at the point $P$ ($A$, $B$ on one arm, $C$, $D$ on the other).

Thales' theorem on CKE sheet If $AC\parallel BD$, then $$\frac{|PA|}{|AB|}=\frac{|PC|}{|CD|}$$ Equivalently $\frac{|PA|}{|PB|}=\frac{|PC|}{|PD|}$. Also, for the parallel segments: $$\frac{|AC|}{|BD|}=\frac{|PA|}{|PB|}$$
Where this comes from (area proof, optional)

The triangles $ACB$ and $ACD$ share the base $AC$, and $B$ and $D$ lie on a line parallel to $AC$ – so they have equal altitudes and equal areas. The triangles $PAC$ and $ACB$ share the altitude from $C$, so the ratio of their areas is the ratio of the bases. Likewise $PAC$ and $ACD$ (altitude from $A$):

$$\frac{|PA|}{|AB|}=\frac{P_{PAC}}{P_{ACB}}=\frac{P_{PAC}}{P_{ACD}}=\frac{|PC|}{|CD|}$$

The last formula, with $|AC|$, $|BD|$, follows from the similarity of the triangles $PAC$ and $PBD$ (AA). $\blacksquare$

The parallel segments $|AC|$, $|BD|$ must not be compared with $|AB|$: the correct form is $\frac{|AC|}{|BD|}=\frac{|PA|}{|PB|}$ (whole segments from $P$), not $\frac{|PA|}{|AB|}$.

The theorem also works when $P$ lies between the parallels (the "bowtie": $A$ and $B$ on opposite sides of $P$) – then the triangles $PAC$ and $PBD$ are "flipped", but the proportions are the same.

2. Interactive: the proportions stay

Drag $P$, $A$, $C$ and $B$ (along the line $PA$). The point $D$ is determined so that $BD\parallel AC$. Observe that the two ratios are always equal – also in the "bowtie", when you drag $B$ past $P$.

3. The converse

Converse of Thales' theorem on CKE sheet If $A$ lies inside the segment $PB$ and $C$ inside $PD$ (or both outside) and $$\frac{|PA|}{|AB|}=\frac{|PC|}{|CD|},$$ then $AC\parallel BD$.
Proof (optional)

Draw through $B$ a line parallel to $AC$; it meets the line $PC$ at a point $D'$ on the same side as $D$. By Thales' theorem $\frac{|PC|}{|CD'|}=\frac{|PA|}{|AB|}=\frac{|PC|}{|CD|}$, so $|CD'|=|CD|$ and $D'=D$. Hence $BD\parallel AC$. $\blacksquare$

The condition on the position of the points is not decoration: equal lengths alone do not imply parallelism. Check it in the demo below.

$A$, $B$, $C$ are fixed, $\frac{|PA|}{|AB|}=\frac23$. Drag $D$ along the line $PC$. There are two positions where $\frac{|PC|}{|CD|}=\frac23$, but the lines are parallel in only one of them.

4. Worked example

The arms of an angle with vertex $P$ are cut by the parallel lines $AC$ and $BD$ ($A$, $B$ on one arm). $|PA|=4$, $|AB|=6$, $|PC|=5$, $|AC|=3$. Find $|CD|$ and $|BD|$.

  1. Thales: $\frac{4}{6}=\frac{5}{|CD|}$, so $|CD|=\frac{30}{4}=7.5$.
  2. Parallel segments: $\frac{|AC|}{|BD|}=\frac{|PA|}{|PB|}=\frac{4}{10}$, so $|BD|=\frac{3\cdot10}{4}=7.5$.

$|CD|=7.5$, $|BD|=7.5$

5. Exercises

  1. easy
    The lines $AC$ and $BD$ are parallel, $|PA|=3$, $|PC|=4$, $|CD|=8$ (notation as above). Find $|AB|$.
    Solution
    $\frac{3}{|AB|}=\frac48$, so $|AB|=6$.
  2. easy
    A vertical pole of height $2$ m casts a shadow of length $1.5$ m. At the same moment the shadow of a tree is $9$ m long. How tall is the tree?
    Solution
    The sun's rays are parallel, and so are the pole and the tree: $\frac{h}{9}=\frac{2}{1.5}$. $h=12$ m
  3. medium
    The legs $AD$ and $BC$ of the trapezoid $ABCD$ ($AB\parallel CD$) are extended until they meet at the point $P$. $|AB|=15$, $|CD|=6$, $|AD|=6$. Find $|PD|$.
    Solution
    $\frac{|PD|}{|PA|}=\frac{|CD|}{|AB|}=\frac{6}{15}=\frac25$, and $|PA|=|PD|+6$. $5|PD|=2|PD|+12$. $|PD|=4$
  4. medium
    The points $A\in PB$ and $C\in PD$ lie inside the segments. Is $AC\parallel BD$ when: a) $|PA|=4$, $|AB|=6$, $|PC|=6$, $|CD|=9$; b) $|PA|=4$, $|PB|=10$, $|PC|=5$, $|PD|=12$?
    Solution
    a) $\frac46=\frac69=\frac23$ and the points lie inside the segments – by the converse theorem, yes. b) $\frac{4}{10}=0.4\ne\frac{5}{12}$ – if the lines were parallel, Thales' theorem would give equal ratios, so no.
  5. exam-level
    On the sides $AC$ and $BC$ of the triangle $ABC$ the points $D$ and $E$ are chosen: $|CD|=4$, $|DA|=6$, $|CE|=6$, $|EB|=9$. Show that $DE\parallel AB$, and find $|AB|$ if $|DE|=5$.
    Solution
    $\frac{|CD|}{|DA|}=\frac46=\frac23=\frac69=\frac{|CE|}{|EB|}$, and $D$, $E$ lie inside the sides, so by the converse of Thales' theorem $DE\parallel AB$. Then $\frac{|DE|}{|AB|}=\frac{|CD|}{|CA|}=\frac{4}{10}$. $|AB|=12.5$
  6. exam-level
    Prove that the segment joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and equal to half of it (the midsegment).
    Solution
    $M$, $N$ – the midpoints of $CA$ and $CB$. $\frac{|CM|}{|MA|}=1=\frac{|CN|}{|NB|}$ and the points lie inside the sides, so by the converse of Thales' theorem $MN\parallel AB$. By Thales' theorem (parallel segments) $\frac{|MN|}{|AB|}=\frac{|CM|}{|CA|}=\frac12$. Q.E.D.

6. On the exam

  • Write which lines are parallel and why – only then the proportion.
  • The most common mistake: $\frac{|AC|}{|BD|}=\frac{|PA|}{|AB|}$. Compare parallel segments with the whole segments from the vertex $P$.
  • With the converse theorem, check and state the position of the points (both inside or both outside the segments).
  • Thales is a special case of the similarity of the triangles $PAC$ and $PBD$ – if you get lost, write out the similar triangles.
