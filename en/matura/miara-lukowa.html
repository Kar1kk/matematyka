Radian measure and the unit circle

We measure an angle by the length of the arc it cuts on a circle of radius $1$. One radian is the angle for which the arc equals the radius. A full turn is the circumference, i.e. $2\pi\approx6.28$ radii.

VII.R.1, VII.R.2 (functions of any angle)

1. Radians

Definition The radian measure of a central angle is the ratio of the length $l$ of the arc it subtends to the radius: $\;\alpha=\frac{l}{r}$ (radians). A full angle has $\frac{2\pi r}{r}=2\pi$ rad.

2. Animation: wrapping the radius

A string whose length is the chosen number of radii is wrapped around the circle. The amount of string that fits is the number of radians in the angle. Try $1$, $3.14$ and $6.28$.

3. Functions of any angle

A directed angle: from the positive $Ox$ half-axis counterclockwise is a positive angle, clockwise is negative. The rotation can be longer than a full turn.

Definition For any number $x\in\R$ let $P=(x_P,y_P)$ be the endpoint of an arc of length $x$ measured on the unit circle from the point $(1,0)$. Then $$\sin x=y_P,\qquad \cos x=x_P$$ $$\tan x=\frac{y_P}{x_P}\quad\left(x\neq\frac\pi2+k\pi\right)$$

The angles $x$ and $x+2k\pi$ ($k\in\Z$) end at the same point, so they have the same function values. The identity $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$ and $\tan x=\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}$ hold for every $x$ (in the domain).

Slide the angle from $-4\pi$ to $4\pi$. The spiral shows how many times and in which direction we have turned; the point $P$ and the values depend only on where the rotation ended.

4. Step-by-step example

Find $\sin\left(-\frac{7\pi}{3}\right)$ and convert $210^\circ$ to radians.

  1. Add full turns: $-\frac{7\pi}{3}+4\pi=\frac{5\pi}{3}$ (that is $300^\circ$, quadrant IV).
  2. On the circle: $300^\circ$ is $60^\circ$ below the $Ox$ axis, so $P=\left(\frac12,-\frac{\sqrt3}2\right)$ and $\sin\frac{5\pi}3=-\frac{\sqrt3}2$.
  3. $210^\circ\cdot\frac{\pi}{180^\circ}=\frac{7\pi}{6}$.

$\sin\left(-\frac{7\pi}{3}\right)=-\frac{\sqrt3}{2},\quad 210^\circ=\frac{7\pi}{6}$

5. Tasks

  1. easy
    Convert to radians: $150^\circ$, $300^\circ$. Convert to degrees: $\frac{7\pi}{4}$, $2$ rad.
    Solution
    $\frac{5\pi}{6}$, $\frac{5\pi}{3}$, $315^\circ$, $\frac{360^\circ}{\pi}\approx114.6^\circ$
  2. easy
    Determine the sign of the number $\sin200^\circ\cdot\cos300^\circ\cdot\tan100^\circ$.
    Solution
    $200^\circ$ – quadrant III, $\sin<0$; $300^\circ$ – quadrant IV, $\cos>0$; $100^\circ$ – quadrant II, $\tan<0$. Product: $(-)(+)(-)$. Positive
  3. medium
    A central angle measures $\frac{2\pi}{3}$ and the radius of the circle is $6$. Find the arc length and the area of the circular sector.
    Solution
    $l=\frac{2\pi}3\cdot6=4\pi$, $\;P=\frac12\cdot\frac{2\pi}3\cdot36=12\pi$. $l=4\pi$, $P=12\pi$
  4. medium
    A wheel of radius $30$ cm rolled without slipping for $1$ m. Through what angle (in radians and approximately in degrees) did it turn?
    Solution
    The distance is the arc length: $\alpha=\frac{l}{r}=\frac{100}{30}$. $\frac{10}{3}$ rad $\approx191^\circ$
  5. medium
    Find $\cos\frac{17\pi}{3}$.
    Solution
    $\frac{17\pi}3=4\pi+\frac{5\pi}3$, so $\cos\frac{17\pi}3=\cos\frac{5\pi}3$. The point for $300^\circ$: $\left(\frac12,-\frac{\sqrt3}2\right)$. $\frac12$
  6. exam-level
    It is known that $x\in\left(\pi,\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)$ and $\cos x=-\frac{12}{13}$. Find $\sin x$ and $\tan x$.
    Solution
    $\sin^2x=1-\frac{144}{169}=\frac{25}{169}$. Quadrant III, so $\sin x<0$. $\tan x=\frac{-5/13}{-12/13}$. $\sin x=-\frac{5}{13}$, $\tan x=\frac{5}{12}$

6. On the exam

  • A range of $x$ given in radians ($x\in(\pi,\frac{3\pi}2)$) is information about the quadrant, i.e. about the signs. Always use it and say so.
  • Large or negative angles: first subtract/add full turns $2k\pi$, then read off the circle.
  • The formulas for arc and sector in radians are shorter than in degrees ($l=\alpha r$ instead of $\frac{\alpha}{360^\circ}\cdot2\pi r$). Do not mix units.
  • On a calculator check the DEG/RAD mode (give results exactly anyway, with $\pi$ and roots).
