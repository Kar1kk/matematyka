Quadrilaterals inscribed in a circle and circumscribed about a circle

A circle can be circumscribed about a quadrilateral when opposite angles add up to $180^\circ$ – because together they “see” the whole circle. A circle can be inscribed in a quadrilateral when the sums of opposite sides are equal – because each side consists of two tangent segments.

VIII.R.1

1. A quadrilateral inscribed in a circle

Theorem on a cyclic quadrilateral on CKE sheet A convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ can be inscribed in a circle if and only if $$\alpha+\gamma=\beta+\delta=180^\circ$$
Proof (required on the exam)

$\Rightarrow$ Angle $\alpha$ (at $A$) is an inscribed angle subtending arc $BCD$, and $\gamma$ subtends arc $BAD$. These two arcs make up the whole circle, so the corresponding central angles add up to $360^\circ$. Inscribed angles are half of them (inscribed angle theorem): $\alpha+\gamma=180^\circ$. Likewise $\beta+\delta=180^\circ$ (or from the sum $360^\circ$).

$\Leftarrow$ Let $\alpha+\gamma=180^\circ$. Exactly one circle passes through $A$, $B$, $D$. Ray $AC$ meets it (after crossing diagonal $BD$) at a point $C'$ on the arc $BD$ not containing $A$, so by the part $\Rightarrow$: $|\angle BC'D|=180^\circ-\alpha=\gamma$.

  • If $C$ lay inside the circle ($C$ between $A$ and $C'$): angle $BCA$ is an exterior angle of triangle $BCC'$, so $|\angle BCA|>|\angle BC'A|$; likewise $|\angle ACD|>|\angle AC'D|$. Adding gives $\gamma>|\angle BC'D|=\gamma$ – a contradiction.
  • If $C$ lay outside ($C'$ between $A$ and $C$): the same inequalities the other way round, $\gamma<\gamma$ – a contradiction.

Hence $C=C'$ lies on the circle. $\blacksquare$

Consequences: a parallelogram inscribed in a circle is a rectangle ($\alpha=\gamma$ and $\alpha+\gamma=180^\circ$); a trapezoid inscribed in a circle is isosceles (task 5).

2. A quadrilateral circumscribed about a circle

Theorem on a tangential quadrilateral on CKE sheet A circle can be inscribed in a convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ if and only if $$|AB|+|CD|=|AD|+|BC|$$
Proof (required on the exam)

$\Rightarrow$ From each vertex come two equal tangent segments (tangent segments theorem): $x$ from $A$, $y$ from $B$, $z$ from $C$, $t$ from $D$. Then

$$|AB|+|CD|=(x+y)+(z+t)$$ $$|BC|+|AD|=(y+z)+(t+x)$$

The right-hand sides are equal. $\blacksquare$

$\Leftarrow$ Let $|AB|+|CD|=|AD|+|BC|$. The bisectors of angles $A$ and $B$ meet at a point equidistant from $AD$, $AB$, $BC$ – so there is a circle tangent to these three sides. Suppose it is not tangent to $CD$. Draw a second tangent from $C$; it meets line $AD$ at $D'\ne D$. Quadrilateral $ABCD'$ is tangential, so by the part $\Rightarrow$: $|AB|+|CD'|=|AD'|+|BC|$. Subtracting from the assumption:

$$|CD|-|CD'|=|AD|-|AD'|=\pm|DD'|$$

so in triangle $CDD'$ one side equals the difference of the other two – a contradiction with the triangle inequality. $\blacksquare$

3. Step-by-step example

An isosceles trapezoid with bases $8$ and $2$ is circumscribed about a circle. Find the leg, the radius of the circle and the area of the trapezoid.

  1. Inscribed circle: $8+2=2c$, so the leg is $c=5$.
  2. The projection of the leg onto the longer base: $\frac{8-2}{2}=3$, so $h=\sqrt{25-9}=4$ and $r=\frac h2=2$.
  3. $P=\frac{8+2}{2}\cdot4=20$ (check: $P=p\cdot r=10\cdot2$).

$c=5$, $r=2$, $P=20$

4. Tasks

  1. easy
    Consecutive angles of a quadrilateral measure $80^\circ$, $110^\circ$, $100^\circ$, $70^\circ$. Can a circle be circumscribed about it?
    Solution
    Opposite angles: $80^\circ+100^\circ=180^\circ$ and $110^\circ+70^\circ=180^\circ$. Yes.
  2. easy
    Quadrilateral $ABCD$ is circumscribed about a circle, $|AB|=7$, $|BC|=9$, $|CD|=8$. Find $|AD|$.
    Solution
    $7+8=9+|AD|$. $|AD|=6$
  3. medium
    A quadrilateral circumscribed about a circle of radius $2$ has perimeter $20$. Find its area.
    Solution
    $P=p\cdot r=10\cdot2$. $P=20$
  4. medium
    An isosceles trapezoid is circumscribed about a circle of radius $3$, and its longer base has length $12$. Find the shorter base, the leg and the area.
    Solution
    $h=2r=6$. Leg $c$, shorter base $b$: $12+b=2c$, projection of the leg $\frac{12-b}{2}=12-c$. By the Pythagorean theorem $(12-c)^2+36=c^2$, so $144-24c+36=0$, $c=7.5$, $b=3$. $P=\frac{12+3}{2}\cdot6=45$. $b=3$, $c=7.5$, $P=45$
  5. exam-level
    Prove that a trapezoid inscribed in a circle is isosceles.
    Solution
    Trapezoid $ABCD$, $AB\parallel CD$. Angles at leg $AD$: $|\angle A|+|\angle D|=180^\circ$. The quadrilateral is cyclic, so $|\angle A|+|\angle C|=180^\circ$. Hence $|\angle C|=|\angle D|$ – the angles at base $CD$ are equal. A trapezoid with equal angles at a base is isosceles (the altitudes from $C$ and $D$ give congruent right triangles, ASA). Q.E.D.
  6. exam-level
    Quadrilateral $ABCD$ is inscribed in a circle, $|AB|=3$, $|BC|=5$, $|\angle ABC|=120^\circ$, $|AD|=|CD|$. Find $|AC|$ and the area of the quadrilateral.
    Solution
    Law of cosines: $|AC|^2=9+25-2\cdot15\cdot\cos120^\circ=49$, $|AC|=7$. It is cyclic, so $|\angle ADC|=60^\circ$, and $|AD|=|CD|$ – triangle $ACD$ is equilateral with side $7$. $$P=\tfrac12\cdot3\cdot5\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}+\tfrac{49\sqrt3}{4}$$ $|AC|=7$, $P=16\sqrt3$

5. On the exam

  • Write the use of a theorem together with its condition: “the quadrilateral is inscribed in a circle, so $\alpha+\gamma=180^\circ$” – this earns points.
  • In a cyclic quadrilateral look for inscribed angles subtending the same arc (e.g. $|\angle BAC|=|\angle BDC|$) – this is the strongest tool.
  • Tangential quadrilateral: immediately mark the tangent segments $x,y,z,t$. Tangential trapezoid: $h=2r$ and $a+b=c+d$.
  • Both theorems concern convex quadrilaterals.
