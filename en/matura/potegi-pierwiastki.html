Powers and roots

A root is a power with a fractional exponent: $\sqrt[n]{a}=a^{1/n}$. So it is enough to know the laws of powers, and comparing numbers comes down to a single question: is the base greater than $1$?

I.1, I.3, I.4, I.5 · proof: properties of powers

1. Roots and rational powers

Definition on CKE sheet The $n$-th root of $a\ge0$ is the number $b\ge0$ for which $b^n=a$. For odd $n$ and $a<0$: $\sqrt[n]{a}$ is the number $b<0$ for which $b^n=a$, e.g. $\sqrt[3]{-8}=-2$, $\sqrt[5]{-32}=-2$. An even root of a negative number does not exist.

Proof (required on the exam): properties of powers

Positive integer exponents. $a^r a^s$ is $r$ factors of $a$ and $s$ more factors, in total $r+s$. Similarly $(a^r)^s$ is $s$ groups of $r$ factors each, i.e. $rs$ factors. Negative exponents and zero follow from the definition $a^{-n}=1/a^n$.

Rational exponents. Bring them to a common denominator: $r=\frac pn$, $s=\frac qn$. Let $x=a^{p/n}$, $y=a^{q/n}$, i.e. $x^n=a^p$, $y^n=a^q$, $x,y>0$. Then

$$(xy)^n=x^n y^n=a^p a^q=a^{p+q}$$

and $xy>0$, so $xy$ is the $n$-th root of $a^{p+q}$: $xy=a^{\frac{p+q}{n}}=a^{r+s}$.

The remaining laws. Quotient: $a^{r-s}a^s=a^r$, so $a^{r-s}=\frac{a^r}{a^s}$. Product of bases: for $x=a^{p/n}$, $y=b^{p/n}$ we have $(xy)^n=a^pb^p=(ab)^p$, so $xy=(ab)^{p/n}$ (the quotient $\left(\frac ab\right)^r$ analogously). Power of a power: for $r=\frac pn$, $s=\frac qm$ and $z=\left(a^{p/n}\right)^{q/m}$ we have $z^{mn}=\left(z^m\right)^n=\left(a^{p/n}\right)^{qn}=a^{pq}$, and $z>0$, so $z=a^{\frac{pq}{mn}}=a^{rs}$.

4. Comparing numbers: a common exponent

Two ways: a common base ($8^{5}=2^{15}$ and $4^{7}=2^{14}$, so $8^5>4^7$) or a common exponent — then you compare the bases, because for $u,v>0$ and $k>0$: $u<v\iff u^k<v^k$.

Each number is a product of factors. “▶ Group” combines them into the same number of groups. Then you compare group with group: all of them are smaller, so the whole product is smaller.

5. Step-by-step example

Compute $\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{16}\cdot 4^{0.75}}{\sqrt8\cdot 2^{-1/3}}$.

  1. Everything as powers of two: $\sqrt[3]{16}=2^{4/3}$, $4^{0.75}=2^{3/2}$, $\sqrt8=2^{3/2}$.
  2. Exponent: $\frac43+\frac32-\frac32-\left(-\frac13\right)=\frac53$.
  3. $2^{5/3}=2\cdot2^{2/3}=2\sqrt[3]{4}$.

$2\sqrt[3]{4}$

Which number is greater: $\sqrt3$ or $\sqrt[3]{5}$?

  1. Both are positive, so raise them to a common power $6=\text{LCM}(2,3)$ — this does not change the direction of the inequality.
  2. $(\sqrt3)^6=3^3=27$, $\;(\sqrt[3]5)^6=5^2=25$.

$27>25$, so $\sqrt3>\sqrt[3]{5}$

6. Tasks

  1. easy
    Compute $\sqrt[3]{-27}+\sqrt[4]{16}-\sqrt[5]{-32}$.
    Solution
    $-3+2-(-2)=$ $1$
  2. easy
    Compute $\left(\frac18\right)^{-2/3}+16^{0.75}$.
    Solution
    $\left(\frac18\right)^{-2/3}=8^{2/3}=(\sqrt[3]8)^2=4$, $\;16^{3/4}=(\sqrt[4]{16})^3=8$. $12$
  3. medium
    Rationalize the denominator: a) $\frac{4}{\sqrt5-1}$, b) $\frac{6}{\sqrt[3]{3}}$.
    Solution
    a) $\frac{4(\sqrt5+1)}{5-1}=$ $\sqrt5+1$. b) Multiply by $\sqrt[3]{9}$: $\frac{6\sqrt[3]9}{\sqrt[3]{27}}=\frac{6\sqrt[3]9}{3}=$ $2\sqrt[3]{9}$.
  4. medium
    Which number is greater: $\left(\frac23\right)^{\sqrt2}$ or $\left(\frac23\right)^{1.5}$?
    Solution
    $\sqrt2\approx1.41<1.5$, and the base $\frac23\in(0,1)$, so the inequality changes direction. $\left(\frac23\right)^{\sqrt2}>\left(\frac23\right)^{1.5}$
  5. exam-level
    Compare the numbers $3^{40}$ and $4^{30}$.
    Solution
    Common exponent $10$: $3^{40}=(3^4)^{10}=81^{10}$, $4^{30}=(4^3)^{10}=64^{10}$. Since $81>64$, $3^{40}>4^{30}$.
  6. exam-level
    Prove that $\sqrt[3]{5\sqrt2+7}-\sqrt[3]{5\sqrt2-7}=2$.
    Solution
    Guess the cubes: $(\sqrt2+1)^3=2\sqrt2+6+3\sqrt2+1=5\sqrt2+7$ and $(\sqrt2-1)^3=2\sqrt2-6+3\sqrt2-1=5\sqrt2-7$. Hence the difference is $(\sqrt2+1)-(\sqrt2-1)=$ $2$, which completes the proof.

7. On the exam

  • $\sqrt{a^2}=|a|$: e.g. $\sqrt{(1-\sqrt3)^2}=\sqrt3-1$, not $1-\sqrt3$. The most common lost point.
  • An even root only of a number $\ge0$; an odd one of any number: $\sqrt[3]{-0.001}=-0.1$.
  • Comparing: bring to a common base or a common exponent. For a base in $(0,1)$ reverse the sign of the inequality.
  • Give the result in the simplest form: without negative exponents and (if the task asks for it) without irrationals in the denominator.
  • Powers with irrational exponents and graphs of $a^x$: exponential and logarithmic functions.
