Inscribed and central angles, tangents, arc and sector

An inscribed angle “sees” an arc from the circumference, a central angle sees it from the centre; from the centre the arc looks twice as wide. Wherever you move the vertex of the inscribed angle along the same arc, the angle does not change.

VIII.1, VIII.5, VIII.6

1. Inscribed and central angles

Definition A central angle has its vertex at the centre $O$ of a circle. An inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle and its sides contain chords. Both subtend the arc that lies inside the angle. The measure of an arc is the measure of the central angle subtending it.
The angles-in-a-circle theorem on CKE sheet
  1. An inscribed angle is half of the central angle subtending the same arc.
  2. Two inscribed angles in the same circle are equal if and only if they subtend equal arcs.
In particular, an inscribed angle subtending a semicircle is a right angle.
Proof (required on the exam)

1. Inscribed angle $APB$, centre $O$, $|OA|=|OB|=|OP|=r$. Three cases:

  • $O$ lies on a side, say $PB$. Triangle $AOP$ is isosceles, so $|\angle OAP|=|\angle OPA|=\alpha$. Angle $AOB$ is an exterior angle of this triangle, so $|\angle AOB|=\alpha+\alpha=2\alpha$.
  • $O$ inside the angle. Draw the diameter $PQ$. It splits the inscribed angle into $\alpha_1+\alpha_2$ and the central angle into two angles to which the first case applies: $2\alpha_1+2\alpha_2=2\alpha$.
  • $O$ outside the angle. Again the diameter $PQ$, but now the inscribed angle is the difference $\alpha_1-\alpha_2$ and the central angle is $2\alpha_1-2\alpha_2$.

2. Equal arcs $\iff$ equal central angles (by the definition of arc measure) $\iff$ equal halves of them, i.e. inscribed angles (by part 1). $\blacksquare$

A chord $AB$ divides the circle into two arcs. Inscribed angles on opposite sides of the chord add up to $180^\circ$: $\alpha$ and $180^\circ-\alpha$ (arcs: $2\alpha+(360^\circ-2\alpha)$).

2. Interactive: the inscribed angle does not change

Drag $P$ along the circle (or press “▶ Move $P$”): as long as $P$ stays on the same arc, the angle $APB$ stays put and is half of the central angle on the red arc. Drag $P$ to the other side of the chord and the angle becomes $180^\circ-\alpha$. You can also move the points $A$ and $B$.

3. Tangents

Tangent and radius A tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency.
The tangent segments theorem on CKE sheet If the tangents at the points $A$ and $B$ meet at $P$, then $|PA|=|PB|$ and $PO$ is the angle bisector of the angle $APB$.
Proof

Triangles $OAP$ and $OBP$ are right triangles (right angles at $A$ and $B$), they share the hypotenuse $OP$ and $|OA|=|OB|=r$. By Pythagoras $|PA|=\sqrt{|OP|^2-r^2}=|PB|$, so the triangles are congruent (SSS) and $|\angle APO|=|\angle BPO|$. $\blacksquare$

Length of a tangent segment
$$t=\sqrt{d^2-r^2}$$
$d$
distance from the point $P$ to the centre of the circle (Pythagoras in triangle $OAP$)

The formula sheet contains two more theorems: the one about the secant and tangent segments ($|PA|\cdot|PB|=|PC|^2$) and the one about the angle between a tangent and a chord. The latter is outside the 2024 requirements, but it is on the CKE formula sheet, so you may cite it; it is enough to know that this angle equals the inscribed angle subtending the same arc.

4. Arc length and sector area

Angle $\alpha$ in degrees on CKE sheet
$$L=\frac{\alpha}{360^\circ}\cdot2\pi r$$ $$P=\frac{\alpha}{360^\circ}\cdot\pi r^2$$
$\frac{\alpha}{360^\circ}$
what fraction of the full angle (disc) the sector takes up
in radians
$L=\alpha r$, $P=\frac12\alpha r^2=\frac12Lr$ (radian measure)

Change the angle and the radius. A sector is simply the fraction $\frac{\alpha}{360^\circ}$ of the disc – the arc is the same fraction of the circumference.

5. Step-by-step example

Points $A$, $B$, $C$ lie on a circle with centre $O$ and radius $6$, $|\angle AOB|=110^\circ$. Find $|\angle ACB|$ when $C$ lies on the longer arc $AB$, and when it lies on the shorter one. Find the length of the shorter arc $AB$.

  1. $C$ on the longer arc: angle $ACB$ subtends the shorter arc, whose central angle is $110^\circ$, so $|\angle ACB|=55^\circ$.
  2. $C$ on the shorter arc: the angle subtends the longer arc ($250^\circ$), so it is $125^\circ=180^\circ-55^\circ$.
  3. $L=\frac{110}{360}\cdot12\pi=\frac{11\pi}{3}$.

$55^\circ$ or $125^\circ$; $L=\frac{11\pi}{3}$

6. Tasks

  1. easy
    A point $P$ lies at distance $13$ from the centre of a circle of radius $5$. Find the length of the tangent segment drawn from $P$.
    Solution
    $t=\sqrt{169-25}$. $t=12$
  2. easy
    Find the arc length and the sector area of a disc of radius $9$ with central angle $40^\circ$.
    Solution
    $\frac{40}{360}=\frac19$. $L=\frac19\cdot18\pi$, $P=\frac19\cdot81\pi$. $L=2\pi$, $P=9\pi$
  3. medium
    Triangle $ABC$ is inscribed in a circle with centre $O$, $|\angle BAC|=70^\circ$, $|\angle ABC|=50^\circ$. Find the angles $BOC$, $AOC$, $AOB$.
    Solution
    $|\angle ACB|=60^\circ$. The triangle is acute, so each central angle is twice the inscribed angle subtending the same arc: $|\angle BOC|=140^\circ$, $|\angle AOC|=100^\circ$, $|\angle AOB|=120^\circ$ (sum $360^\circ$).
  4. medium
    A chord $AB$ has length equal to the radius of the circle. Find the measures of the inscribed angles subtending this chord.
    Solution
    Triangle $AOB$ is equilateral, so $|\angle AOB|=60^\circ$. The inscribed angle on one side of the chord: $30^\circ$, on the other: $180^\circ-30^\circ$. $30^\circ$ or $150^\circ$
  5. exam-level
    From a point $P$ the tangents $PA$ and $PB$ are drawn to a circle with centre $O$ and radius $6$. $|\angle APB|=60^\circ$. Find $|PA|$ and the length of the shorter arc $AB$.
    Solution
    In the quadrilateral $OAPB$ the angles at $A$ and $B$ are right angles, so $|\angle AOB|=360^\circ-90^\circ-90^\circ-60^\circ=120^\circ$. $PO$ is the angle bisector: in triangle $OAP$ the angle at $P$ is $30^\circ$, $\tan30^\circ=\frac{6}{|PA|}$, $|PA|=\frac{6}{\sqrt3/3}=6\sqrt3$. Arc: $\frac{120}{360}\cdot12\pi=4\pi$. $|PA|=6\sqrt3$, $L=4\pi$
  6. exam-level
    In triangle $ABC$ the median $CM$ has length equal to half of the side $AB$. Show that the angle $ACB$ is a right angle.
    Solution
    $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$, so $|MA|=|MB|=\frac12|AB|=|MC|$. Thus the points $A$, $B$, $C$ lie on a circle with centre $M$, and $AB$ is its diameter. Angle $ACB$ is an inscribed angle subtending a semicircle, so $|\angle ACB|=90^\circ$, which was to be shown.

7. On the exam

  • Always determine which arc the angle subtends – on which side of the chord the vertex lies. This decides between $\alpha$ and $180^\circ-\alpha$.
  • In a justification, write the full name: “the inscribed and central angles subtending the same arc $AB$”, “the inscribed angle subtending a semicircle”.
  • Point of tangency = right angle. Draw the radius and the segment to the centre – you almost always get a right triangle.
  • The formulas for arc and sector on the formula sheet use degrees; if the angle is in radians, use $L=\alpha r$.
