Linear systems and their geometric interpretation

Every equation $ax+by=c$ is a line. Solving a system means finding the common point of two lines, and two lines have one common point, none, or infinitely many.

IV.1

1. Two methods

Substitution
$$\begin{cases} x+2y=8\\ 3x-y=3 \end{cases}$$

From the second one $y=3x-3$. Substitute into the first: $x+6x-6=8$, so $x=2$, $y=3$.

when
when one of the unknowns has coefficient $\pm1$
Opposite coefficients (elimination)
$$\begin{cases} 2x+3y=7 &|\cdot 2\\ 3x-2y=4 &|\cdot 3 \end{cases}$$

$4x+6y=14$ and $9x-6y=12$. Add side by side: $13x=26$, $x=2$, $y=1$.

when
“ugly” coefficients, with no fractions along the way
Multiplying an equation by a number $\neq0$ does not change the line, and the sum of two equations is a new line through the same common point. That is why the method works (animation in section 4).

Quick test: the determinant
$$\begin{cases} a_1x+b_1y=c_1\\ a_2x+b_2y=c_2 \end{cases}$$ $$W=a_1b_2-a_2b_1$$
$W\neq0$
independent system (the lines are not parallel)
$W=0$
parallel lines: dependent or inconsistent, the calculation decides ($0=0$ or $0=5$)

The condition $A_1B_2-A_2B_1=0$ for parallel lines is on the formula sheet (general-form equations). More on the position of lines: relative position of lines.

How to write the solution of a dependent system?

E.g. $\begin{cases} x-2y=2\\ -2x+4y=-4\end{cases}$: the second is the first times $-2$. All the points of the line $x-2y=2$:

$$\left\{\left(x,\ \tfrac{x-2}{2}\right): x\in\R\right\}$$

Just “infinitely many solutions” is not enough, give the set (the parameter $x$ arbitrary).

Change the coefficients. The type of the system and the common point are computed live. Try: $a_2=2$, $b_2=2$, $c_2=6$ (dependent), then $c_2=2$ (inconsistent).

4. Animation: the opposite-coefficients method

Multiplying an equation does not move the line. Adding rotates the new line (green) about the common point until it becomes vertical ($y$ disappears), then horizontal ($x$ disappears). For parallel lines the new line “runs off to infinity”.

5. Step-by-step example

Solve the system $\begin{cases} \frac{x+1}{2}-\frac{y-1}{3}=2\\ 3(x-y)=2x-7 \end{cases}$.

  1. Get rid of the fractions (first times $6$): $3x+3-2y+2=12$, i.e. $3x-2y=7$.
  2. Rearrange the second: $3x-3y=2x-7$, i.e. $x-3y=-7$, so $x=3y-7$.
  3. Substitute: $3(3y-7)-2y=7$, $7y=28$, $y=4$, $x=5$.
  4. Check in the original system: $\frac{6}{2}-\frac{3}{3}=2$ ✓, $3\cdot1=3=10-7$ ✓.

$\begin{cases} x=5\\ y=4 \end{cases}$ (an independent system, the lines meet at $(5,4)$)

6. Tasks

  1. easy
    Solve by the opposite-coefficients method: $\begin{cases} 3x+4y=10\\ 5x-6y=-34 \end{cases}$
    Solution
    First times $3$, second times $2$: $9x+12y=30$, $10x-12y=-68$. Add: $19x=-38$, $x=-2$. From the first one $4y=16$. $x=-2,\ y=4$
  2. easy
    Determine the number of solutions and give the geometric interpretation:
    a) $\begin{cases} 2x-3y=6\\ -4x+6y=-12 \end{cases}$   b) $\begin{cases} 2x-3y=6\\ 4x-6y=5 \end{cases}$
    Solution
    a) The second is the first times $-2$: the same line. Dependent: $\{(x,\tfrac23x-2): x\in\R\}$.
    b) First times $2$: $4x-6y=12$, while the second says $4x-6y=5$. Subtract: $0=7$. Inconsistent, parallel lines.
  3. medium
    Without solving, decide whether the system $\begin{cases} 6x-4y=1\\ 9x-6y=2 \end{cases}$ has a solution.
    Solution
    $W=6\cdot(-6)-9\cdot(-4)=0$, so the lines are parallel. Left sides: the second $=\tfrac32\cdot$ the first, but $\tfrac32\cdot1\neq2$. The system is inconsistent, no solutions.
  4. medium
    Solve $\begin{cases} \frac{x}{2}+\frac{y}{3}=4\\ \frac{x}{3}-\frac{y}{4}=\frac54 \end{cases}$
    Solution
    Times $6$ and times $12$: $3x+2y=24$, $4x-3y=15$. First times $3$, second times $2$: $9x+6y=72$, $8x-6y=30$. Add: $17x=102$, $x=6$, from the first one $2y=6$. $x=6,\ y=3$
  5. exam-level
    The lines $y=x+1$, $y=-x+5$ and $y=1$ bound a triangle. Find its vertices and area.
    Solution
    Each vertex is the solution of a system of two lines: $x+1=-x+5\Rightarrow(2,3)$; $x+1=1\Rightarrow(0,1)$; $-x+5=1\Rightarrow(4,1)$. The base on the line $y=1$ has length $4$, the altitude is $3-1=2$. $(0,1),(4,1),(2,3)$, area $4$

7. On the exam

  • Give the answer as a pair: $x=5,\ y=4$ or $(5,4)$. Just $x$ is an unfinished task.
  • Check in the original system, not the transformed one: that way a copying error shows up.
  • $0=7$ after subtracting means an inconsistent system, $0=0$ a dependent one. Say so in words and give the interpretation (parallel / coinciding lines).
  • Dependent system: give the solution set with a parameter, e.g. $\{(x,2x-1): x\in\R\}$.
  • The vertices of a figure from the equations of its sides = solutions of systems of pairs of lines (typical in analytic geometry).
